Anyone who needs space – for household goods after a move, tools, seasonal items or business stock – usually has two options: rent a unit in a self-storage building, or put a storage container on their own land. Both solve the same problem but have very different cost structures. The unit is available immediately and can be cancelled monthly, but costs rent per square metre for as long as you use it. The container requires investment, a site and installation up front, but is very cheap to run afterwards.

This article calculates both options side by side, states the assumptions openly and shows where the result tips. It is no substitute for a quote: all amounts are rough guide values without guarantee. Rules referred to are German; if you are elsewhere, check local requirements. For how to choose a storage container in general, see the [storage container guide](/en/ratgeber/lagercontainer-ratgeber); the plain question of buying versus renting a container is covered in [Buy or rent a container](/en/ratgeber/container-kaufen-oder-mieten).

## Starting point: area against area

A 20-foot container measures roughly 5.90 × 2.35 m inside, i.e. about 13.9 m² of floor area (about 33 m³ at the standard internal height of 2.39 m). As the self-storage comparison we therefore use a unit of about **14 m²**. Note that self-storage operators price by area, location and features, and ceiling height and usable stacking height differ from operator to operator. When you compare real quotes, look not only at square metres but also at cubic metres and how high you can actually stack.

For the container the reverse applies: the external dimensions (6.06 × 2.44 m) take up considerably more of your land than the internal area suggests, and you need room by the doors for loading and unloading. All dimensions in detail: [Container sizes and dimensions](/en/ratgeber/container-groessen-und-masse).

## Assumptions for the worked example

So that the calculation can be followed, here are the figures. They are assumptions within typical orders of magnitude, not market prices:

| Item | Assumption |
|---|---|
| Used 20-ft storage container, purchase | approx. €2,400 |
| Delivery | approx. €250 |
| Base (point supports or similar) | approx. €400 (depends heavily on the site) |
| Running costs (rust protection, seals, lock, insurance if any) | approx. €50 per year |
| Resale after use | approx. €1,700, less approx. €250 collection |
| Renting a container | approx. €95 per month, approx. €250 each for delivery and collection |
| Self-storage unit, approx. 14 m² | three price levels: approx. €12 / 18 / 25 per m² per month |

The container figures match the reference assumptions in [What does a container cost?](/en/ratgeber/was-kostet-ein-container) and [Rental prices and calculation](/en/ratgeber/container-mietpreise-kalkulation). Self-storage prices are deliberately set as a range because they vary widely by city, operator and features: put your own quote in at the end. 14 m² times €12, €18 or €25 gives approx. €168, €252 or €350 rent per month.

The one-off outlay for the purchased container in this calculation is €2,400 + €250 + €400 = **approx. €3,050**, plus €50 per year. If the resale proceeds come back later (€1,700 − €250 = €1,450), the net cost falls accordingly.

## Total cost after 12, 24 and 60 months

| Option | after 12 months | after 24 months | after 60 months |
|---|---|---|---|
| Buy container, no resale | approx. €3,100 | approx. €3,150 | approx. €3,300 |
| Buy container, with resale | approx. €1,650 | approx. €1,700 | approx. €1,850 |
| Rent container (€95/month) | approx. €1,640 | approx. €2,780 | approx. €6,200 |
| Unit, 14 m² at €12/m² (€168/month) | approx. €2,016 | approx. €4,032 | approx. €10,080 |
| Unit, 14 m² at €18/m² (€252/month) | approx. €3,024 | approx. €6,048 | approx. €15,120 |
| Unit, 14 m² at €25/m² (€350/month) | approx. €4,200 | approx. €8,400 | approx. €21,000 |

Checking two figures: renting a container for 24 months = €500 (delivery and collection) + 24 × €95 = €2,780. Buying a container over 60 months = €3,050 + 5 × €50 = €3,300; with resale €3,300 − €1,450 = €1,850.

Two things stand out. First, the unit is not automatically behind over short periods: if you need space for only a few months, self-storage has no delivery, no installation and no base to pay for. Second, the gap widens quickly, because the unit consists entirely of running costs while the purchased container is almost entirely one-off cost.

## Where the result tips: break-even

The break-even is the number of months after which the unit has cost more in total than the container. Arithmetically: one-off container costs divided by (monthly unit rent minus the container's running costs of about €4.17 per month).

| Unit price (14 m²) | Break-even vs. bought container, no resale | with resale | Break-even vs. rented container |
|---|---|---|---|
| €168/month (€12/m²) | approx. 19 months | approx. 10 months | approx. 7 months |
| €252/month (€18/m²) | approx. 12 months | approx. 7 months | approx. 3 months |
| €350/month (€25/m²) | approx. 9 months | approx. 5 months | approx. 2 months |

To check using €252/month: without resale €3,050 ÷ (€252 − €4.17) ≈ 12.3 months; with resale (€3,050 − €1,450) ÷ €247.83 ≈ 6.5 months; against the rented container €500 ÷ (€252 − €95) ≈ 3.2 months.

The rule of thumb: **the more expensive the unit per square metre, the sooner the container pays off – at mid-range prices after about a year.** If you need the space for only three to six months, a unit or – where you have the space – a rented container is usually the better choice than buying.

## What the calculation leaves out

The figures are deliberately lean. Several factors shift the result in individual cases, and not all of them can be expressed in euros.

**In favour of self-storage:**

- **No land needed.** Without a site the container is not an option. The unit is the only variant that works without your own land.
- **No permit question.** Whether a container is allowed on your land depends on location, duration and use. More in [Containers and building permits](/en/ratgeber/container-baugenehmigung) and [Neighbour law and boundary distances](/en/ratgeber/container-nachbarrecht-grenzabstand). With a unit this does not arise.
- **Flexible size.** If your needs grow or shrink, you can often swap units. A purchased container has a fixed size.
- **No effort for transport, base or resale.** That is cost and time which the container calculation only partly captures.
- **Operator facilities.** Some buildings offer heated or climate-controlled rooms, access control and video surveillance. What exactly differs by operator and should be in the quote.

**In favour of your own container:**

- **Access without opening hours.** The container is on your land; you get to it whenever you like. Self-storage sites may have set access times, depending on the operator.
- **Much cheaper in the long run.** After break-even the container's monthly average keeps falling.
- **Customisable.** Shelving, ventilation, insulation, lock or alarm can be set up as you wish – see [Using a storage container properly](/en/ratgeber/lagercontainer-richtig-nutzen) and [Securing a container](/en/ratgeber/container-sichern-diebstahlschutz).
- **Residual value.** A well-kept container can be sold: [Selling a used container](/en/ratgeber/container-gebraucht-verkaufen). The proceeds are not guaranteed, though.

**Against the container:** condensation for sensitive goods without ventilation or insulation ([Container ventilation](/en/ratgeber/container-belueftung)), theft risk on an isolated plot, and the fact that the container permanently occupies your land.

## Three scenarios compared

The tables show averages. What matters is what your need looks like. Three typical situations:

| Scenario | Duration | Better suited | Reason |
|---|---|---|---|
| Household goods during a move or renovation | 2–6 months | Unit, alternatively rented container | Short duration; the container's transport costs weigh heavily |
| Tools, garden and seasonal items at home | several years | Purchased container | Permanent need, own land available |
| Business stock, fluctuating quantity | unclear | Unit to start, switch later | Need still uncertain; switch to a container once it is settled |

For temporary storage during a move the rental route is worth checking: €500 transport plus 4 × €95 = €880 for four months, against 4 × €168 = €672 to 4 × €350 = €1,400 for the unit. Everything depends on the unit price; with a cheap unit, it can even be the least expensive option.

## A process for deciding

1. **Estimate the duration.** Under six months: unit or rented container. Over twelve to eighteen months: consider buying.
2. **Check the site.** Is there land, a load-bearing base and access for the delivery vehicle? If not, only the unit remains. Details: [Container foundations](/en/ratgeber/container-fundament) and [Transport and delivery costs](/en/ratgeber/container-transport-lieferkosten).
3. **Clarify permissibility.** It depends on location, duration and use; if in doubt, ask the building authority.
4. **Collect quotes.** At least two unit quotes and two container quotes, each with all additional costs. Help: [Comparing container quotes](/en/ratgeber/container-angebote-vergleichen).
5. **Calculate with your own figures.** The calculator [Buy or rent?](/en/rechner/kaufen-oder-mieten) shows the break-even for the container; then set the unit rent against it yourself.

## Checklist for comparing quotes

**For the self-storage unit:**

- Monthly rent including all mandatory fees (administration, lock, insurance)?
- Notice period and minimum term?
- Usable height and floor area of the unit, not just the rented area?
- Access times and access control?
- Which insurance is included, what does it cover, what not?
- Rent changes during the term: when and how announced?
- What may you store, what is excluded?

**For the container:**

- Purchase price, condition and warranty ([Purchase contract and warranty](/en/ratgeber/container-kaufvertrag-gewaehrleistung))?
- Delivery including crane or hook-lift, manoeuvring space available?
- Base planned and costed?
- Lock, ventilation and shelving as one-off costs?
- Insurance clarified ([Insuring a container](/en/ratgeber/container-versichern))?
- Think about the resale route – the condition at sale determines the proceeds.

## Important note

All amounts in this article are assumptions and guide values without guarantee; self-storage prices in particular differ considerably by region and operator. Whether a container may be placed on your land is governed by the building law of the relevant German federal state (state building code and, outside built-up areas, additionally section 35 of the Federal Building Code) and depends on location, duration and use. Clarify this with the competent authority before buying. For tax questions with commercial use, seek advice.

## Conclusion

The self-storage unit is the flexible solution for short or uncertain needs, and the only one when no land is available. Your own storage container is the economical solution for a lasting need: in our worked example, break-even against the unit at mid-range prices comes after about a year (without resale) or roughly half a year (with resale). What matters is that you calculate with **your** quotes: put unit rent, container price, delivery and base into the formula, check permissibility at the site, and then decide – not on first impressions, but on total cost over the time you actually need the space.
