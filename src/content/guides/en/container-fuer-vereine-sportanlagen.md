Sports clubs share a typical problem: playing activity grows or the old changing room is beyond saving, but a new building fails on money, time and permits. Containers promise an interim or permanent solution. This guide covers the three classic club building blocks – **changing rooms with showers, an equipment store and a common room** – and concentrates on what differs for clubs compared with businesses: changing users, a high peak load when showering, land that belongs to someone else, and volunteer officers. Serving food and drink at festivals and tournaments is covered in [Containers for events and catering](/en/ratgeber/container-fuer-events-und-gastronomie); this guide is about permanent operation on the sports ground. The legal points refer to German law.

## Which building block needs which container?

| Building block | Typical form | Key question |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Equipment store (goals, line-marking trolleys, mowers) | Storage container, possibly with double doors | Access for equipment, theft protection, ventilation |
| Changing room without showers | Insulated module with benches and heating | Space per team, heating, ventilation |
| Changing room with showers | Changing/sanitary module with water and waste-water connection | Hot water, connected load, waste water |
| Common room / clubhouse | Coupled, insulated modules | Building law, fire safety, escape routes |

The types are introduced under [changing-room containers](/en/container/umkleidecontainer), [sanitary containers](/en/container/sanitaercontainer), [storage containers](/en/container/lagercontainer) and [canteen containers](/en/container/kantinencontainer). The equipment store is the simplest option: according to [What does a container cost?](/en/ratgeber/was-kostet-ein-container), a used 20 ft storage container costs about €1,500–3,000. Changing rooms and sanitary modules cost considerably more because of the building services; the price ranges are in the same guide.

## Space: calculate with the team, not the floor area

A 20 ft container measures roughly 5.90 × 2.35 m inside, about 13.9 m² (standard height 2.39 m). A 40 ft container reaches about 28 m². The figure alone says little – in a changing room, bench length counts.

A planning assumption you can check: a team of 16 (squad including substitutes) needs about 0.5 m of bench per person, so roughly 8 m of bench. This is a rule of thumb, not a standard; the requirements of the league, the municipality or the operator of the ground take precedence.

- **Benches along both long walls** of a 20 ft module (each about 0.4 m deep): over 5.90 m of length that is just over 11 m of bench, minus the door, radiator and corners – enough for one team. The central aisle remains 2.35 − 2 × 0.4 = about 1.55 m, but only if kit bags are not left on the floor.
- **Two teams at once** (home and away) mean two separate changing rooms. Add a referee room and a shower area and you quickly end up with several modules or a 40 ft unit with partitions.
- **Staggered use**: training groups that arrive one after another can share a room. That saves modules but lengthens occupancy – and therefore heating time.

The calculation uses standard height; a high cube (2.70 m inside) does not change the floor area but improves air volume and headroom.

## Worked example: showers are the expensive part

Showers determine connected load, waste water and running costs. A worked example with **explicit assumptions**: 16 people shower for 4 minutes each, flow 8 l/min, mixed temperature 38 °C, cold water 10 °C.

- Mixed water: 16 × 4 min × 8 l/min = **512 l**.
- Heating by 28 K (38 − 10 °C): 512 l × 28 K × 1.163 Wh/(l·K) ≈ **16.7 kWh** per team.
- Two teams per training or match day: about **33 kWh**.
- With 150 such days a year and an assumed electricity price of €0.35/kWh: 33 kWh × 150 × €0.35 ≈ **€1,750 a year** for hot-water electricity – with direct electric heating and without losses. A heat pump or solar thermal system lowers this, at higher purchase cost.

More important than the cost is the **power**. An electric instantaneous heater would have to deliver the 28 K at 8 l/min immediately: 8/60 l/s × 28 K × 4.186 kJ/(kg·K) ≈ **15.6 kW per shower point**. Three showers running together come to about 47 kW – more than any temporary site supply and many club connections can provide (see the guide values in [Power supply for the container](/en/ratgeber/container-stromanschluss)). The usual answer is a **hot-water storage tank** that charges outside the peaks: for the 512 l of mixed water, about 290 l of 60 °C storage water is arithmetically enough, mixed with cold water to 38 °C ((38 − 10) ÷ (60 − 10) = 0.56 hot-water share). In practice the tank is sized larger, and storage brings hygiene questions (legionella) that your specialist contractor must assess.

Then there is the **waste water**: 512 l per team has to drain away. Whether a sewer connection, a holding tank or a small treatment plant is possible is governed by water law (the Water Resources Act, WHG, plus state water acts); details are in [Water supply and waste water](/en/ratgeber/container-wasseranschluss-abwasser). If you plan changing rooms without showers, most of these tasks fall away.

## Location: the club is often not the owner

Many sports grounds belong to the municipality or a parent body, and the club uses them under a lease, a user agreement or a revocable permission. That has consequences:

- **The landowner's consent** in writing before you order anything. Without it, a container on someone else's land is a risk, even if it is technically easy to place.
- **Ownership of the container**: whether the club may later take its container away or whether it becomes part of the land depends on how it is attached to the ground and on the contract (see [Ownership, temporary structures and the land register](/en/ratgeber/container-eigentum-scheinbestandteil-grundbuch)). Settle removal and compensation in the contract.
- **Term**: a container meant to stay for 15 years does not fit a lease that ends in three.

On building law: whether a changing-room or clubhouse container needs a permit depends on location, duration and use. Sports grounds are often in the outer area or in green space with special zoning; the relevant rules are the state building code and, for outer-area sites, additionally § 35 of the Federal Building Code (BauGB). Talk to the building authority before ordering (see [Containers and building permits](/en/ratgeber/container-baugenehmigung) and [Development plans and the outer area](/en/ratgeber/container-bebauungsplan-aussenbereich)). As soon as people stay in the container, fire safety and escape routes apply ([Fire safety rules](/en/ratgeber/container-brandschutz-vorschriften)); for a clubhouse open to the public, accessibility requirements are also possible and differ between federal states.

## Noise and neighbours

Sports grounds have their own rules: the **Sports Facilities Noise Protection Ordinance (18th BImSchV)** sets out how noise from sports facilities is assessed. A new common room or terrace at the container can change the noise situation even though the pitch has always been there. Clarify this with the municipality before planning a clubhouse with an outdoor area. The structural options are in [Noise control and acoustics in containers](/en/ratgeber/container-laermschutz-akustik), distance questions in [Boundary distances and neighbour law](/en/ratgeber/container-nachbarrecht-grenzabstand).

## Equipment store: order, security, flammables

The equipment store is the most sensible starting point because it needs no water and almost no electricity. Points to plan:

- **Access**: line-marking trolleys, goals and mowers must get out. Check door width (about 2.34 m opening in a standard container), threshold and ramp; a double-door or open-side container makes access easier.
- **Theft protection**: ride-on mowers and electric tools are attractive to thieves. Locks, anchoring and location are described in [Theft protection for containers](/en/ratgeber/container-sichern-diebstahlschutz).
- **Ventilation**: wet nets, grass cuttings and damp equipment cause condensation; see [Ventilation in containers](/en/ratgeber/container-belueftung).
- **Fuel, gas cylinders, batteries**: petrol for mowers, paint for line marking and lithium batteries from robotic mowers need special care. In general: small quantities, kept apart from everything else, after consulting your insurer and the fire service. You can only get a binding answer from qualified people on site.

For everyday use, [Using a storage container properly](/en/ratgeber/lagercontainer-richtig-nutzen) is the natural follow-up.

## Buy or rent?

Clubs have no profit motive but they do have budget limits. The decision depends on the **term** and on who is allowed to invest.

| Situation | Rather rent | Rather buy |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Interim solution during refurbishment (1–2 years) | yes | no |
| Permanent equipment store | no | yes |
| Changing room with showers, lease uncertain | yes | only after clarification |
| Grant or subsidy possible | clarify before applying | clarify before applying |

As a guide, sanitary containers cost about €250–700 a month according to [Rental prices and calculation](/en/ratgeber/container-mietpreise-kalkulation) – over twelve months that is arithmetically €3,000–8,400. For a short period this can be cheaper than buying, for a permanent need it cannot; the method is shown in [Buy or rent a container?](/en/ratgeber/container-kaufen-oder-mieten), and the [buy-or-rent calculator](/en/rechner/kaufen-oder-mieten) runs your own figures. For grants from the municipality, state or sports federation: ask about the conditions **before** you order. Many schemes exclude measures that have already begun – whether and what is funded varies from place to place.

## Liability, insurance, tax

In a club, volunteer officers carry duties to keep premises safe: for showers, for instance, slip hazards and legionella; for changing rooms, fire safety. Which insurance is sensible (contents, building, liability, natural hazards) is covered in [Insuring a container](/en/ratgeber/container-versichern). The tax treatment of purchase and depreciation depends on non-profit status and the type of use; general notes are in [Tax and depreciation](/en/ratgeber/container-steuer-abschreibung), and the decision is for your tax adviser.

## Checklist for the committee

1. Clarify the need: how many teams, how many users at once, with or without showers?
2. Land: owner, contract term, written consent.
3. Building law: ask the building authority (state building code, outer area), fire safety and escape routes.
4. Connections: electricity (peak load), water, waste water – agree costs with specialist firms.
5. Calculate buy versus rent; clarify funding before ordering.
6. Noise and neighbours: sports noise, terrace, hours of use.
7. Insurance, liability and maintenance (hot water, heating) allocated.
8. Record removal and ownership of the container in the contract.
9. Document the decision at the members' meeting.

## Important note

This guide is general orientation and not legal, tax or technical planning. All prices, outputs and consumption figures are guide values without guarantee; the worked example rests on the assumptions stated. The state building code and the requirements of the municipality, the landowner and the specialist firms take precedence.

## Conclusion

Containers are a practical solution for clubs that need an equipment store, changing room or clubhouse quickly. The equipment store is simple; a changing room with showers needs planning: in the example a team's hot water takes about 17 kWh and the peak output is about 16 kW per shower point – so storage tank, connection and waste water are decisive. Clarify land, building law and funding before ordering, and the project stays manageable for the club.
