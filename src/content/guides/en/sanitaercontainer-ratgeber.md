Sanitary containers are technically more demanding than storage or office containers because they need water, waste water, power and heat at the same time – and because hygiene, frost protection and cleaning have to be organised while they are in use. The purchase price or monthly rent of the module is therefore only part of the bill. This guide shows which variants exist, which connection questions should be settled before ordering and how to estimate the total cost of a rental. An overview of the container type is also available on the [sanitary container](/en/container/sanitaercontainer) page. Legal references relate to the situation in Germany.

## What sanitary containers are used for

The classic location is the **construction site**. As soon as workers are on site for any length of time, they need toilets and washing facilities; shell construction and earthworks simply do not have them. Which welfare facilities are required overall is covered in the guide [containers on the construction site – health and safety](/en/ratgeber/container-arbeitsschutz-baustelle).

At **events** – festivals, sports events, markets – the issue is short-term peak demand. Throughput, robustness and quick cleaning matter more than comfort. Compared with simple portable toilet cabins, sanitary containers offer running water and more capacity per footprint. For the wider framework of such deployments see [containers for events and catering](/en/ratgeber/container-fuer-events-und-gastronomie).

During the **refurbishment** of residential or office buildings they bridge the loss of existing bathrooms: while a bathroom is being renewed, a shower container in the courtyard replaces the in-house installation for a few weeks. And in **temporary extensions** – accommodation, school annexes, staff housing, club facilities – they form the sanitary core of a modular complex, often combined with residential, changing-room and welfare containers (see [changing-room containers](/en/container/umkleidecontainer) and [containers for clubs and sports grounds](/en/ratgeber/container-fuer-vereine-sportanlagen)).

## Variants at a glance

Sanitary containers are highly configurable. They are usually portable cabins in 10, 20 or, less often, 30-foot lengths rather than converted shipping containers.

| Variant | Usual equipment | Usual uses |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Toilet container** | 2–6 WC cubicles, sometimes urinals, wash trough or individual basins, mirror | Construction site, event, public space |
| **Shower container** | 2–6 shower cubicles with changing area, shelves, floor drain, water heater | Refurbishment, sports ground, accommodation, camp |
| **Combined WC + shower** | 1–2 WCs, 1–2 showers, washbasin, shared lobby | Small sites, residential modules, holiday camps |
| **Separate ladies / gents** | Two separate entrances, mirrored layout | Larger sites, businesses, accommodation |
| **Accessible version** | Larger cubicle with manoeuvring space, hinged support rails, wheelchair-accessible basin, emergency call, ramp | Public use, events, administration |

Regardless of layout, there are two basic technical forms: containers **with integrated fresh and waste water tanks** for sites without pipes, and containers **for a fixed connection** to mains water and sewer. Which one fits depends less on the module than on the site and the length of deployment – see the decision table below.

For accessible versions, DIN 18040 is often used as the planning basis in Germany; among other things it provides for a manoeuvring space of 1.50 × 1.50 m. In a portable cabin with an internal width of around 2.3 m, such a cubicle takes up practically the entire width. Whether and to what extent accessibility is required follows from state building law, the tender or conditions set by the event organiser.

## Tank or fixed connection: the key decision

The technical connection determines cost and effort more than the interior fit-out.

| Criterion | Tank solution | Fixed connection |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Site requirement | Access for suction and water tankers | Mains water pipe and sewer within reach |
| Ongoing effort | Refilling and emptying at fixed intervals | Hardly any, as long as nothing freezes or blocks |
| One-off effort | Low | Pipework, possibly a lifting station, coordination with water supplier and sewer operator |
| Capacity limit | Tank volume – reached quickly with showers | Practically none |
| Suitable for | Short deployments, events, sites without pipes | Several months on site, modular complexes, accommodation |

**How quickly a tank runs dry (worked example, assumption):** ten people shower once a day, using 40 to 60 litres per shower. That is 400 to 600 litres per day. A 1,000-litre fresh water tank therefore lasts only around 1.7 to 2.5 days – and the waste water tank fills at the same rate. With toilet and hand-washing use only, consumption falls considerably, as the rental example below shows.

**Fresh water.** With a fixed connection the container is supplied from a site water connection or a building's supply. To prevent water flowing back from the container installation into the drinking water network, suppliers generally require a suitable protection device such as a backflow preventer (system separator). Details on pipework and costs are in the guide [water connection and waste water](/en/ratgeber/container-wasseranschluss-abwasser).

**Waste water.** Ideally the container is connected to the sewer; if the sewer is higher than the container outlet, a lifting station is needed. For connection to a public sewer, the requirements of the local sewer operator apply. Without a sewer, a waste water tank collects the effluent and is emptied by a suction tanker. Waste water from showers and toilets must not simply be left to soak away; in Germany the legal framework comes from the Federal Water Act (WHG), the state water acts and municipal by-laws.

## Power and hot water

A CEE connection is usual. Which size is needed is decided almost entirely by how hot water is produced.

| Principle | Advantage | Disadvantage |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Electric instantaneous water heater** | Compact, no volume limit, no warm-up time | High connected load, three-phase supply needed |
| **Hot water cylinder / boiler** | Low connected load | Limited volume, heat-up time, takes up space |
| **Gas instantaneous water heater** | Independent of electrical capacity | Cylinder logistics, flue, additional inspection and safety requirements |

**What an instantaneous heater requires (worked example):** electric instantaneous heaters for showers are usually rated at 18 to 27 kW. On a 400 V three-phase supply that corresponds to a current per phase of around 26 A (18 kW) to 39 A (27 kW), calculated as power ÷ (√3 × 400 V). A red 32 A CEE socket delivers around 22 kW – a 27 kW unit exceeds that on its own, and if two showers run at once the load doubles. Many site distribution boards are not designed for this. Cylinders spread the energy over time and manage with much smaller connections.

**Energy costs (worked example, assumption):** heating 400 litres of shower water per day from 10 °C to 38 °C takes around 13 kWh (400 l × 4.19 kJ/(kg·K) × 28 K ÷ 3,600). At €0.30 to €0.40 per kWh that is approx. €3.90 to €5.20 per day, excluding cylinder and pipe losses.

The basics of CEE sizes, ratings and cable cross-sections are in the guide [power supply for containers](/en/ratgeber/container-stromanschluss). Work on the fixed electrical installation belongs in the hands of a qualified electrician.

**Ventilation.** Wet rooms without windows need mechanical extraction, ideally with an overrun. Without it, moisture condenses on cold surfaces, followed by mould and odour. More on this under [ventilating containers](/en/ratgeber/container-belueftung).

## Frost protection and winter operation

Frost is the most critical point in operation. Supply pipes, traps, cisterns, tanks and all external pipework are at risk.

- Lay supply pipes frost-free or fit them with self-regulating trace heating and insulate them.
- Keep the container at around 5 to 10 °C continuously, not only when it is in use.
- Insulate or heat external tanks.
- During longer shutdowns – over the holidays, for example – drain the entire system including traps and cisterns.

A frozen sanitary container is rarely obvious straight away. The damage often only shows when it thaws and burst pipes start leaking. Frost protection is therefore not a matter of comfort but of preventing damage. Further advice is in the guide [containers in winter](/en/ratgeber/container-im-winter).

## Siting

Sanitary containers need a load-bearing, level base. Being out of level matters more here than with other containers because floor drains and shower trays are designed for a defined fall – if the container is tilted, water collects in the cubicle. Usual solutions are surveyed pad or strip foundations or a level, compacted surface; basics under [container foundations](/en/ratgeber/container-fundament).

Access should be slip-resistant: a step or short platform on construction sites, a ramp with a gentle gradient and a firm surface for accessible use. The location should allow short walking distances to workplaces without being in traffic or crane zones, and must be reachable for the suction tanker if a tank is used. Whether siting requires a permit depends on location, duration and use – see [containers and planning permission](/en/ratgeber/container-baugenehmigung).

## Hygiene and cleaning

A sanitary container is only as good as its cleaning. There are no fixed standard values for cleaning intervals; the following figures are planning guides and depend heavily on actual use.

| Use | People | Planning guide for cleaning |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Construction site, ongoing | approx. 10–20 | 2–3 × per week |
| Construction site, large unit | over approx. 30 | daily |
| Event | changing, high | several times a day, attendant on site |
| Accommodation / residential module | permanent residents | daily to several times a week |
| Refurbishment support | 1–2 households | weekly or self-cleaning |

Responsibilities should be agreed in writing. With rented containers, cleaning is usually a chargeable extra. If it is handled in-house, it needs a named person and a schedule – a general expectation that everyone will tidy up after themselves is no substitute.

Consumables include toilet paper, soap, paper towels or hand dryers, disinfectant, bin bags and sanitary bins. Without organised refilling and checks you quickly end up with a container that works but nobody wants to use.

## Requirements on construction sites and in businesses

For workplaces in Germany – construction sites included – the Workplaces Ordinance (ArbStättV) and the Technical Rules for Workplaces set the framework, for sanitary rooms in particular **ASR A4.1**. It contains requirements on the minimum number of toilets and washing facilities depending on the number of employees, on separation by sex, on walking distances, and special provisions for construction sites. Requirements for heating, lighting and ventilation come on top.

Take the specific figures from the current text of ASR A4.1 and check them against the risk assessment rather than relying on rules of thumb. An overview with a floor-area and cost example for a complete welfare container complex is in the guide [health and safety on the construction site](/en/ratgeber/container-arbeitsschutz-baustelle).

## Worked example: six months' rental on a construction site

**Assumptions:** 20-foot combined container (WC + shower), deployment from April to September (no winter package), twelve workers, delivery and collection by lorry with loading crane in the local area, cleaning twice a week over 26 weeks. All figures come from the tables above.

**Option A – fixed connection to site water and sewer:**

| Item | Calculation | approx. from | approx. to |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Rent | 6 × €400–700 | €2,400 | €4,200 |
| Delivery and collection | 2 × €350–800 | €700 | €1,600 |
| Connections | one-off | €400 | €2,500 |
| Cleaning | 52 × €40–120 | €2,080 | €6,240 |
| Final cleaning | one-off | €150 | €500 |
| **Total** | | **€5,730** | **€15,040** |

The deposit of approx. €800–2,000 comes on top as a refundable security and is not included in the total.

The proportions are notable: in this example cleaning can cost more than the rent itself. When comparing quotes, it is therefore worth asking not only for the monthly rate but also for the cleaning interval and the price per visit – see the guide [comparing quotes](/en/ratgeber/container-angebote-vergleichen).

**Option B – tank solution without pipes:** assume around 16 litres of waste water per person per working day (two toilet flushes of 6 litres, hand-washing twice at 2 litres); nobody showers. With twelve people that is around 190 litres per day and just under 1,000 litres per five-day week – so a 1,000-litre waste water tank has to be emptied roughly weekly.

| Additional item | Calculation | approx. from | approx. to |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Tank surcharge on rent | 6 × €60–150 | €360 | €900 |
| Collections | 26 × €100–300 | €2,600 | €7,800 |
| **Extra cost compared with A** | | **€2,960** | **€8,700** |

From this, deduct the cost of the water and waste water pipework included in the connection costs of option A; in option B fresh water also has to be delivered, which is not costed here. If people also showered, the tanks would be full after around two days, as the example above shows. The calculation shows that even for a few months on site it is worth seriously considering a fixed connection, even if it costs into the low four figures as a one-off.

## Buy or rent?

For short and medium deployments, renting is usually more economical because maintenance, inspection and upkeep of the building services lie with the rental company. The guide [container rental prices](/en/ratgeber/container-mietpreise-kalkulation) puts the rough break-even for sanitary containers at approx. 30–48 months – much later than for storage containers, because building services, upkeep and depreciation weigh more heavily. Buying is therefore mainly an option for permanent needs, such as accommodation or company yards, and only if cleaning, maintenance and frost protection can be covered in-house. The general trade-off is covered in [buying or renting a container](/en/ratgeber/container-kaufen-oder-mieten); the [buy or rent?](/en/rechner/kaufen-oder-mieten) calculator works with your own figures.

## Checklist before ordering

- How many people use the facility at the same time, and is separation by sex required?
- Is accessible access required?
- Are mains water, sewer and sufficient power within reach – or is a tank solution needed?
- Is the connected load sufficient for the planned hot water production, even with several showers running at once?
- Who cleans at what interval, and what does a visit cost?
- Who refills consumables and checks the facility?
- With a tank solution: how often does it need emptying and refilling, and is access clear for the suction tanker?
- Will it operate through the winter, and is frost protection planned?
- Is the base load-bearing and exactly level?
- Have permit questions for location and duration been resolved?

On delivery, a [handover report](/en/ratgeber/container-anlieferung-abnahmeprotokoll) helps record existing damage to fittings, cubicles and pipework.

## Important note

This article is for general guidance only and does not replace legal, plumbing or electrical advice. The current versions of ArbStättV and ASR, the risk assessment, the requirements of the water supplier, sewer operator and authorities, and the employers' liability insurance association (Berufsgenossenschaft) are decisive. Electrical connections and gas appliances belong in the hands of appropriate specialist firms. All cost figures are guide values without guarantee; the worked examples are based on the stated assumptions.

## Conclusion

Sanitary containers are the most technically demanding standard container category, because they need water, waste water, power and heat at once and their usefulness depends on day-to-day operation. The decision rarely hinges on the module itself, but on four questions: are there pipes for a fixed connection, is there enough power for hot water, who handles cleaning and refilling, and will it run through the winter? The worked example shows that cleaning and tank emptying can easily exceed the monthly rent – costing these items before ordering avoids the most expensive surprises.
