## The one question everything hangs on

A container costs anything from a few thousand to several tens of thousands of euros. Anyone using one for business wants to deduct the cost – and this is where tax treatment splits into two different worlds. This guide describes the position under German law.

If the container is a **movable asset** (bewegliches Wirtschaftsgut), it is depreciated over a comparatively short useful life, letting it out is an ordinary VAT-able supply, and a sale is a normal disposal of a fixed asset.

If it is a **building** or part of a building, the long building depreciation applies, it is included in the valuation of the property, and letting it can – as with real estate – be exempt from VAT.

The distinction essentially turns on whether the container is **firmly connected to the land**, is meant to stay there permanently and gives shelter to people or goods. What matters is not the manufacturer's label or the wording of the sales contract but the actual circumstances.

| Feature | Points to movable asset | Points to building |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Foundation | placed loosely, timber bearers, pad foundations without a fixed connection | solid strip foundation or slab with fixed anchoring |
| Utilities | site power, mobile connections | fixed connection to sewer, water, heating |
| Time on site | limited, moved repeatedly | permanently in the same place |
| Transportability | can be moved by crane at any time | only after substantial dismantling |
| Planning treatment | temporary placement, possibly permit-free | approved as a building |
| General perception | site cabin, storage box | residential or office building |

Typically, a site or storage container that gets moved is a movable asset. An office or residential container set on a foundation, connected to utilities and staying put tends towards building status. In between lies a grey area that the tax office assesses in case of doubt. So clarify this question with your tax adviser **before you buy**, not when the accounts are being drawn up.

Two distinctions are often confused:

- **Tax law and civil law** apply their own tests. Whether a container remains a temporary fixture (Scheinbestandteil) under civil law or becomes an integral part of the land is a different question from its tax classification – see [ownership, temporary fixture and land register](/en/ratgeber/container-eigentum-scheinbestandteil-grundbuch).
- **Planning law** is separate again: a container that is movable for tax purposes may still need planning permission. That depends on location, duration and use, see [containers and planning permission](/en/ratgeber/container-baugenehmigung).

## Depreciation period

The useful life of movable assets is based on the official German depreciation tables (AfA-Tabellen); for buildings the rates are set by statute. The figures below are guide values; what counts is the classification in the individual case.

| Classification | Useful life (guide) | Method |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Site cabin, construction-site container | approx. 8–10 years | straight-line, movable |
| Storage and materials container | approx. 10–15 years | straight-line, movable |
| Office container as a movable asset | approx. 8–10 years | straight-line, movable |
| Container as operating equipment (e.g. a technical or refrigerated container directly serving the business process) | approx. 10–15 years | straight-line, movable |
| Container as a non-residential building in business assets | 33 1/3 years (3 % p.a.) | building depreciation |
| Container as a residential building | 50 years (2 %) or 33 1/3 years (3 %), depending on completion date | building depreciation |

For movable assets, German legislators have repeatedly allowed **declining-balance depreciation** for limited periods in recent years; there are also time-limited special rules for new residential buildings. Whether such a rule applies to your date of purchase or start of construction is something your tax adviser should check – the windows change.

### Worked example 1: movable or building

Suppose a fitted-out office container costs €20,000 net.

| | movable asset (10 years) | building (33 1/3 years) |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Depreciation per year | €2,000 | €600 |
| Depreciation after 5 years | €10,000 | €3,000 |
| Difference after 5 years | | €7,000 less expense |

At an assumed marginal tax rate of 30 %, the €7,000 difference corresponds to roughly €2,100 of tax relief arriving earlier. That is a **cash-flow effect**, not a permanent saving: over the full useful life the same amount is depreciated in both cases, just at different speeds.

### Worked example 2: incidental costs and the first year

A used 20 ft storage container costs €2,400 net, delivery by a lorry with loader crane €450 net (the range for such a local trip is approx. €350–800, see [transport and delivery costs](/en/ratgeber/container-transport-lieferkosten)). The container is delivered in October and used immediately; a useful life of ten years is assumed.

| Item | Amount |
| --- | --- |
| Purchase price, net | €2,400.00 |
| Delivery (incidental acquisition costs) | €450.00 |
| **Acquisition cost** | **€2,850.00** |
| Depreciation per full year (€2,850 ÷ 10) | €285.00 |
| Depreciation in year 1 (October–December, 3/12) | €71.25 |
| Depreciation in years 2 to 10 | €285.00 each |
| Depreciation in year 11 (January–September, 9/12) | €213.75 |

The example shows two things: delivery is **depreciated along with the container**, not expensed immediately. And in the year of purchase depreciation is only **pro rata by month** – buy in December and you depreciate just 1/12 in the first year. Total over all years: €71.25 + 9 × €285 + €213.75 = €2,850.

### Special rules worth checking

**Low-value assets and the pooled item.** Below the low-value asset threshold, immediate write-off is possible; for a higher value band a pooled item (Sammelposten) is an alternative. A complete container is usually above these limits – individual separately usable items such as shelving or a heater may fall below them. The thresholds are adjusted by legislation; please check the current figures.

**Investment deduction and special depreciation (§ 7g EStG).** Businesses below a profit threshold can deduct part of planned acquisition costs in advance and additionally claim special depreciation. One condition is that the asset is used **exclusively or almost exclusively for business** in the year of acquisition and the following year – mainly is not enough. Selling the container shortly after purchase or using it privately risks reversal. § 7g does not apply to buildings, which is another reason to settle the classification early.

## VAT and input VAT

When you buy a container for your business, the VAT included is in principle deductible as **input VAT**, provided you are entitled to deduct and the container is used for taxable supplies. A proper invoice with all mandatory details is required. The same applies to hire: under these conditions the VAT on the hire invoice is deductible on an ongoing basis.

| Situation | Input VAT or VAT | Note |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Purchase or hire for the business, taxable use | input VAT deductible | proper invoice required |
| Small business (Kleinunternehmer) | no input VAT deduction | gross price is the expense |
| Partly private use | proportionate | document the split |
| You let a movable container (storage, site, temporary office) | letting is taxable | input VAT on purchase deductible; no option needed |
| You let a container with building status long term | in principle exempt like letting real property | option to tax only when letting to businesses for their taxable supplies; not possible for residential use |
| You let short term as accommodation (holiday let) | usually taxable at the reduced rate | except for small businesses; see [holiday accommodation](/en/ratgeber/container-ferienunterkunft-vermieten) |
| Purchase from a private seller or under the margin scheme | no input VAT shown | compare net costs, see example below |

The table shows why the classification also matters for VAT: letting a movable container is an ordinary taxable supply. Only once the container counts as a building do the rules for letting real property – exemption and possible option to tax – come into play.

### Worked example 3: dealer or private seller

The same used 20 ft container is offered twice: by a dealer at €2,400 net plus 19 % VAT (€2,856 gross) and privately at €2,600 with no VAT.

| Buyer | Dealer offer (€2,856 gross) | Private offer (€2,600) | Cheaper |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| entitled to deduct input VAT | effectively €2,400 (€456 input VAT reclaimed) | €2,600 | dealer, by €200 |
| small business or private individual | €2,856 | €2,600 | private, by €256 |

For a buyer entitled to deduct input VAT, the seemingly dearer dealer offer is therefore cheaper. A private purchase also often comes with no statutory warranty at all, because it can be excluded between private parties – see [sales contract and warranty](/en/ratgeber/container-kaufvertrag-gewaehrleistung). Whether buying makes sense at all or hire suits better is covered in [buy or rent?](/en/ratgeber/container-kaufen-oder-mieten).

### Later change of use

If the circumstances relevant to input VAT deduction change – for example from taxable business use to exempt long-term residential letting – an **input VAT adjustment** under § 15a UStG may apply. The adjustment period is five years for movable assets and ten years for land and buildings. There are simplification thresholds for small amounts of input VAT; for a fitted-out container costing five figures, however, the adjustment will usually be relevant.

## Property tax

German property tax (Grundsteuer) is linked to land ownership. A mobile container that counts as a movable asset is normally not subject to it.

Things are different once the container becomes a **building**, i.e. firmly connected and permanently placed. It is then included in the valuation of the property. Since the property tax reform, the federal states use different models, so how much a container building affects the bill depends on the state. Changes to the property – such as a newly erected building – must be reported to the tax office in line with the rules of the state concerned.

Practical consequence: the question "movable or building" has several effects – on income tax, on VAT when letting and on property tax. It should be answered consistently and with reasons. It strengthens your position if planning treatment and tax classification match; see [building application and change of use](/en/ratgeber/container-bauantrag-nutzungsaenderung).

## Letting containers as a source of income

Anyone letting containers earns different types of income depending on the arrangement and classification:

| Arrangement | Type of income (guide) | Consequences |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Letting with services, frequently changing tenants, larger scale | business income | trade registration, trade tax (with an allowance for sole traders and partnerships) |
| Occasional letting of a single movable container without extra services | other income (§ 22 No. 3 EStG) | small annual exemption limit; losses only offsettable to a limited extent |
| Long-term letting of a container with building status | income from letting and leasing (§ 21 EStG) | building depreciation, expenses as for a property |

The distinction is tricky in individual cases and affects trade registration, trade tax and record-keeping obligations. Here too: clarify in advance.

A second point concerns the **intention to make a profit**. If a container is let at a loss for years, the tax office may treat it as a hobby (Liebhaberei) and disallow the losses. A plausible forecast at the outset is therefore not a formality. Which running costs belong in such a calculation is shown in [running costs and depreciation in value](/en/ratgeber/container-betriebskosten-wertverlust); for market rents see [calculating hire prices](/en/ratgeber/container-mietpreise-kalkulation).

## Private use and letting to relatives

If a business container is also used privately, the private share must be recorded as a **withdrawal** and taxed. With mixed use, document the business share – for example with an occupancy schedule, a usage agreement or photos. Note that even noticeable private use can jeopardise the conditions for § 7g EStG (see above).

Arrangements in which a container is let to close relatives or to your own company are subject to the **arm's-length test**: a written contract, a market rent and actual performance with regular payment. If any of these is missing, the contract is often not recognised for tax purposes.

## Expense or acquisition? Typical booking questions

| Transaction | Classification (usual case) | Note |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Transport, crane, placement on purchase | incidental acquisition costs | depreciate along with the container (see worked example 2) |
| Moving it to a new site later | running expense | keep the invoice; supports movable classification |
| Repairs, e.g. replacing floor panels, fixing rust damage | maintenance expense | immediately deductible |
| Later full fit-out, insulation, sanitary, windows | may be subsequent acquisition costs | substantial improvement or change of function |
| Hire payments | running expense | note input VAT on the hire invoice |
| Lease instalments | expense or attribution to the lessee, depending on the contract | see [leasing](/en/kaufen-mieten/kauf/leasing) |

## What you should keep

- **Sales contract and invoice:** acquisition cost, input VAT, start of depreciation.
- **Delivery note or acceptance record with date:** month of acquisition for pro rata depreciation.
- **Foundation and placement records, photos of the set-up:** argument for movable or building.
- **Evidence of relocations:** supports movable classification.
- **Hire and usage agreements:** allocation of income, arm's-length test.
- **Receipts for fit-out and repairs:** distinguishing maintenance from subsequent acquisition costs.

## Conclusion

The tax treatment of a container hinges on the distinction between movable asset and building – and with it between roughly eight to fifteen years and 33 1/3 or more years of depreciation, between taxable and in principle exempt letting, and between no property tax effect and one. Foundation, utilities, time on site and planning treatment are the features that decide the question. Settle the classification before you buy, include delivery and placement in the acquisition cost, document the actual circumstances and compare used offers by net cost rather than by the price tag.
