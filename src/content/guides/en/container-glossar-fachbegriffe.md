Container listings and guides use plenty of terms that aren't self-explanatory – some come from international shipping, others from German building law. This glossary groups the key terms by topic and links to a fuller article wherever there is more to say.

## Dimensions, standards and paperwork

**ISO container**
A container built to the international ISO standards for dimensions and corner castings. This lets it be loaded and stacked worldwide with standardised equipment (crane, hook-lift truck, semi-trailer).

**TEU**
Short for "Twenty-foot Equivalent Unit", the measure of capacity for container ships and terminals. A 20-foot container counts as 1 TEU, a 40-foot container as 2 TEU. Rarely relevant for private buyers, but it turns up often in listings that come from the shipping trade.

**High cube**
A container with increased standard height (usually 2.90 m instead of 2.59 m external height). The extra space is needed for interior insulation, floor build-up and ceiling lining during conversion. More on standard dimensions in [Sizes & dimensions](/en/ratgeber/container-groessen-und-masse).

**CSC plate**
A metal plate on the container confirming approval under the international CSC Convention for sea transport, and documenting regular inspections. Not required for pure land use, but it hints at the year of manufacture and origin. Details in [Container number & CSC plate](/en/ratgeber/container-nummer-csc-plakette).

**Corner casting**
The four reinforced corner fittings on every container. They carry the entire load when stacking, lifting and lashing, which makes them the single most important spot to check when assessing condition.

## Condition and trade

**One-trip**
A container that has completed only a single freight journey – usually the delivery run from the factory – and is therefore practically as good as new. The term says nothing about the actual quality of the workmanship.

**Cargo-worthy**
A container approved for sea transport: watertight, with a valid CSC plate and functioning locking gear. A grade below near-new stock, but still suitable for demanding use.

**WWT (wind and watertight)**
The container reliably keeps out rain and draughts but is no longer approved for international sea transport. Usually sufficient for land-based storage. A full overview of condition grades is in [Buying a used container](/en/ratgeber/gebrauchten-container-kaufen).

**Corten steel**
Weathering steel that forms a dense protective rust layer on its surface, which then protects it from further corrosion. Many shipping containers are made of Corten steel; that protective layer must not simply be stripped off when painting or repairing.

## Technical and conversion terms

**Thermal bridge**
A point in the building envelope where heat escapes noticeably faster to the outside than in the surrounding area – on steel containers, typically along the continuous steel profiles. Thermal bridges promote condensation and mould. Explained in detail in [Thermal bridges in containers](/en/ratgeber/waermebruecken-container).

**Spray foam (PU insulation)**
Polyurethane insulation that is sprayed on as a liquid and then expands. It seals even uneven steel surfaces without gaps, which is why it is often used in container conversions. Details in [Spray foam insulation](/en/ratgeber/spruehschaum-daemmung-container).

**Floor load / load-bearing capacity**
The weight a container floor can carry permanently without deforming. Relevant both for storing heavy goods and for later adding screed or tiling.

## Law and permits

**Change of use**
A building-law term for switching a building's or plot's approved use, for example from storage to occupied space. A change of use can require a permit even without any structural work. More in [Building application & change of use](/en/ratgeber/container-bauantrag-nutzungsaenderung).

**Special use of public land**
Use of public streets or paths beyond ordinary common use, for example temporarily placing a container on a pavement. This usually requires a special-use permit from the local authority. Details in [Containers on public land](/en/ratgeber/container-oeffentlicher-grund-sondernutzung).

**Warranty**
A seller's statutory liability for defects that already existed at the time of sale. Private sellers frequently exclude warranty by contract, while commercial sellers can only limit it to a certain extent. Covered in detail in [Purchase contract & warranty](/en/ratgeber/container-kaufvertrag-gewaehrleistung).

## Important note

> The explanations in this glossary are general and do not replace legal advice. In particular, building-law terms such as change of use and special use of public land are interpreted differently across German states and municipalities. Binding information is provided only by the responsible authority.

**Note on German law:** Terms such as change of use, special use of public land and the statutory warranty rules reflect German law. Outside Germany, check the equivalent local rules.

## Conclusion

Knowing these basic terms makes it much easier to make sense of container listings, contracts and guides – from condition grade to build type to building-law jargon. Each term links to a fuller article for anyone who wants to dig deeper into a specific topic.
