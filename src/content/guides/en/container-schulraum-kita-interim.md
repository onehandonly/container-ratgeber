Schools and nurseries sometimes need rooms that are not meant to exist in two years' time: the old building is being refurbished, a new building has been approved but not yet built, or the number of children is rising faster than the number of places. Room modules built to container dimensions are a common answer because they are prefabricated and can be dismantled afterwards. This article is aimed at operators, head teachers, nursery managers and project managers, and answers four questions: How many modules do I need? What must I clarify beforehand? Which technical points decide room quality? And roughly what does an interim solution cost?

It is not a substitute for planning by architects and specialist engineers, nor for advice from the authorities. The legal position described is that of **Germany**. All amounts are indicative figures without guarantee. An overview of the container type is given under [School and nursery containers](/en/container/schul-und-kitacontainer).

## First question: container or something else?

For school and nursery use, the realistic option is room modules – factory-built units with insulation, windows, ventilation and fire protection. A used shipping container with a retrofit fit-out is generally not the right basis: the effort for insulation, openings, sound insulation and documentation quickly exceeds the price of a ready-made module. For the distinction see [Office container quality: kit or European build](/en/ratgeber/buerocontainer-qualitaet-bausatz) and [Fitting out a residential container](/en/ratgeber/wohncontainer-ausbauen).

| Question | Favours temporary modules | Favours another solution |
|---|---|---|
| How long is the space needed? | about 6 months to a few years, with a clear end date | permanently: new build or extension, conventional or modular |
| How fast must it be ready? | Prefabrication shortens on-site construction time | Time is not critical |
| Who owns the modules? | Rental: no capital tied up | Purchase only if reuse at changing sites is foreseeable |
| How robust must the use be? | School-grade floors, doors and walls are available | Domestic or office-grade fittings often do not survive school life |

For the difference between container construction, prefabricated houses and masonry see [Modular, prefabricated or solid construction](/en/ratgeber/modulbau-fertighaus-massivbau-vergleich).

## Calculating the space needed

The calculation does not start with the container but with the room schedule: which rooms must be replaced, and what areas does your federal state or school authority require for them? Binding area standards for classrooms, group rooms and ancillary rooms differ by state, school type and operator. Use the values from your school authority or youth welfare office, not the figures in this article.

By way of illustration, a calculation with **assumed** values: a classroom is to have about 60 m² usable area. The module basis is a 20 ft unit with the internal area of a container (about 5.90 × 2.35 m).

| Size | Internal dimensions | Area |
|---|---|---|
| 20 ft module | approx. 5.90 × 2.35 m | approx. 13.9 m² |
| 40 ft module | approx. 12.03 × 2.35 m | approx. 28.3 m² |
| Four 20 ft modules side by side and end to end | – | approx. 55 m² |
| Two 40 ft modules side by side | – | approx. 56 m² |

Both variants come close to the assumed 60 m² but just miss it. That is a typical finding: the module grid and the room schedule rarely match exactly. Either a third 40 ft module is added (about 85 m², too large and too expensive), or the room schedule is adapted to the grid. Manufacturers also supply modules with dimensions other than ISO containers; ask before fixing the grid. Standard container dimensions are in [Container sizes and dimensions](/en/ratgeber/container-groessen-und-masse).

Note two features of modules with an internal height of about 2.4 m, as is usual for standard containers: the clear room height is tight for rooms with many occupants, which puts more strain on air quality and heat. And load-bearing walls between coupled modules can only be removed with structural design.

## Clarifying permit and operating licence

This is where schedule and cost are decided. There are two separate tracks to start in parallel.

**Building law.** State building codes often treat schools and nurseries as special buildings, in some cases depending on the number of people or children. As a rule this means stricter requirements for escape routes, fire protection and documentation. Whether a procedure is needed, and which, depends on the federal state, the location, the duration and the use. A time-limited building permit for the interim period is a common route; clarify it early with the building control authority. General background: [Containers and planning permission](/en/ratgeber/container-baugenehmigung) and [Planning application and change of use](/en/ratgeber/container-bauantrag-nutzungsaenderung). For schools there are additionally state-specific school building rules against which the authority assesses the requirements.

**Nursery operating licence.** Operating a day-care centre requires a licence under Book VIII of the German Social Code (§ 45 SGB VIII); the competent authority is the supra-local youth welfare office or the state body determined by state law. The authority checks whether the premises are suitable for the well-being of the children. Moving into container modules therefore has to be notified not only under building law but also under operating law. Ask before ordering which space requirements apply.

**Other bodies.** The fire service or fire protection assessor, the accident insurance provider (for schools, the state accident insurance fund) and, where relevant, the public health office are sensible contacts before plans are fixed. If the client is a public body, procurement law also applies, which affects tender duration and contract design.

## Technology that decides room quality

Buildings for children are occupied for long periods, densely, and are sensitive to poor air and noise. This is where interim buildings most often fall short.

| Topic | What to look out for | Further reading |
|---|---|---|
| Ventilation | Window ventilation alone is often not enough in densely occupied rooms. As a guide for indoor air, a CO₂ concentration below about 1,000 ppm applies; values above 2,000 ppm are considered hygienically unacceptable. Mechanical ventilation with heat recovery or CO₂ traffic lights help. | [Ventilation](/en/ratgeber/container-belueftung) |
| Summer heat protection | Lightweight construction heats up. External shading, night ventilation and window orientation belong in the design, not in retrofits. | [Insulation](/en/ratgeber/container-daemmen), [Thermal bridges](/en/ratgeber/waermebruecken-container) |
| Sound | Room acoustics (reverberation) affect speech intelligibility. Partitions between classrooms need adequate sound insulation; consider impact sound in multi-storey arrangements. | [Noise control and acoustics](/en/ratgeber/container-laermschutz-akustik) |
| Fire protection | Escape routes, smoke detectors or a fire alarm system and extinguishers follow the fire safety concept. Individual modules must not be viewed in isolation. | [Fire protection](/en/ratgeber/container-brandschutz-vorschriften) |
| Sanitary | Number and fittings of toilets depend on occupancy and age group; children need different heights. Clarify water and drainage connections early. | [Water and drainage](/en/ratgeber/container-wasseranschluss-abwasser) |
| Electricity | Digital equipment, kitchen and ventilation increase the connected load. | [Power connection](/en/ratgeber/container-stromanschluss) |
| Accessibility | Ramps, door widths and an accessible toilet are part of the design for publicly accessible buildings; state rules apply (often DIN 18040-1). | – |

## Cost example: how rental duration matters

The following calculation is an **example with assumptions**, not a quotation. It assumes a classroom group made of four 20 ft modules in school grade:

| Item | Assumption |
|---|---|
| Rent per module per month | approx. €350 (upper range for insulated office and room modules according to [Rental prices and calculation](/en/ratgeber/container-mietpreise-kalkulation); school-grade fit-out may be higher) |
| Delivery and collection | approx. €250 each per module, one-off |
| Setting up, connections, foundations, removal | lump sum approx. €6,000, one-off |

Monthly rent is 4 × €350 = €1,400. Transport for four modules (delivery and collection) costs a one-off 4 × 2 × €250 = €2,000. Adding €6,000 for set-up gives €8,000 in one-off costs.

| Rental period | Total cost | Cost per month |
|---|---|---|
| 12 months | approx. €24,800 | approx. €2,070 |
| 18 months | approx. €33,200 | approx. €1,840 |
| 24 months | approx. €41,600 | approx. €1,730 |

The effect: the shorter the use, the more heavily the one-off costs weigh on the monthly price. Pay attention to minimum rental period, extension options and who owes the removal. An extension is usually cheaper than a second set-up. General notes on the contract: [Rental agreement and law](/en/ratgeber/container-mietvertrag-recht) and [Renting a container: process](/en/ratgeber/container-mieten-ablauf). Whether buying rather than renting makes sense is something you can check with the [rent-or-buy calculator](/en/rechner/kaufen-oder-mieten).

Not included in the example are planning costs, furniture, outdoor areas, replacement playground, fire detection technology and any relocation costs. Depending on the project these can make up a considerable share.

## Plan the schedule backwards

The most critical date is usually not delivery but the start of operation. Plan backwards from the desired start, for example the start of the school year:

1. **Needs and room schedule** defined (operator, management).
2. **Preliminary talks** with building control, fire protection and – for nurseries – the youth welfare office.
3. **Quotations** with itemised prices, see [Comparing quotations](/en/ratgeber/container-angebote-vergleichen).
4. **Permit procedure** and, where applicable, tender.
5. **Foundations and connections**, see [Foundations](/en/ratgeber/container-fundament).
6. **Delivery and assembly**, acceptance, briefing.
7. **Operating licence or release for use**; only then move in.

Allow several months for steps 2 to 4; the duration depends on the authority and procedure. Starting in spring to open in autumn leaves little margin.

## Checklist for operators and managers

- Room schedule and area standards agreed with the school authority or youth welfare office
- Duration of use and removal date fixed in writing
- Building control contacted: procedure, time limit, special-building requirements
- Fire safety concept available; escape routes and extinguishing equipment planned
- Ventilation concept (fresh air, CO₂) and shading included in the quotations
- Sound insulation between rooms and room acoustics considered
- Sanitary facilities for children, accessibility and water connection clarified
- Foundation, power, data and access for delivery and crane checked
- Rental agreement: minimum term, extension, removal, liability for damage
- Insurance for building and contents clarified, see [Insuring a container](/en/ratgeber/container-versichern)
- Move in only after acceptance and, for nurseries, after the operating licence

## Important note

This article gives general information on a legally and technically demanding subject and is not legal, building or fire protection advice. Building law and school building rules are state law in Germany; the nursery operating licence is governed by Book VIII of the Social Code and the relevant state law. Requirements differ by federal state, location, duration and use. Binding information is provided by the building control authority, the fire protection authority and the youth welfare office; involve architects and specialist engineers in planning. All figures are example values and not quotations.
