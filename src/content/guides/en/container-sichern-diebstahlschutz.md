A shipping container is made of profiled Corten steel sheet and can hardly be forced open with simple hand tools. Walls and roof can be cut with an angle grinder, but that is loud, slow and conspicuous – so most offenders go for the doors. The real weak point is the doors and their locking mechanism. Anyone who focuses protection here secures both container and contents most effectively. This guide shows which mechanical, structural and electronic measures are worthwhile and roughly what they cost.

## Understanding risks and weak points

Before investing in locks and technology, it pays to take a sober look at the typical points of attack.

**The door locking mechanism.** Standard containers are locked with four vertical locking bars (two per door leaf) operated by turning handles; the bars engage in cams on the frame at top and bottom. These handles are the preferred point of attack: if the padlock is destroyed or the locking gear is levered open, the doors are free. Many handles can be secured with a simple bolt or pin – if this is missing, the locking mechanism can even be released without tools.

**The exposed padlock.** A normal padlock with a visible shackle is cut through in seconds with bolt cutters. Visibility here is a disadvantage, not an advantage.

**The location.** Containers often stand in remote spots – on construction sites, storage yards or at the edge of a property. Lack of lighting, no view from the road and no neighbours give offenders time and quiet to work. A poorly chosen location renders even good locks ineffective.

**The value of the contents.** Tools, machines, copper and fuel are popular targets. Anyone who knows valuable goods are inside will invest more effort. Discretion is therefore a form of protection in itself.

## Mechanical security: the first line of defence

Mechanics are the foundation of any container security. They delay and deter – and time is a thief's greatest enemy.

**Lockbox / lock case.** The lock case is a welded-on or pre-fitted steel box that fully encloses and conceals the padlock. Bolt cutters and angle grinders can no longer reach the shackle. Many newer containers already have a lockbox from the factory; retrofitting almost always pays off.

**Hardened padlock / discus lock.** The lock case should hold a padlock without an exposed shackle. A discus lock (round or disc lock) offers hardly any surface for bolt cutters, because the shackle sits almost entirely inside the housing. Look for hardened steel and a high protection rating.

**Container lock / cross-bar lock.** The cross-bar sits across both door handles and blocks their rotation. Even if a lock falls, the locking gear stays mechanically jammed. The cross-bar is clearly visible and therefore also acts as a deterrent.

**Bolt securing of the locking gear.** The turning handles of the locking bars can be secured against rotation with a hardened bolt or a security screw. This inexpensive measure prevents tool-free release and complements lockbox and cross-bar.

| Measure | Protective effect | approx. cost |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Lockbox / lock case (retrofit) | Conceals the lock, protects against bolt cutters and grinder | approx. €30–80 |
| Hardened discus / padlock | Hardly any surface for bolt cutters | approx. €20–60 |
| Container lock / cross-bar lock | Blocks both door handles, strong deterrent | approx. €60–150 |
| Bolt securing of door handles | Prevents tool-free release | approx. €10–30 |
| Lighting with motion sensor | Removes cover, deters | approx. €20–100 |
| Alarm system (standalone) | Reports opening, deters | approx. €40–200 |
| GPS tracker | Location tracking after theft | approx. €30–150 plus subscription |
| Trail camera / video surveillance | Evidence, deterrence | approx. €60–300 |

The strongest effect comes from combining measures: lockbox with discus lock plus cross-bar plus secured handles. Each layer costs the offender time, and most give up before they get through.

## Location and visibility

Structural and organisational measures often cost nothing and yet have a strong effect.

**Face the doors to a wall or to each other.** Position the container so that the doors face a solid wall, a fence or another container. With two containers, the door sides can be placed against each other so they block one another – neither can then be opened. Doors facing a building or the property boundary make any access difficult.

**Good lighting.** Motion-controlled lights remove an offender's cover. A suddenly flaring light has a strong psychological effect and makes the container visible to neighbours or passers-by. Solar-powered floodlights are quick to fit even without a power connection.

**Visibility instead of hiding.** A container visible from the road or from occupied buildings is targeted less often. High hedges and screens protect privacy, but they also give the thief cover.

## Electronic security

Technology does not replace mechanics but usefully complements it – above all for alerting and tracking.

**Alarm system.** Standalone, battery-powered alarm systems with a door or vibration sensor sound a loud siren when opened. Models with a mobile module additionally send a notification to your smartphone. For remote locations, wireless, battery-operated systems are practical since no power connection is needed.

**GPS tracker.** A hidden GPS tracker does not help prevent theft but aids recovery – especially when a whole container or valuable machines are hauled away. Most trackers require an inexpensive data subscription.

**Camera / video surveillance.** Visible cameras deter, recordings secure evidence. Off-grid trail cameras with motion triggering and mobile sending are well suited to sites without infrastructure. Observe data protection rules: film only your own premises, not neighbouring plots or public paths. Whether and how a camera is permitted depends on data protection law (in Germany the GDPR and national rules) and the individual case (see “Important note”).

## Organisational measures

**Store nothing valuable in plain sight.** What cannot be seen from outside is less tempting. Stow high-value goods to the rear and out of sight when the doors are open.

**Keep an inventory list.** An up-to-date list with photos, serial numbers and values speeds up reporting to police and insurer in the event of a loss and improves the chances of recovery.

**Check your insurance.** Clarify whether container and contents are covered by a contents, construction or business policy. Note the required security standards – many insurers require specific locks or alarms as a condition for payment. Document your security measures. Details on policies, exclusions and obligations are in the guide [Insuring a container](/en/ratgeber/container-versichern).

For more on placement, see the guides on [container foundations](/en/ratgeber/container-fundament) and [neighbour law and boundary distances](/en/ratgeber/container-nachbarrecht-grenzabstand). Whether your site needs planning permission depends on location, duration and use – see [Planning permission for containers](/en/ratgeber/container-baugenehmigung).

## Worked example: what a staged security concept costs

The table adds up the ranges from the overview above. The levels build on each other; figures are rough guides without guarantee, and running costs (e.g. mobile or tracker subscriptions) come on top.

| Level | Measures included | approx. total cost |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 1 – Basic | Lockbox, discus lock, cross-bar, bolt securing | approx. €120–320 |
| 2 – Extended | Level 1 plus motion-sensor lighting and standalone alarm | approx. €180–620 |
| 3 – Comprehensive | Level 2 plus GPS tracker and trail camera | approx. €270–1,070 plus subscription |

For context, an assumed example: if you store tools and equipment worth €5,000, level 1 equals about 2.4–6.4% of the contents' value. At €30,000, even level 3 at no more than €1,070 is around 3.6% – and is often a condition for the insurer to pay out after a loss. Whether level 2 or 3 is worthwhile therefore depends less on the container's price than on the value of what is inside.

> Note: The percentages follow from the assumed example values; they are not market statistics.

## Which level suits which use?

| Use | Typical risk | Recommendation |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Garden store, visible, low-value contents | Opportunistic theft | Level 1 |
| Container on a building site with tools and machines | Targeted theft, often at night and at weekends | Level 2, doors to a wall; remove contents in the evening where possible |
| Remote plot without neighbours | Long undisturbed attack time | Level 2 or 3, alarm with mobile alert |
| Commercial store with high-value goods | High loss, removal of the whole unit possible | Level 3, clarify insurer requirements beforehand |
| Rented container | Duty of care often lies with the tenant | Agree lock and extra security with the lessor, see [Container rental agreements](/en/ratgeber/container-mietvertrag-recht) |

## Checklist: annual security check

- Check lock, lockbox and cross-bar for smooth operation and damage; lightly lubricate the lock cylinder.
- Inspect door seals, hinges and locking bars (care tips in the guide [Maintaining a container and protecting it from rust](/en/ratgeber/container-pflege-und-rostschutz)).
- Replace batteries in alarm, tracker and camera; test the alert path.
- Check lighting and motion sensors for function and alignment; cut back vegetation that hides the container.
- Update the inventory list and photos.
- Compare the sum insured and the policy's security requirements with the actual set-up.

## If a break-in has happened

1. Change nothing and do not disturb traces; photograph damage and the scene.
2. Report it to the police; an inventory list with serial numbers helps the investigation.
3. Inform your insurer without delay and follow their claims instructions.
4. Secure the container, for instance with a temporary lock, to prevent further damage.

More on using a container as storage is in [Using a storage container properly](/en/ratgeber/lagercontainer-richtig-nutzen). For used containers, check the doors and locking gear as described in the [inspection checklist](/en/ratgeber/container-besichtigung-pruefprotokoll).

## Important note

This guide is not legal, insurance or security advice. Video surveillance is subject to data protection law (in Germany, among others, the GDPR); anyone who records areas beyond their own property or identifiable people must expect legal problems. Which security measures your insurer requires is set out in your policy terms. Costs are approximate ranges and may differ by product and supplier.

## Conclusion

The container itself is robust – it is mainly the doors that need protecting. The most effective strategy is a combination of mechanical layers: a lockbox with a hardened discus lock, a cross-bar over both handles and a bolt securing the locking gear. Together this basic setup costs approx. €120–320 and reliably deters opportunistic offenders. Combined with a smart location with doors facing a wall, good lighting and, where needed, alarm, GPS and camera, a layered protection concept emerges. Anyone who also stores discreetly, keeps an inventory list and checks insurance cover is well prepared for an emergency. Security does not come from a single measure, but from the interplay of many small hurdles that rob the offender of time and quiet.
