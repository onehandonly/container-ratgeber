The classic wooden garden shed has some competition. More and more people are discovering the shipping container as a robust, durable and surprisingly flexible alternative. Whether as a quiet garden office, a well-organised workshop or an atmospheric sauna: a container can be turned into a genuine retreat with manageable effort. In this guide you will learn which usage ideas really work, what you need to consider legally and roughly what costs to expect.

## Why a Container in the Garden?

Containers bring a number of tangible advantages that make them attractive compared to bricked or timber-built garden houses.

- **Robust and durable:** A steel container defies wind, weather and moisture. Where wood eventually rots, steel lasts for decades – and even longer with a little rust prevention.
- **Affordable to buy:** Used sea containers are often significantly cheaper than a high-quality wooden kit of comparable size.
- **Mobile and modular:** A container can be relocated by crane, taken along or combined with additional modules. If you move house, you simply take the garden house with you.
- **Secure:** Solid steel walls and lockable doors reliably protect tools, equipment and valuables from break-ins.
- **Quick to set up:** Prepare the foundation, unload the container, done. The shell stands in a single day.

It is precisely the combination of stability, security and a low entry price that makes the container a serious option – not only for pragmatists, but also for the design-ambitious.

## Usage Ideas: From Garden Office to Sauna

A container is an empty space with potential. The interior of a 20 ft container measures roughly 5.90 × 2.35 m on plan (about 13.9 m²) and about 2.39 m in height, or about 2.70 m for a high cube; details on dimensions and door opening are in [Container Sizes and Dimensions](/en/ratgeber/container-groessen-und-masse). Depending on the fit-out, a completely different place emerges. An overview of the most popular concepts.

**Garden office and home office:** The classic in the age of remote work. An insulated, well-lit container a few steps from the house creates a separation between work and private life – without commuting. Fit-out, indoor climate and costs are covered in [Home Office in a Container](/en/ratgeber/homeoffice-im-container).

**Workshop and hobby room:** Whether a wood workshop, bike store or craft room: the container offers robust space for machines, tools and projects that would be in the way inside the house.

**Storage and tool shed:** Lawnmower, garden furniture, tyres, seasonal goods – the container replaces the overcrowded cellar. It only stays dry with ventilation and an intact roof (see [Container Ventilation](/en/ratgeber/container-belueftung)), and it only becomes theft-resistant with a proper lock and a protected locking mechanism ([Securing a Container](/en/ratgeber/container-sichern-diebstahlschutz)).

**Sauna:** A well-insulated container can be converted into a garden sauna. A heater, wooden panelling inside and a small anteroom turn it into a wellness room. This is also where the requirements are highest: wood-fired and electric heaters need clearances to combustible parts, a wood-fired heater additionally needs a flue and inspection, and the interior lining must withstand heat and moisture. See the guide [Fire Safety in Containers](/en/ratgeber/container-brandschutz-vorschriften).

**Pool and technical house:** Filter system, heat pump and pool accessories find protected space in the container. One section can double as a changing room or outdoor shower.

**Guest room:** With insulation, a window, a bed and a mini bathroom, the container becomes a charming guest house – ideal for visitors who appreciate their own retreat.

### Ideas Compared

| Usage idea | Typical equipment | Effort |
|---|---|---|
| Garden / home office | Insulation, power, window, flooring, heating | Medium |
| Workshop / hobby room | Power, lighting, workbench, possibly light insulation | Low–Medium |
| Storage / tool shed | Shelving, ventilation, lock | Low |
| Sauna | Strong insulation, sauna heater, wood panelling, power | High |
| Pool / technical house | Power, ventilation, water connection | Medium |
| Guest room | Insulation, power, window, mini bathroom, heating | High |

The rule of thumb: the closer the container is to permanent human occupancy, the higher the fit-out standard for insulation, ventilation and services – and the more likely the "garden house" becomes a habitable room in the legal sense.

## Permit and Boundary Distance

The most important point first: a container in the garden is not automatically exempt from permits. Whether you need a building permit depends on the federal state, the size, the use and the location. In Germany, building law is a matter for the individual states, so the regulations vary accordingly.

Roughly speaking: small buildings without a habitable room and without a fireplace are exempt from procedure in many federal states up to a certain volume; the limits differ considerably between the state building codes, and stricter rules apply in the outlying area (section 35 of the Federal Building Code, BauGB). What counts is the enclosed space, i.e. the external volume: a 20 ft container of about 6.06 × 2.44 × 2.59 m comes to roughly 38 m³, and as a high cube at 2.90 m height to roughly 43 m³. Depending on the state's limit it may therefore fall inside or outside the exemption. As soon as people stay in them permanently – for example in a garden office or guest room – or a fireplace such as a sauna heater is used, the requirements rise significantly.

The **boundary distance** to the neighbouring plot is also regulated and varies depending on the federal state and the specific use. A pure tool shed may often move closer to the boundary than a heated habitable room. Be sure to clarify this in advance with the building authority; background on setback areas and neighbour consent is in [Neighbour Law and Boundary Distance](/en/ratgeber/container-nachbarrecht-grenzabstand), and on development plans in [Development Plans and Outlying Areas](/en/ratgeber/container-bebauungsplan-aussenbereich).

> Important: Never rely on blanket statements from the internet. Ask your local building supervisory authority before setting up. A short phone call can save you an expensive dismantling in case of doubt.

You will find details, exemption limits and typical pitfalls in the detailed article [Container Building Permit](/en/ratgeber/container-baugenehmigung).

## Insulation, Power and Light

Steel is an excellent conductor of heat – and in its raw state that is exactly the problem. Without insulation, the container becomes an oven in summer and a freezer in winter. In addition, condensation threatens on the cold interior walls, which leads to rust and mould over time.

For every use with quality of stay, proper insulation is therefore mandatory. Common builds consist of a substructure, insulation material and interior panelling made of wood or boards. A correct vapour barrier is important so that no moisture gets into the build-up. You can read how this works professionally in the guide [Insulating a Container](/en/ratgeber/container-daemmen).

On the subject of **power**, you have two paths: a fixed supply from the house (installed and protected by a qualified electrician, see [Power Supply for Containers](/en/ratgeber/container-stromanschluss)) or a self-sufficient solution with a solar module, battery and inverter ([Solar Panels on a Container](/en/ratgeber/photovoltaik-auf-dem-container)). For an occasionally used garden office, a small photovoltaic island for light, laptop and phone charging is often enough. Plan for sufficient sockets – retrofitting is laborious.

**Light** is an underestimated factor. Windows and a door with a glass section raise the quality of stay enormously. Every cut-out in the steel wall interferes with the structure, however, and needs a welded-in frame – procedure and costs are in [Retrofitting Windows and Doors](/en/ratgeber/container-fenster-tueren-nachruesten). Roof windows or a strip of glazing bring additional daylight into the depth of the room. For the evenings, warm-white LED lights create a homely atmosphere.

## Looks and Cladding

The raw steel look is not everyone's taste – and it does not have to be. A container can be transformed in design far beyond its industrial origin.

- **Wood cladding:** A ventilated façade of larch, Douglas fir or thermally treated wood turns the steel box into a high-quality garden building that blends in harmoniously.
- **Paint:** If you want to keep the container character, you can create a fresh, individual look with special metal paint – from restrained anthracite to a bold accent colour.
- **Greening:** Trellises with climbing plants let the walls turn green over the years. A **green roof** additionally improves the microclimate and retains rainwater, but it adds load and requires a watertight roof skin; check load capacity and sealing beforehand ([Roof Sealing and Roof Load](/en/ratgeber/container-dach-abdichten-dachlast)). Painting and rust prevention at cut edges and corners are covered in [Care and Rust Protection](/en/ratgeber/container-pflege-und-rostschutz).

With a small terrace in front, a few planters and coherent lighting, the container quickly becomes a place where you like to spend time – visually far removed from any construction-site image.

## Cost Range: Two Worked Examples

Costs depend strongly on condition, size and fit-out standard. The following figures are **guide values without guarantee**, aligned with the guides [What Does a Container Cost?](/en/ratgeber/was-kostet-ein-container) and [Home Office in a Container](/en/ratgeber/homeoffice-im-container). Only a concrete quote shows how far your project deviates.

**Example A – simple storage (used 20 ft container):**

| Item | Range approx. |
|---|---|
| Used container | €1,500 – 3,000 |
| Delivery with crane or loader crane | €250 – 800 |
| Point foundations / base | €600 – 2,000 |
| Shelving, ventilation, lock (assumption) | €200 – 800 |
| **Total** | **approx. €2,550 – 6,600** |

**Example B – garden office (used 20 ft high cube, two windows, air conditioner):**

| Item | Range approx. |
|---|---|
| Used container | €2,000 – 3,500 |
| Delivery and crane | €250 – 800 |
| Point foundations | €600 – 2,000 |
| Two windows incl. openings | €1,800 – 3,600 |
| Interior insulation, mineral wool (approx. 58 m²) | €3,480 – 6,380 |
| Interior plasterboard lining (approx. 58 m²) | €2,030 – 3,480 |
| Basic electrical installation with sub-distribution | €2,200 – 4,900 |
| Split air conditioner with heating function | €1,500 – 3,500 |
| Floor build-up and covering | €600 – 1,500 |
| **Total** | **approx. €14,460 – 29,660** |

The difference shows where the money goes: for storage the container itself makes up roughly half, for the garden office only about an eighth to a seventh – the rest goes into the fit-out. If you do a lot yourself, you save mainly on lining, flooring and painting; electrics and cuts into the steel wall belong in professional hands. A sauna or a guest room with a mini bathroom will tend to exceed Example B because of wet-room fittings, water and wastewater ([Water Supply and Wastewater](/en/ratgeber/container-wasseranschluss-abwasser)). Running costs and loss of value are covered in [Running Costs and Depreciation](/en/ratgeber/container-betriebskosten-wertverlust).

> Note: All prices are non-binding approximate values (as of 2026) and can vary strongly by region and market situation. Obtain several quotes ([Comparing Quotes](/en/ratgeber/container-angebote-vergleichen)).

## Checklist Before Installation

- Use decided (storage, workshop or habitable room) – it determines permit and fit-out.
- Building authority asked: permit requirement, boundary distance, development plan.
- Neighbours informed before the crane arrives.
- Foundation planned ([Container Foundations](/en/ratgeber/container-fundament)) and access for lorry and crane measured.
- Insulation, ventilation and power designed before the fit-out, not afterwards.
- Insurance cover clarified ([Insuring a Container](/en/ratgeber/container-versichern)).

## Important Note

> This article is no substitute for legal, building or electrical advice in an individual case. It describes the situation under German law: building law is state law (state building codes, and in the outlying area additionally section 35 BauGB); whether your garden-house container needs a permit depends on location, duration and use. More in [Container Building Permit](/en/ratgeber/container-baugenehmigung).

## Conclusion

A container is far more than a steel box for tools. Robust, secure and comparatively affordable, it offers the ideal basis for an individual garden house – from a minimalist tool shed to a full-fledged home office or guest room. Three things are decisive for a good result: an honest clarification of the legal situation before setting up, professional insulation for lasting quality of stay, and a well-thought-out design that integrates the container into the garden. Those who observe these points get a durable, flexible and characterful garden house that will bring joy for many years.
