This guide covers **delivery day**: the minutes while the container is being put down and the driver is still on site. It complements two neighbouring topics without overlapping them. How to inspect a used container **before buying** is covered in the [inspection protocol for the viewing](/en/ratgeber/container-besichtigung-pruefprotokoll). How delivery is organised and what it costs is covered in [Transport and Delivery Costs](/en/ratgeber/container-transport-lieferkosten). This guide is about **acceptance**: is what arrives what was agreed – and is it undamaged and correctly positioned? The legal points refer to German law.

## Why delivery day matters, legally and practically

Loading, the journey and unloading are the typical places where damage occurs. If you do not check the container on delivery, you face two problems later: you can hardly prove that damage already existed on delivery, and the driver – the carrier's contact – has gone. There is also a practical side: a container with its doors facing the wrong way can only be corrected by another crane or hook-lift visit.

Who bears the risk until handover is governed by the sales contract (for consumers buying from a dealer, in principle only from handover to the buyer); the basics are in [Sales Contract and Warranty](/en/ratgeber/container-kaufvertrag-gewaehrleistung). Regardless of that, in practice: documented damage can be resolved, undocumented damage rarely can.

## Preparation: before the lorry arrives

Most handover problems originate beforehand. Clarify these points the day before:

- Have the **container number** from the contract or order confirmation ready (eleven characters, structure explained in [Container Number and CSC Plate](/en/ratgeber/container-nummer-csc-plakette)).
- Have the **condition description and photos** from the offer or viewing printed or on your phone – they are your target state.
- **Mark the position**: location of the contact points, door direction and alignment with paint or battens. For the base, see [Container Foundations](/en/ratgeber/container-fundament).
- **Access clear and load-bearing**: move vehicles, check branches and cables. Otherwise the failed trip described in the transport guide as the most expensive avoidable mistake looms.
- **A contact person on site**: whoever signs must be able to decide.
- **Equipment**: smartphone (dated photos), torch, tape measure, small spirit level or level app, pen, paper for notes.
- **Light and weather**: in darkness or heavy rain you can hardly judge paint damage and dents; tell the driver in good time and agree on a daylight slot.

## The procedure as a timeline

Allow about **20 to 30 minutes** for the handover. Many carriers include a standing time of roughly 15 to 30 minutes at no extra charge and then bill waiting time (order of magnitude approx. €60–120 per started hour according to the transport guide). Ask what is included in the offer and keep to it.

| When | What happens | What you do |
|---|---|---|
| Arrival | Lorry on the access road, not yet unloaded | Compare the container number on the end wall and plate with the contract; photograph all four sides **before** unloading |
| Before putting down | Driver positions the lorry | Confirm door direction and position with the driver again, point out obstacles |
| Putting down | Container is rolled off or lifted | Stand aside at a safe distance, never under the load; watch the movements |
| After putting down, driver still present | Hook or chains released | Walk around with the checklist (below); record damage if necessary |
| Handover | Delivery note is signed | Only sign once remarks are entered; keep a copy or photo of the annotated note |
| After departure | Driver has gone | Photograph any defects you still discover and report them in writing straight away |

## Checklist for the container

This check is shorter than the viewing and has a different aim: you compare against the **target state** and look for **new transport damage**.

| Check | What to look for | Typical sign of transport damage |
|---|---|---|
| Number, type, size | Matches the contract (20 or 40 ft, standard or high cube) | Wrong container delivered |
| Contact area | Corner fittings and floor cross-members underneath | Fresh scrape marks, bent areas |
| Top corner fittings | Lifting points of crane or lifting gear | Burred openings, fresh deformation |
| Walls and roof | Dents, cracks in the panels, fresh paint chips | New dents with bare metal (no surface rust yet) |
| Doors | Open, close and lock both leaves | Sticking doors, bent locking bars |
| Seals | Continuous, not torn | Pinched or torn rubber seal |
| Interior | Light test, floor, smell | New pinpricks of light, fresh damage to the floor |
| Distortion | Measure both door diagonals | Clear difference after impact or lifting damage |
| Accessories | Lock, keys, vents, shelving as per contract | Missing parts |

A tip for telling old from new: fresh damage often shows **bare, bright metal** or no rim of surface rust yet, whereas older damage is rusted or has a paint edge. This is an indication, not proof; do not over-rely on it and compare with the photos from the viewing or offer.

Check the **interior** with the door open and a second person outside. Never lock yourself in the container.

## Checklist for the placement

An undamaged container can still be in the wrong place. Check **before** the driver leaves:

| Item | Target | If not |
|---|---|---|
| Door direction | Towards the planned side, doors can swing fully open | Have it corrected at once while crane or lorry are still there |
| Position and distance | Markings and planned distances kept | Near a boundary, consider [boundary distances](/en/ratgeber/container-nachbarrecht-grenzabstand) first; correct only with the driver |
| Support | All four corners bear, no rocking | Have it packed or re-placed; for foundations see [Container Foundations](/en/ratgeber/container-fundament) |
| Level | Spirit level on roof or floor: only slight tilt | Re-place if it deviates |
| Clearance | Doors open, access and maintenance possible | Correct as for door direction |
| Ground | No settlement under the contact points | On soft ground re-place rather than improvising later |

Why this matters **before** departure is shown by the worked example below: a later correction is almost always a second trip with a lorry-mounted crane, which according to the transport guide means roughly €350–800 per trip. How a container is lifted properly is covered in [Lifting a Container Correctly](/en/ratgeber/container-richtig-anheben).

## Decision: accept, accept with reservation or refuse

After the walk-around there are three routes. The decision depends on how serious the findings are.

| Finding | Recommendation | What the delivery note should say |
|---|---|---|
| Nothing unusual | Accept | "No objections" – only if you really checked |
| Minor damage (scratches, small dents, paint chips), container usable | Accept with reservation | Specific description of the damage, location and date, photo references |
| Damage affecting use or safety (distorted frame, sticking doors, holes, leaks) | Refuse acceptance or accept only under an explicit reservation; consult the seller | Describe the damage precisely, "acceptance refused" or "accepted subject to inspection" |
| Wrong type, size or number | Refuse acceptance | State the deviation ("delivered 20 ft standard, ordered 20 ft high cube") |
| Access or ground unsuitable | The driver decides whether to put it down | Note the reason, consult the seller |

Important: **remarks must be specific.** "Damage possible" is worthless; "dent approx. 30 cm in the left side wall, 80 cm from the rear corner post, paint cracked" holds up. Photograph every spot with a tape measure for scale, plus an overview showing where it is on the container and one showing the container number.

Do not refuse acceptance out of annoyance at trifles: an unjustified refusal can lead to extra costs (failed trip, return delivery). Accepting with reservation plus a written notice of defects usually protects your rights better.

## Deadlines: who must notify what and when

The deadlines depend on your role and the constellation. This summarises the basics of German law.

| Constellation | Basics | Practical consequence |
|---|---|---|
| Consumer buys from a dealer | No statutory duty to give notice of defects like between merchants; warranty rights under sales law | Still note it immediately and notify in writing promptly; otherwise evidence problems |
| Business buys from business | Duty to inspect and give notice of defects (section 377 German Commercial Code): inspect without undue delay, report apparent defects without undue delay | Rights can be lost if you are late |
| Damage caused by the carrier | Special notification deadlines for transport damage (section 438 Commercial Code): damage apparent from outside no later than on delivery, damage not apparent within seven days | Note it on the delivery note; report concealed damage quickly |
| Private to private | Statutory warranty rights, often excluded by contract | Record the condition together at handover, also for your own protection |

Whether the damage is to be claimed from the seller, the carrier or your insurer depends on who commissioned the transport and who bears the risk. If in doubt, clarify with the seller; for insurance see [Insuring a Container](/en/ratgeber/container-versichern).

## Worked example: what a remark is worth

The figures are **assumed guide values**. A consumer buys a used 20-foot storage container from a dealer for €2,400. On delivery, three fresh damages are found:

| Damage | Remedy | Guide value (approx.) |
|---|---|---|
| Dent in the side wall, paint cracked | Straighten, fill, repaint | €150–400 |
| Paint damage at the corner area | Sand, prime, repaint | €50–150 |
| Door seal torn | Replace seal set | €100–250 |
| **Total** | | **approx. €300–800** |

With a remark and photos the damage is clearly documented as delivery damage; the seller must repair it or adjust the price. Without a remark the buyer is often in a weaker position on the question of proof. At the upper end it is about €800, i.e. one third of the purchase price (800 ÷ 2,400).

A second example on placement: the container stands with its doors facing the house wall, and the driver has gone. Moving it again needs a lorry with a loader crane, which according to the transport guide means roughly €350–800 per trip (local area). That comes on top of the first delivery. Checking the door direction costs one minute.

## Rented containers: same logic, different goal

When renting, the protocol also matters for the **return**: the condition at handover is the yardstick against which wear and damage are measured at collection. A dated handover protocol with photos (outsides, doors, floor, roof, container number) protects against disputes over the deposit. The rental process is covered in [Renting a Container: Step by Step](/en/ratgeber/container-mieten-ablauf), the contract side in [Rental Contract and Law](/en/ratgeber/container-mietvertrag-recht).

## Template for the handover protocol

You can copy and adapt this skeleton:

- Date, time, place, names (driver, recipient)
- Container number per contract / on container: matches yes / no
- Type and size per contract / delivered
- External condition: no objections / finding (place, type, size, photo no.)
- Doors and locks: working yes / no
- Interior and floor: no objections / finding
- Placement: door direction, support, tilt – target met yes / no
- Accessories per contract complete yes / no
- Acceptance: unconditional / with reservation / refused
- Signatures of both sides; each side receives a copy (or photo)

## Common mistakes on delivery day

- **Signing blank.** Never sign "no objections" before the walk-around.
- **Giving in to time pressure.** The driver has a schedule; the inspection is still your right. Plan it in and say so beforehand.
- **Photos only after departure.** The link to the handover is then missing.
- **Repairing the defect yourself before the seller could respond.** That can endanger claims and evidence; see [Sales Contract and Warranty](/en/ratgeber/container-kaufvertrag-gewaehrleistung).
- **Not checking the placement.** Door direction and support are the most common avoidable corrections.

## Important note

This guide gives general information on the basics of German sales and transport law and is not legal advice. Deadlines and legal consequences depend on the contract, the parties and the individual case. Prices are guide values without guarantee. For major damage or disputes, contact a lawyer or a consumer advice centre.

## Conclusion

The handover on delivery day is a short appointment with a big effect: compare the container number, take photos before unloading, walk around with the checklist, check the placement, word remarks specifically and only then sign. Anyone who plans for this separates delivery damage cleanly from later defects, avoids the second trip and has solid evidence in a dispute. What applies legally beyond delivery day is covered in [Sales Contract and Warranty](/en/ratgeber/container-kaufvertrag-gewaehrleistung).
