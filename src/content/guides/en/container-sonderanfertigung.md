The market offers two routes to a container made to measure: the **standard container with conversion** (a sea container in which openings, insulation and fittings are done to your wishes) and the **custom build**, where a manufacturer fabricates frame and shell to your dimensions, for instance as a room module of any length. Both are often called "custom-made", yet they differ technically, in price and contractually. This guide helps with the decision and with placing the order. Legal remarks refer to Germany.

## Standard, conversion or custom build?

| Criterion | Standard container from stock | Standard container with conversion | Custom build |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Dimensions | fixed (ISO external sizes, see [sizes and dimensions](/en/ratgeber/container-groessen-und-masse)) | as standard, interior adjustable | free within manufacturing and transport limits |
| Lead time | often short, depending on stock | longer because of the conversion | usually longest; agree it in writing |
| Planning effort | low | medium | high (drawings, coordination, possibly structural calculations) |
| Price | lowest entry | extra cost depending on scope | usually highest per m² |
| Stacking, shipping, resale | possible because standardised | limited, depending on the alteration | usually not tied to ISO logistics |
| Contractual risk | low | medium | higher (deposits, no withdrawal) |

Rule of thumb: if your project fits an interior of 5.90 × 2.35 m (20 ft) or the 12.03 m length (40 ft), a standard container with conversion is usually the more economical route. A custom build pays off when dimensions, structure, appearance or logistics genuinely cannot be met otherwise – for example widths ISO containers do not offer, or modules that must connect to a building. More on the quality of room modules: [Office containers: quality and kit](/en/ratgeber/buerocontainer-qualitaet-bausatz).

## Worked example: what is left inside?

A common planning mistake is to calculate with the **interior dimensions of the empty sea container**. As soon as you insulate and line it, the space shrinks. Example with assumed build-ups (assumption, not a standard): 75 mm per side wall (e.g. 60 mm insulation plus 15 mm lining), ceiling 75 mm, floor 80 mm.

| Dimension | Empty 20 ft container (approx.) | After fit-out (example) |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Length | 5.90 m | approx. 5.75 m (example: 75 mm at each end) |
| Width | 2.35 m | 2.20 m (2.35 − 2 × 0.075) |
| Height, standard | 2.39 m | approx. 2.24 m (2.39 − 0.08 − 0.075) |
| Height, high cube | 2.70 m | approx. 2.55 m (2.70 − 0.155) |
| Floor area | approx. 13.9 m² | approx. 12.7 m² (5.75 × 2.20) |

In this example the loss is around 9 % of the floor area – and the clear height in a standard container ends up well below 2.40 m. For habitable rooms the state building codes contain minimum heights that vary by state and use; check this before ordering. If you know exactly which interior dimension you need, calculate backwards: usable size plus build-up gives the raw size required. If that does not fit in a 20 ft container, a high cube ([high-cube container](/en/container/high-cube-container)) or a larger type often helps. For the insulation build-up see [Insulating a container](/en/ratgeber/container-daemmen).

## Transport limits: where custom builds hit boundaries

Fabrication is rarely the problem; the route to the site often is. Common limits in German road traffic are a vehicle and load width of 2.55 m and an overall height of 4.00 m including the vehicle. If a load is wider or higher, a permit or exemption, escort vehicles and longer planning time usually follow. An ISO container, 2.438 m wide, stays under that width. A custom build 3.00 m wide, for example, usually needs an abnormal-load permit. Details and cost factors are in [Transport and delivery costs](/en/ratgeber/container-transport-lieferkosten) and [Transporting a container](/en/ratgeber/container-transportieren). Before ordering, clarify whether the access route, the crane standing area and the siting location can carry the vehicle and lifting equipment.

## Typical special requests and what they lead to

- **Openings in the side walls:** in a sea container the corrugated steel contributes to the strength. Large openings weaken the wall and generally need framing and reinforcement designed by the manufacturer. See [Retrofitting windows and doors](/en/ratgeber/container-fenster-tueren-nachruesten).
- **Additional doors and gates:** more access but less closed wall; alternatives are double-door and open-side types ([open-side container](/en/container/open-side-container)).
- **Insulation, electrics, plumbing:** the most frequent extra-cost drivers because trades interlock. Orientation in [Fit-out costs by trade](/en/ratgeber/container-ausbaukosten-gewerke) and [Electrical planning](/en/ratgeber/container-elektroplanung-innenausbau).
- **Paint in a colour of your choice:** coating build-up, surface preparation and drying time are part of the lead time; only a properly built-up system protects lastingly, see [Care and rust protection](/en/ratgeber/container-pflege-und-rostschutz).
- **Adding storeys and coupling:** demanding structurally and for fire protection, see [Stacking and coupling](/en/ratgeber/container-stapeln-und-koppeln).

Each of these alterations can affect load-bearing capacity, fire protection, energy law and permission. See [Fire protection](/en/ratgeber/container-brandschutz-vorschriften), [Energy law](/en/ratgeber/container-geg-energierecht) and [Building permission](/en/ratgeber/container-baugenehmigung).

## Lead time: what to agree realistically

Specific numbers of weeks vary greatly with workload, materials and effort and cannot be stated generally. The usual pattern: the more customisation, the longer from order to delivery. It therefore makes sense to:

1. Agree a binding delivery date or period **in writing**, not just "approx.".
2. Settle what applies in case of delay (grace period, withdrawal, possibly damages under the statutory rules).
3. Allow time buffers if permission, foundation or connections depend on the delivery.
4. Clarify the sequence: foundation and connections must be in place before the container arrives ([foundation](/en/ratgeber/container-fundament)).

## Contract, deposit and withdrawal

Legally, an item made to your specification is usually a contract for the **supply of goods to be manufactured**. In principle, sales law applies to it and – for non-fungible goods – individual rules of contract-for-work law in addition (section 650 of the German Civil Code, BGB). For you as a consumer this means, in brief:

- **No right of withdrawal in distance selling** is possible for goods made to the customer's specification (section 312g(2) no. 1 BGB). Anyone ordering a custom build online or by phone therefore generally has no 14-day withdrawal period. More in [Warranty and purchase contract](/en/ratgeber/container-kaufvertrag-gewaehrleistung).
- **Deposit and stage payments:** deposits are usual for custom builds because the manufacturer procures material. Agree payments by progress rather than high prepayment, and check the supplier carefully; warning signs are in [Fake dealers and prepayment fraud](/en/ratgeber/fake-containerhaendler-vorkasse-betrug).
- **Specification:** dimensions with tolerances, materials, coating, doors/windows, fittings, scope of delivery (unloading? positioning?), documentation.
- **Acceptance:** on handover check dimensions, door function, coating and welds, and record defects in writing; pointers in the [inspection checklist](/en/ratgeber/container-besichtigung-pruefprotokoll).
- **Warranty:** it applies to a custom build too; its scope and duration depend on the contract type and your role (consumer or business).

## Enquiry checklist for comparable quotes

1. Use and duration (permanent, temporary, habitable room yes/no)
2. Desired **usable interior size** (not external size), calculated with build-up thicknesses
3. Openings: number, size, position, door type
4. Insulation, ventilation, electrics, plumbing, heating
5. Coating and colour
6. Siting location, foundation, access, crane standing area
7. Is transport and unloading included in the quote?
8. Delivery date in writing, payment plan, acceptance, warranty
9. Documents you will later need for permission or insurance

If you collect several quotes, compare them using the method in [Comparing container quotes](/en/ratgeber/container-angebote-vergleichen). For price orientation see [What does a container cost](/en/ratgeber/was-kostet-ein-container); custom builds can be considerably above that in individual cases.

## Typical mistakes

- Planning with external or empty interior dimensions instead of the usable size
- Not checking transport limits and access
- A vague specification ("as agreed")
- High prepayment with no counter-performance
- Clarifying permission only after ordering

## Important note

This guide is orientation, not legal advice. The legal classification of a contract depends on the individual case and on your role as consumer or business. Building-law requirements are state law (state building codes) and depend on location, duration and use. Dimensions and build-ups in the examples are assumptions without guarantee.

## Conclusion

A custom build is not a luxury but a trade-off between fit, time, price and contractual risk. Calculate backwards from the usable size, clarify transport in advance and have performance, date and payment plan confirmed in writing.
