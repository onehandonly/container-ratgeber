Steel containers are built for long service, and many last for decades. Sooner or later, though, the question arises what to do with the container: the property is sold, the lease ends, the building site is finished, or the steel shell is so damaged that repair no longer pays. This guide treats the end of use as a planning task in its own right. It complements [Selling a used container](/en/ratgeber/container-gebraucht-verkaufen), which covers selling a working container, with the cases where a decision and dismantling come first. The legal and tax references relate to Germany.

## Assess first: is the container really at its end?

What matters is not age but the condition of the load-bearing parts. A container with faded paint and surface rust is not a disposal case. A rusted-through frame is much more likely to be.

| Finding | Assessment | Obvious route |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Paint faded, surface rust, doors and seals fine | fully usable | Keep or sell; if needed [repaint](/en/ratgeber/container-streichen-beschichtungssystem) |
| Floor soft or swollen, steel frame intact | repairable | [Repair or replace the floor](/en/ratgeber/container-boden-sanieren-austauschen), then keep using |
| Roof or walls with isolated rust-through | limited use | Have repaired, or sell as a project container |
| Distorted frame, corner posts or rails rusted through | load-bearing parts damaged | No longer lift or stack; recycle, or obtain an expert opinion |
| Fitted-out container with bonded insulation, no longer wanted on site | depends on frame condition | Relocate or recycle; how easily the fittings come out decides the effort |

Whether load-bearing parts are still adequate cannot be judged reliably from a distance. If in doubt, use the approach in [Inspecting a container: checklist](/en/ratgeber/container-besichtigung-pruefprotokoll). Distortion at corner posts and corner castings is a serious warning sign, because the loads pass through these nodes when lifting and stacking.

## Five routes compared

| Route | When it makes sense | Effort | Financial result |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Keep using on site | Use changes only internally | low | no extra cost |
| Relocate to another site | Need remains, location goes | medium (crane, haulage, possibly new foundation) | saves a new purchase, costs haulage |
| Sell | Good condition, buyer within reach | medium (advert, handover, loading) | proceeds, less effort |
| Repurpose as a building element (garden room, store, shelter) | Frame sound, shell leaky or heavily worn | medium | low, but no haulage |
| Recycle as scrap | Frame rusted through, no safe use left | haulage, disposal of fittings | low proceeds, often close to zero |

Before settling on scrap, look at [Buy or rent?](/en/ratgeber/container-kaufen-oder-mieten): with a rented container the question does not arise, as the contract governs the return ([Rental agreement and law](/en/ratgeber/container-mietvertrag-recht)). With an owned container the decision is entirely yours.

## Dismantling in the right order

If you take down a fitted-out container, time and money are saved when the steps build on each other. The order is a practical recommendation, not a rule.

1. **Disconnect power and water.** A qualified electrician disconnects the electrics; with permanently installed systems this is specialist work ([Power supply](/en/ratgeber/container-stromanschluss), [Electrical planning](/en/ratgeber/container-elektroplanung-innenausbau)). Water and waste connections must be sealed properly so that nothing seeps into the ground ([Water supply and wastewater](/en/ratgeber/container-wasseranschluss-abwasser)).
2. **Clear the contents.** Anything that does not belong to the container comes out first. Hazardous substances, paints, batteries and electrical appliances have their own disposal routes.
3. **Separate the fittings.** Shelving, partitions, furniture and loose insulation can usually be reused or disposed of separately. Bolted constructions clearly beat bonded ones here.
4. **Remove external additions.** Canopy, stairs, photovoltaic mounting, railings and cabling ([Photovoltaics on the container](/en/ratgeber/photovoltaik-auf-dem-container)).
5. **Settle the foundation and supports.** Pad foundations and gravel beds are easier to remove than a concrete slab; what stays behind should be agreed beforehand ([Foundations](/en/ratgeber/container-fundament)).
6. **Plan the removal.** Access, crane standing area and weight need checking in advance ([Transporting a container](/en/ratgeber/container-transportieren), [Lifting correctly](/en/ratgeber/container-richtig-anheben)).

## Timber floor and insulation: the difficult parts

The steel shell is the easy part. Floor and fittings are harder.

**Timber floor.** The floor is plywood on steel cross-members. Whether and how the floor was treated is not always visible. Under waste law, treated timber may be classified differently from untreated timber. Before removal, ask the responsible waste contractor or the municipal waste advice service how the floor is to be classified and handed over. There is no blanket answer; it depends on the material, its condition and local acceptance terms. For removal tips see [Repairing or replacing the floor](/en/ratgeber/container-boden-sanieren-austauschen).

**Insulation.** Loosely fitted mineral wool or boards can be removed separately. Sprayed rigid foam, by contrast, adheres to the steel and can hardly be separated by material ([Spray foam in containers](/en/ratgeber/spruehschaum-daemmung-container)). This lowers the scrap proceeds and raises the effort. Anyone thinking of the end of life at fit-out stage chooses removable constructions ([Insulating a container](/en/ratgeber/container-daemmen)).

**Coatings.** Old paint can contain harmful substances where age and origin are unknown. Wear respiratory and dust protection when sanding or cutting. More on safety is in [Occupational safety around containers](/en/ratgeber/container-arbeitsschutz-baustelle).

## Worked example: what is left of the scrap?

The example is deliberately simplified. It shows the method, not a reliable market price. Scrap prices fluctuate and regional dealers bid differently, so the assumptions are stated openly.

**Assumptions:**

- used 20 ft standard container, tare weight approx. 2,200–2,300 kg (reference: [Sizes and dimensions](/en/ratgeber/container-groessen-und-masse))
- timber floor approx. 250–300 kg (rough estimate from approx. 28 mm plywood over roughly 14 m² floor area); it does not count as steel
- steel content therefore approx. 1.9–2.0 t
- assumed scrap price approx. €150–250 per tonne (pure assumption; ask locally)
- haulage by crane and lorry: assumption approx. €250–400 (order of magnitude as in [Container transport costs](/en/ratgeber/container-transport-lieferkosten), varies regionally)

| Item | Lower bound | Upper bound |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Proceeds: 1.9 t × €150 or 2.0 t × €250 | approx. €285 | approx. €500 |
| Haulage | approx. €250 | approx. €400 |
| **Result (proceeds minus haulage)** | **approx. – €115** | **approx. + €250** |

The range comes from setting the smallest income against the highest cost and vice versa: 285 – 400 = – 115 and 500 – 250 = + 250. The result therefore lies between a small loss and a small gain. Not included are costs for the timber floor, for fittings, or for removing a foundation. If the scrap dealer collects the container or offsets the haulage, the calculation shifts in your favour, but that depends on the quote.

**Comparison with selling:** the fictional asking price of a working container in [Selling a used container](/en/ratgeber/container-gebraucht-verkaufen) is approx. €1,750. A container with a sound frame generally has a higher used value than its material value. Scrapping is therefore the first choice only when the frame no longer carries load. On residual value over the service life see [Running costs and depreciation](/en/ratgeber/container-betriebskosten-wertverlust).

## Keep legal and tax matters in view

- **Planning permission and time limits.** If the container was approved for a limited period or with conditions, removal may be required at the end of that period. What applies to your case depends on location, duration and use; see the basics in [Planning permission for containers](/en/ratgeber/container-baugenehmigung) and, where removal is contested, [Building application and change of use](/en/ratgeber/container-bauantrag-nutzungsaenderung).
- **Leased or rented land.** Check whether the contract obliges you to restore the original condition. This concerns foundations, cables and ground surfacing.
- **Public land.** For a container on public land, the permit ends with the special-use permission; removal and clearance are set out in the notice ([Special use of public land](/en/ratgeber/container-oeffentlicher-grund-sondernutzung)).
- **Tax.** If a container used for business is sold or disposed of, this affects depreciation and residual book value. Discuss it with your tax adviser ([Tax and depreciation](/en/ratgeber/container-steuer-abschreibung)).
- **Ownership.** For permanently attached containers, clarify beforehand who owns them ([Ownership and component parts](/en/ratgeber/container-eigentum-scheinbestandteil-grundbuch)).

## Checklist: retiring a container

- Condition of frame, corner posts and floor assessed, photos taken
- Alternatives checked: keep, relocate, sell, repurpose, recycle
- At least two quotes obtained for sale or recycling
- Power and water connections disconnected professionally
- Contents cleared, hazardous substances disposed of separately
- Fittings and insulation removed, disposal route for the timber floor clarified
- Foundation and connections: obligation to remove under contract or permit checked
- Crane position, access and weight for removal clarified
- Disposal or sale receipt kept
- Insurer and tax adviser informed

## Important note

All figures are general guide values without guarantee, in particular the amounts in the worked example. Waste, building and tax law follow the relevant statutes, state building codes and local by-laws in Germany. The information from the responsible waste contractor, the authority and your tax adviser is what counts. Work on electrics and load-bearing parts belongs in specialist hands.

## Conclusion

A container is rarely worthless at the end. Before sending it for scrap, check whether continued use, relocation or sale yields more; the pure steel value at best just offsets the haulage. Anyone who plans early, disconnects power and water cleanly, clarifies the timber floor with the waste contractor and chooses removable fittings saves time and money when retiring a container. If the container is still in good shape, read on in [Selling a used container](/en/ratgeber/container-gebraucht-verkaufen).
