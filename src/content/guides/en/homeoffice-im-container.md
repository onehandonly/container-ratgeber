Anyone who works from home permanently knows the problem: the kitchen table becomes the office, and in the evening the laptop is still next to the dinner plates. A container in the garden seems an elegant fix – a room of its own and a door you close behind you. This guide looks at the idea from a decision-making angle: what does such a workplace cost overall, what are the running costs, and when is an alternative the better choice? General ideas for garden use are covered in [Container as a garden house](/en/ratgeber/container-als-gartenhaus); furnishing is covered in [Setting up an office container](/en/ratgeber/buerocontainer-einrichten). Note that the legal and tax points below refer to German law.

## Who a container home office suits

The container plays to its strengths when several conditions come together:

- You work from home regularly and over several years, not just occasionally.
- There is no quiet, lockable room in the house or flat.
- There is outdoor space with access for a delivery lorry and a power supply.
- You want a short build time and a structure that can be removed again, rather than an extension.

It is less suitable if you work remotely only a few days a month, if the plot is rented and the landlord has not agreed, or if the plot is so tight that boundary distances can hardly be kept.

## Size: how much space do you really need?

A 20 ft container offers roughly 5.90 × 2.35 m inside, i.e. just under 14 m² of floor area. After insulation and lining, each wall loses about 6–12 cm, and the clear height of a high-cube (about 2.70 m inside) drops to roughly 2.4–2.5 m. Exact dimensions are given in [Container sizes and dimensions](/en/ratgeber/container-groessen-und-masse).

| Use | Floor area (guide) | Fits in |
|---|---|---|
| One desk, little filing | approx. 8–10 m² | 20 ft, possibly partitioned |
| One desk plus meeting corner | approx. 12–14 m² | full 20 ft |
| Two desks | approx. 18–22 m² | 2 × 20 ft or 40 ft |
| One desk plus small WC | approx. 16–18 m² | 40 ft or coupled modules |

With about 28 m² of internal area, a 40 ft container is usually oversized for a single office. More on joining units is in [Stacking and coupling containers](/en/ratgeber/container-stapeln-und-koppeln).

## Worked example: garden office from a 20 ft high-cube

The example assumes a used 20 ft high-cube that is insulated and fitted with two windows, basic electrics and a split air-conditioning unit. All figures are guide values without guarantee, taken from the ranges in our cost guides ([What does a container cost?](/en/ratgeber/was-kostet-ein-container), [Fit-out costs by trade](/en/ratgeber/container-ausbaukosten-gewerke), [Foundations](/en/ratgeber/container-fundament)).

For the insulated area: two long and two end walls at about 2.39 m inside height give roughly 39 m², the roof about 14 m² – around 53 m² in total, rounded here to 50 m².

| Item | Basis | Cost (approx.) |
|---|---|---|
| Used 20 ft container | purchase price | €2,000 – 3,500 |
| Delivery and crane | per trip / call-out | €250 – 800 |
| Pad foundations | concrete | €600 – 2,000 |
| Two windows incl. openings | 2 × (€900 – 1,800) | €1,800 – 3,600 |
| Internal insulation, mineral wool | 50 m² × €60 – 110 | €3,000 – 5,500 |
| Plasterboard lining | 50 m² × €35 – 60 | €1,750 – 3,000 |
| Basic installation plus sub-distribution | specialist firm | €2,200 – 4,900 |
| Split unit with heating function | incl. installation | €1,500 – 3,500 |
| Floor build-up and covering | estimate | €600 – 1,500 |
| **Total** | | **approx. €13,700 – 28,300** |

Not included are the supply cable from the house (depends strongly on length), furniture, network equipment and any building permit. If you do the insulation and lining yourself the total falls; the electrical installation belongs in professional hands.

**Running costs:** For an insulated office container with a split heat pump used all year, [Running costs and depreciation](/en/ratgeber/container-betriebskosten-wertverlust) gives roughly 1,500–3,000 kWh, or €450–900 of electricity per year. Maintenance (see [Maintenance and rust protection](/en/ratgeber/container-pflege-und-rostschutz)) and insurance come on top.

**Monthly view over 10 years:** €13,700 – 28,300 spread over 120 months is about €115 – 236 per month. Electricity adds about €38 – 75 per month. Your office therefore costs roughly **€150 – 310 per month**, excluding maintenance, insurance and the supply cable. Put this range into your own comparison, for example against renting a workspace elsewhere or an extra room.

## Decision aid: container, garden shed, extension or external workspace

| Criterion | Container | Timber garden house | Extension | External workspace |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Build time | days to weeks | weeks | months | immediate |
| Investment | medium to high | low to medium | high | none, ongoing rent |
| Insulation and climate effort | high (steel) | lower | as for living space | n/a |
| Removal / moving | possible | limited | no | not needed |
| Added property value | low | low | high | none |
| Permit effort | depends on state and site | depends on state and site | usually a building application | none |

Steel conducts heat very well, so insulation costs more than for a timber-frame cabin. In return, steel walls are sturdy, tight and quick to relocate. If you plan to stay for good and want more living space, consider an extension.

## Climate, air and acoustics

**Summer heat:** An unshaded steel body heats up strongly. Roof insulation, a light roof and wall finish, shading for windows and a split air-conditioning unit all help. The principle is explained in [Thermal bridges in containers](/en/ratgeber/waermebruecken-container).

**Fresh air:** An insulated, airtight room needs controlled ventilation, otherwise CO₂ and humidity rise – and with them the risk of condensation in the wall build-up. Options are described in [Ventilation in containers](/en/ratgeber/container-belueftung).

**Winter:** Thin uninsulated steel walls cool quickly. Electric heating is usual in a small office; see [Containers in winter](/en/ratgeber/container-im-winter).

**Acoustics:** Rain on a steel roof and echo in a small room disturb video calls. Roof insulation with decoupling from structure-borne noise and absorbers on walls and ceiling help; see [Noise protection and acoustics](/en/ratgeber/container-laermschutz-akustik).

## Technology: power, network, electrics

- **Power:** The office needs at least its own protected circuit, often more with air conditioning and heating. Supply cable and protection must be installed by qualified electricians; see [Power supply for containers](/en/ratgeber/container-stromanschluss) and [Electrical planning](/en/ratgeber/container-elektroplanung-innenausbau).
- **Internet:** A steel container acts as a Faraday cage and noticeably weakens Wi-Fi and mobile signal. Plan a network cable (buried cable or conduit) from the house to the container and an access point inside rather than relying on Wi-Fi through the steel wall.
- **Surge protection:** Where cables run between house and container, surge protection makes sense. Have an electrician assess it.
- **Own power:** A photovoltaic system on the container can cover part of the daytime load; see [Photovoltaics on the container](/en/ratgeber/photovoltaik-auf-dem-container).

## Permits and neighbours

Whether a container home office needs a permit depends on the federal state, the site, size, duration of installation and use. A room where you work permanently is judged differently from a plain equipment shed. The relevant rules are the state building code (Landesbauordnung), any development plan and – in the outlying area – section 35 of the Federal Building Code (BauGB). Setback distances to neighbours also apply. See [Container building permit](/en/ratgeber/container-baugenehmigung) and [Neighbour law and boundary distance](/en/ratgeber/container-nachbarrecht-grenzabstand). Ask the local building authority before ordering and get the answer confirmed in writing.

If you live in a rented house you also need the landlord's consent.

## Tax: principles, not promises

Whether and how much of the cost of a home workplace you can claim depends on your work situation (employed, self-employed, business) and on whether the room counts as a home office room. Since 2023 simplified rules with flat rates apply to working from home; for a separate outbuilding such as a container the classification is a case-by-case matter. General points on depreciation and acquisition cost are in [Container: tax and depreciation](/en/ratgeber/container-steuer-abschreibung). Clarify the treatment with a tax adviser before buying.

## Checklist before ordering

1. Fix the location: distances, access for delivery and crane, sun exposure, cable routes.
2. Obtain planning information and clear consent from the landlord or owners' association.
3. Consider how long you will use it and the resale option ([Selling a used container](/en/ratgeber/container-gebraucht-verkaufen)).
4. Define the fit-out: insulation, windows, climate, ventilation, electrics. See [Retrofitting windows and doors](/en/ratgeber/container-fenster-tueren-nachruesten) and [Insulating a container](/en/ratgeber/container-daemmen).
5. Plan the network cable and power supply with specialist firms.
6. Check insurance for container and office equipment ([Insuring a container](/en/ratgeber/container-versichern)) and theft protection ([Theft protection](/en/ratgeber/container-sichern-diebstahlschutz)).
7. Calculate alternatives – timber house, extension, external workspace – over the same period.
8. Optional: compare ready-made office modules, see [Office containers](/en/container/buerocontainer) and the options under [Buy & Rent](/en/kaufen-mieten).
