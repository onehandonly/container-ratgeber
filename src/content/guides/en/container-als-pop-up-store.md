A pop-up store is a shop for a limited time: a few weeks or months, a clear occasion, a limited range of goods. Containers suit this because they arrive as a finished unit, can be placed at changing locations and are collected afterwards. This guide looks at the project from a costing perspective: what does the time limit cost, how much revenue per trading day is needed, and which questions should you settle before committing to rent and stock? Use at events and in catering is covered in [Containers for events and catering](/en/ratgeber/container-fuer-events-und-gastronomie); the type is also described on the [event container](/en/container/eventcontainer) page. The legal points below refer to the situation in Germany.

## When a container pop-up makes sense

Compared with an empty shop unit, a container has advantages and drawbacks. It fits best in these situations:

- **You are testing a location or a product** without signing a multi-year commercial lease.
- **There is a plot but no building** – for example a car park, a courtyard, a festival site or a garden centre.
- **The offer is seasonal**, such as a Christmas market, beach season or a farm shop at harvest time.
- **The brand needs a presence** that stands out and is mobile.

It is less suitable for high space requirements (clothing with fitting rooms, furniture), for products with strict storage conditions and no power, or where the site has no access for delivery vehicles and a crane.

## Size and layout

A 20 ft container offers roughly 5.90 × 2.35 m inside, i.e. just under 14 m². Only part of this is sales area: till, storage and a staff spot also need room. A rule of thumb helps, but does not replace planning:

| Area | Share (guide) | Note |
|---|---|---|
| Sales and customer area | approx. 50–60 % | with a serving hatch there is no customer space; the area becomes storage |
| Till / counter | approx. 10–15 % | till system, card terminal, packaging |
| Storage | approx. 20–30 % | shelves along the end wall, stack goods upwards |
| Staff / other | approx. 5–10 % | coat hooks, hand washing, supplies |

There are two basic forms:

1. **Walk-in shop.** Customers enter the container. The door opening of a 20 ft container measures approx. 2.34 × 2.28 m (high cube approx. 2.58 m high). Access is normally at container floor level above the ground; plan a ramp early if you need step-free access.
2. **Serving hatch or window.** A cut-out long wall with a hatch or counter turns the side into a service point. This saves space and works well for drinks, snacks and small items, but depends on the weather and is impractical for consultative sales. For opening the walls see [Retrofitting windows and doors](/en/ratgeber/container-fenster-tueren-nachruesten).

Several units can be placed side by side or coupled; see [Stacking and coupling containers](/en/ratgeber/container-stapeln-und-koppeln).

## Rent or buy?

For a time-limited presence, renting is the obvious choice: you budget for delivery and return, but bear no depreciation and no resale. Owning pays off if you run several pop-ups in a row at different places. As a rough guide:

| Criterion | Rent | Buy (used) |
|---|---|---|
| Period of use | a few weeks to a few months | many months to years, changing sites |
| Up-front outlay | low | higher, but with a residual value |
| Modifications (windows, branding) | often only limited | free, but at your own cost |
| Storage between uses | not needed | own storage space needed |
| Planning certainty | check the rental term | full responsibility for condition and transport |

Before deciding, compare the options with the [rent or buy calculator](/en/rechner/kaufen-oder-mieten); the basics are under [renting a container](/en/kaufen-mieten/miete) and in the guide [Renting a container: process](/en/ratgeber/container-mieten-ablauf). Typical rents are in [Rental prices and costing](/en/ratgeber/container-mietpreise-kalkulation). Whether you may modify a rented container is governed by the contract – see [Container rental agreements](/en/ratgeber/container-mietvertrag-recht).

## Worked example: three months of pop-up

The example assumes a rented 20 ft container as a shop unit standing on private land for three months. The values are guide figures without guarantee; real quotes can differ considerably.

| Item | Basis | Cost (approx.) |
|---|---|---|
| Rent of shop container | 3 × €300 – 600 | €900 – 1,800 |
| Delivery and collection | per trip, crane if needed | €500 – 1,200 |
| Site | lease or pitch fee for 3 months | €300 – 2,400 |
| Fees (e.g. special use, notifications) | depending on municipality | €100 – 600 |
| Electricity and consumption | 3 months | €150 – 450 |
| Branding / wrapping | one-off | €300 – 1,500 |
| Fit-out (shelves, counter, till system) | one-off | €600 – 2,500 |
| Insurance | contents, liability | €100 – 300 |
| **Total fixed costs** | | **approx. €2,950 – 10,750** |

Staff, stock, marketing and taxes are not included. For comparison: a used 20 ft storage container with delivery and later collection comes to roughly €2,500, but is considerably more once fitted out as a shop – ranges in [What does a container cost?](/en/ratgeber/was-kostet-ein-container) and [Fit-out costs by trade](/en/ratgeber/container-ausbaukosten-gewerke).

### Break-even revenue per trading day

The question is: how much must you sell each day to cover fixed costs? You need the gross margin, i.e. the share of revenue left after the cost of goods.

**Break-even revenue = fixed costs ÷ gross margin**

Assume a margin of 40 % and 72 trading days (about six days a week over 12 weeks):

| Scenario | Fixed costs | Total break-even revenue | per trading day |
|---|---|---|---|
| low | €2,950 | approx. €7,375 | approx. €102 |
| medium | €6,000 | approx. €15,000 | approx. €208 |
| high | €10,750 | approx. €26,875 | approx. €373 |

With a margin of only 30 %, the medium value rises to approx. €278 per day. Staff costs are not covered yet: if you run the shop yourself, include your own time honestly. Also check whether you can realistically open on 72 days – weather, public holidays and illness reduce the number.

## Location and permits

A temporary container is not automatically legally straightforward. Three questions matter:

- **Who owns the land?** On public land a special-use permit from the municipality is generally required (see [Containers on public land](/en/ratgeber/container-oeffentlicher-grund-sondernutzung)). On private land you need the owner's consent, in writing and with a term.
- **Is the location permissible under building law?** Whether a building permit is required depends on location, duration and use; the state building codes and local rules apply. More in [Containers and building permits](/en/ratgeber/container-baugenehmigung).
- **Does the use fit the zone?** Retail to end customers may be assessed differently in commercial, mixed and core zones than in residential or outlying areas ([Zoning plan and outlying areas](/en/ratgeber/container-bebauungsplan-aussenbereich)).

There are also operating duties: business registration, permits depending on the range (for example food or alcohol), price labelling, proper till records and compliance with shop opening hours. Some of these rules are state law and differ – ask the trade office and the public order office before you order.

## Technology and equipment

- **Power.** Lighting, till, card terminal and heating or air conditioning quickly add up to a demand beyond a simple socket. Planning and connection by a qualified firm; see [Power connection](/en/ratgeber/container-stromanschluss) and [Electrical planning](/en/ratgeber/container-elektroplanung-innenausbau).
- **Climate.** An uninsulated steel container gets hot in summer and cold in winter; details in [Insulating a container](/en/ratgeber/container-daemmen) and [Ventilation](/en/ratgeber/container-belueftung).
- **Fire safety.** Extinguishers, clear escape routes and safe electrics are a must. See [Fire safety](/en/ratgeber/container-brandschutz-vorschriften).
- **Security.** Goods and takings overnight are an issue – see [Theft protection](/en/ratgeber/container-sichern-diebstahlschutz).
- **Insurance.** Contents, liability and damage to the rented container should be clarified separately: [Insuring a container](/en/ratgeber/container-versichern).
- **Noise.** Music, cooling units and customers in the evening can disturb neighbours: [Noise control and acoustics](/en/ratgeber/container-laermschutz-akustik).

## Process and timetable

Plan backwards from the opening date:

1. **12–8 weeks before:** secure the site, approach the owner or municipality, contact the trade office and building authority.
2. **8–6 weeks before:** obtain quotes for the container ([Comparing quotes](/en/ratgeber/container-angebote-vergleichen)), fix the rental period and return in the contract.
3. **6–4 weeks before:** commission power, branding and fit-out; plan range and till.
4. **2 weeks before:** arrange delivery (access, crane position, ground), clarify foundation or base plates ([Foundations](/en/ratgeber/container-fundament)).
5. **Opening:** acceptance with photos, initial stocking, opening promotion.
6. **Dismantling:** cleaning, handover report, return date, disconnecting services.

## Checklist before ordering

- Site and the consent of the owner or municipality are available in writing.
- Building-law permissibility has been checked with the competent authority.
- Break-even revenue per trading day is calculated and realistic.
- The rental agreement states term, delivery, collection, deposit and permitted modifications.
- Power, heating/cooling and lighting are planned.
- Till, payment methods, price labelling and opening hours are settled.
- Insurance for contents and liability is in place.
- Dismantling and condition on return are agreed.

## Important note

This guide gives general orientation and is no substitute for legal, tax or permit advice. Building law, trade law and shop opening hours differ by federal state and municipality. Speak to the competent authorities before signing a binding contract and, if needed, to a professional.

## Conclusion

A container pop-up makes sense when you need a time-limited test with manageable fixed costs and have settled location and permissibility early. Work out the break-even revenue per trading day before ordering: if it is above what the location can realistically deliver, even an eye-catching container will not help.
