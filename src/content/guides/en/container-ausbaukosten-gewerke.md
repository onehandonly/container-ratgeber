## The basic cost structure

Anyone converting a container into an office, workshop, retail space or habitable room almost always underestimates the ratio. A used 20-foot storage container costs approx. €1,500–3,000 (see [What does a container cost?](/en/ratgeber/was-kostet-ein-container)). A professionally executed fit-out of that same container costs several times as much.

The reason is mundane: a fit-out involves the same trades as any other building – insulation, electrics, plumbing, heating, windows, doors, finishes. The small area does not make the work cheaper; proportionally it makes it dearer, because travel, set-up time and minimum quantities are spread across just under 14 m².

The table below shows how this plays out. The shares are **calculated from the two contractor examples further down**, not estimated across the board – for your own project they may differ considerably depending on specification.

| Cost block | Office without sanitary facilities (approx. €23,700) | Habitable room with WC and kitchenette (approx. €34,100) |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Container shell | approx. 11% | approx. 7.6% |
| Transport and foundation | approx. 6.3% | approx. 4.4% |
| Windows, door, openings | approx. 21% | approx. 14.7% |
| Insulation and internal lining | approx. 27% | approx. 19% |
| Electrical installation | approx. 11.8% | approx. 8.2% |
| Heating | approx. 10.1% | approx. 7% |
| Floor and decorating | approx. 12.2% | approx. 8.5% |
| Plumbing incl. pipework and tiling | – | approx. 30.5% |

Adding sanitary facilities shifts everything: plumbing becomes the largest single block, because supply and waste have to be routed through the steel wall, laid frost-proof and connected to the site network.

## Trade by trade

The figures below apply roughly to a standard-height 20-foot container (internally approx. 5.90 × 2.35 × 2.39 m, i.e. just under 14 m² of floor area) and assume work by trade contractors. Where a trade has its own guide on this site, the ranges have been aligned with it. Prices refer to the German market.

### Cutting and reinforcing openings

| Item | Rough cost |
| --- | --- |
| Window cut-out incl. welded-in frame, standard size approx. 1 × 1 m | approx. €600–1,500 per opening |
| Door cut-out incl. frame, standard door | approx. €900–2,000 |
| Reveal insulation | approx. €50–200 per opening |
| Additional structural reinforcement for several or large openings | approx. €500–2,000 |
| Supplying and fitting a window, uPVC, double glazed | approx. €500–900 each |
| Window, triple glazed, high quality | approx. €800–1,500 each |
| External door, insulated, with frame | approx. €900–2,000 |
| Roller shutter or grille | approx. €300–800 per opening |

Welding in frames is not an optional luxury. The walls and roof of a container are structurally connected to the steel frame and stiffen it; every large opening weakens the structure and has to be compensated for by a surrounding profile. For projects requiring a permit, the structural engineer will demand calculations anyway. Ask explicitly whether the window and door themselves are included in the cut-out price – this is one of the most common ambiguities in quotes. Formats and installation are covered in [Retrofitting windows and doors](/en/ratgeber/container-fenster-tueren-nachruesten).

### Insulation and lining

| Item | Rough cost |
| --- | --- |
| Internal mineral wool incl. sub-frame, vapour control layer and junctions | approx. €60–110 per m² of component area |
| Internal PUR/PIR boards incl. sub-frame | approx. €70–130 per m² |
| Closed-cell spray foam by a specialist, insulation layer only | approx. €40–90 per m² |
| External insulation system with cladding | approx. €120–220 per m² |
| Plasterboard lining, filled and taped | approx. €35–60 per m² |
| Wood-based or exposed timber lining | approx. €50–120 per m² |

When comparing: the mineral wool and PUR/PIR figures here include the sub-frame. Prices for "the insulation layer only" are considerably lower – as in the cost table of [Spray foam insulation in containers](/en/ratgeber/spruehschaum-daemmung-container) – and cannot be compared directly with these rows.

**Worked example, component area (standard height, 2.39 m internal):**

| Component | Calculation | Area |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Side walls | 2 × 5.90 m × 2.39 m | approx. 28.2 m² |
| End walls | 2 × 2.35 m × 2.39 m | approx. 11.2 m² |
| Ceiling | 5.90 m × 2.35 m | approx. 13.9 m² |
| less two windows (1 m² each) and one door (approx. 2 m²) | | approx. −4.0 m² |
| **Walls and ceiling, net** | | **approx. 49.3 m²** |
| Floor (counted under floor build-up in the examples) | 5.90 m × 2.35 m | approx. 13.9 m² |

With mineral wool including sub-frame (€60–110/m²), insulating walls and ceiling therefore costs approx. €3,000–5,400, and plasterboard lining over the same area (€35–60/m²) another approx. €1,700–3,000 – together approx. €4,700–8,400. In a high cube (2.70 m internal) the wall area grows by around 5 m². Whether to insulate internally or externally determines interior dimensions and building physics alike; the trade-off is set out in [Insulating a container](/en/ratgeber/container-daemmen) and [Thermal bridges in shipping containers](/en/ratgeber/waermebruecken-container).

### Electrical installation

| Item | Rough cost |
| --- | --- |
| Electrical fit-out, 20 ft, incl. consumer unit and materials | approx. €1,500–4,000 |
| of which consumer unit with RCD and breakers | approx. €400–900 |
| additional socket, switch, outlet | approx. €60–130 each |
| LED ceiling light, fitted | approx. €90–250 each |
| External connection with CEE socket | approx. €250–600 |
| Earthing and equipotential bonding of the steel body | approx. €200–500 |
| Initial verification with test report | approx. €150–400 |

Two particularities push the price above that of a masonry room: the steel body must be **earthed and included in equipotential bonding**, and every cable penetration through sheet steel needs proper edge protection. Improvised solutions are a typical cause of fire, see [Fire safety for containers](/en/ratgeber/container-brandschutz-vorschriften). Circuit planning is covered in [Electrical planning for the fit-out](/en/ratgeber/container-elektroplanung-innenausbau); the supply cable to the container is a separate item, described in [Power supply for containers](/en/ratgeber/container-stromanschluss).

### Plumbing

| Item | Rough cost |
| --- | --- |
| WC with hand basin, complete | approx. €2,000–4,500 |
| Shower in addition | approx. €1,500–3,500 |
| Small kitchenette with connections | approx. €1,500–4,000 |
| Hot water, instantaneous heater or small cylinder incl. installation | approx. €400–1,200 |
| Supply and waste pipework, laid frost-proof | approx. €80–200 per linear m |
| Waste connection to an existing sewer on the plot | approx. €500–2,500 |
| Sealed cesspit, if no sewer is within reach | approx. €2,000–8,000 plus emptying |
| Small sewage treatment plant, if no sewer is within reach | approx. €5,000–15,000 |

The biggest uncertainty in the whole project is not the sanitaryware but the question of **where the waste water goes**. With 12 m of pipework to an existing sewer you get approx. €960–2,400 for the pipes plus approx. €500–2,500 for the connection, i.e. approx. €1,460–4,900. If no sewer is within reach, a cesspit or small treatment plant comes into play – with water-law clearance and running costs. What a cesspit costs in operation is calculated in [Water supply and waste water](/en/ratgeber/container-wasseranschluss-abwasser). Clarify this before buying the container, not afterwards.

### Heating and ventilation

| Item | Rough cost |
| --- | --- |
| Electric infrared or convector heating | approx. €300–900 |
| Split air conditioner with heat pump function | approx. €1,500–3,500 incl. installation |
| Small air-to-water heat pump | approx. €6,000–14,000 |
| Wood or pellet stove incl. flue | approx. €3,000–7,000 |
| Ventilation grille, passive | approx. €20–80 each |
| Extract fan with humidity control | approx. €150–500 |
| Decentralised ventilation with heat recovery | approx. €800–2,000 per unit |

A split unit with heat pump function is an obvious default for fitted-out containers: it heats, cools and dehumidifies and can be sized well for the small heat load. Whether, and which, requirements of the German Buildings Energy Act (GEG) apply to your container – for example on the share of renewable energy for newly installed heating – depends on use and on whether it counts as a building; see [GEG and containers](/en/ratgeber/container-geg-energierecht). Direct electric heating is cheap to buy but expensive to run. For ventilation planning see [Ventilating a container](/en/ratgeber/container-belueftung).

### Floor and finishes

| Item | Rough cost |
| --- | --- |
| Replacing the original floor by a contractor (materials and labour) | approx. €1,000–2,800 |
| New floor build-up with insulation and board, over the original floor | approx. €60–120 per m² |
| Vinyl or LVT flooring, laid | approx. €35–70 per m² |
| Tiling in wet areas | approx. €70–150 per m² |
| Internal decorating | approx. €15–35 per m² |
| External painting of the container | approx. €25–60 per m² |

The original floor of a shipping container usually consists of plywood panels around 28 mm thick on steel cross members and is often treated with wood preservatives. For habitable rooms it is therefore either replaced or permanently and tightly built over. The examples below assume it is built over (floor build-up approx. €2,000 for just under 14 m²: 13.9 m² × €95–190/m² for build-up plus finish gives approx. €1,320–2,640). When replacement is worthwhile is explained in [Repairing and replacing a container floor](/en/ratgeber/container-boden-sanieren-austauschen).

## Worked examples

All amounts are assumptions within the ranges above, not quotes. Transport and foundation are aligned with the reference values in [Transport and delivery costs](/en/ratgeber/container-transport-lieferkosten) and [Container foundations](/en/ratgeber/container-fundament).

**Example 1: Simple office, 20 ft, no sanitary facilities, trade contractors**

| Item | Amount |
| --- | --- |
| Used container, wind and watertight | approx. €2,600 |
| Local delivery by lorry with loading crane, set down on the foundation | approx. €600 |
| Concrete pad foundations | approx. €900 |
| Two windows and one door: cut-outs with frames, units, reveals | approx. €5,000 |
| Insulation of walls and ceiling incl. plasterboard lining | approx. €6,500 |
| Electrical installation incl. earthing and test report | approx. €2,800 |
| Heating, split unit | approx. €2,400 |
| Floor build-up with insulation and finish | approx. €2,000 |
| Decorating | approx. €900 |
| **Total** | **approx. €23,700** |

That is around €1,700 per square metre of internal floor area (€23,700 ÷ 13.9 m²). Not included: the power supply cable to the container, permit and design fees, furniture.

**Example 2: Habitable room with WC and kitchenette, 20 ft, trade contractors**

| Item | Amount |
| --- | --- |
| Basic fit-out as in example 1 | approx. €23,700 |
| WC with hand basin | approx. €3,200 |
| Kitchenette with connections | approx. €2,600 |
| Hot water | approx. €700 |
| Pipework and connection to existing sewer, 12 m | approx. €2,400 |
| Tiling and waterproofing the wet area | approx. €1,500 |
| **Total** | **approx. €34,100** |

Without a reachable sewer, a cesspit or small treatment plant partly replaces the "pipework and connection" item – the total can then rise by several thousand euros depending on the solution.

**Example 3: Self-build of the office from example 1**

| Item | Amount |
| --- | --- |
| Container, transport, foundation | approx. €4,100 |
| Cut-outs with frames contracted out, windows and door as materials | approx. €4,000 |
| Insulation, sub-frame, boards | approx. €2,300 |
| Electrical materials, connection and testing by a contractor | approx. €1,400 |
| Heating, split unit incl. installation by a contractor | approx. €2,400 |
| Floor and paint, materials | approx. €1,100 |
| **Total materials and contracted work** | **approx. €15,300** |

The saving compared with example 1 is approx. €8,400, i.e. a good third (around 35 per cent) – bought with roughly 150 to 250 hours of your own labour. Arithmetically that is approx. €34–56 saved per hour worked. The cut-outs remain a large item even in a self-build, because welding on the load-bearing structure should be left to professionals. Also usually not suitable for DIY: connecting and testing the electrical installation, refrigerant work on the split unit, gas work and anything that requires proof for building control.

## What grows with floor area – and what does not

Fitting out a 40-foot container instead of a 20-footer does not automatically double the cost. The trades behave differently:

| Behaviour | Trades | Effect of doubling the area |
| --- | --- | --- |
| grows roughly with area | insulation, lining, floor build-up, finish, decorating | roughly twice as expensive |
| grows with quantity | windows, doors, cut-outs, sockets, lights | depends on the design, not on the length |
| barely grows | meter position/consumer unit, earthing, test report, sewer connection, heater for a small heat load | roughly the same |
| grows less than proportionally | transport, foundation | dearer, but not double |

This is why the cost per square metre usually falls for larger units while the total rises. Conversely, a 10-foot container is particularly expensive per square metre, because the fixed items fall on even less area.

## A sensible sequence of trades

A large part of the extra cost arises not from high prices but from work done in the wrong order. This sequence has proved itself:

1. **Check the condition and treat rust** before anything is lined – see [Inspection and inspection report](/en/ratgeber/container-besichtigung-pruefprotokoll).
2. **All cut-outs and welding** first. Welding after insulation is a fire hazard and damages coating and insulation.
3. **Define and make penetrations for power, water, waste and ventilation** while the wall is still open.
4. **First fix** for electrics and plumbing.
5. **Insulation, vapour control layer and sub-frame**, with reveals connected.
6. **Lining, floor, tiling.**
7. **Second fix** of switches, lights, sanitaryware and heater, followed by testing of the electrical installation with a report.

When obtaining quotes, ask for them to be broken down by exactly these trades. This makes gaps visible, such as whether reveal insulation sits with the window fitter or the drylining contractor. How to make quotes comparable is described in [Comparing quotes](/en/ratgeber/container-angebote-vergleichen).

## Where costs get out of hand

| Cause | Extra cost (approx.) | Prevention |
| --- | --- | --- |
| No sewer within reach, cesspit or treatment plant needed | approx. €2,000–15,000 for the installation | clarify with the municipality and water authority before buying |
| Turns out to need a permit after all | approx. €2,000–8,000 for design and evidence | clarify in advance, see [Container planning permission](/en/ratgeber/container-baugenehmigung) |
| More rust on the shell than visible at purchase | approx. €1,000–4,000 | inspection, condition grade, photos |
| Several large openings require additional reinforcement | approx. €500–2,000 | agree openings with a structural engineer early |
| Mobile crane needed in addition because the loading crane cannot reach | approx. €600–1,800 per day | check the pitch and access in advance, [transport cost calculator](/en/rechner/transportkosten) |
| Layout changed after work has started | approx. €1,000–5,000 | finalise the design before work begins |
| Original floor has to come out after all | approx. €1,000–2,800 | check the floor during inspection |

One further effect costs area rather than money: with 10 cm of internal insulation on all walls, the floor area shrinks from approx. 13.9 m² to approx. 12.3 m², i.e. by around 12 per cent; with 8 cm it is around 9 per cent. Ceiling and floor build-ups reduce the room height as well.

## Important note

> All prices are **rough guide values without guarantee** and refer to typical conditions in Germany. Tradespeople's rates vary considerably by region, material prices change constantly, and the actual effort depends heavily on the condition of the container and local circumstances. Only individual quotes are binding. Whether a fit-out requires a permit and which energy requirements apply depends on location, duration and use. Electrical, gas and refrigerant work, welding on the structure and evidence for building control belong in the hands of specialist contractors and qualified designers.

## Conclusion

In a container fit-out, the container is the smallest item: in the examples its share is around 8 to 11 per cent. For a professionally executed 20-foot office without sanitary facilities the example comes to approx. €23,700, with WC and kitchenette to approx. €34,100 – depending on specification and region the actual amounts may be considerably higher or lower. The biggest uncertainties are the waste water question and the permit; both should be settled before buying. Self-builders save a good third in the example, but still have to contract out the cut-outs, electrical connection, refrigerant work and evidence.
