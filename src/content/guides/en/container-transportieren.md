Whether it is a roll-off container for rubble, an office container for a building site or a shipping container used for storage, a container only becomes useful once it stands safely in its place. Delivery often decides whether an appointment runs smoothly or whether the vehicle has to leave again empty-handed. This guide explains the available delivery methods, the requirements the access route and standing area must meet, and roughly what costs to expect.

## Overview of delivery methods

Depending on the container type, size and ground conditions, different vehicles are used. The delivery method determines how much space the vehicle needs and how precisely the container can be set down.

**Hook lift / roll-off truck:** The classic system for roll-off containers (skips). The container is pulled onto the tilted loading bed via a chain or hook and rolled off backwards when unloaded. Ideal for open skips and enclosed roll-off containers. The container rolls back a little as it is set down, so manoeuvring space in the direction of travel is required.

**Skip loader:** Uses two side arms that lift the container and set it down relatively vertically. This allows the container to be placed more precisely and in a tighter space than with a roll-off truck. Suitable for smaller skips, for example on cramped inner-city plots.

**Truck with loader crane:** An onboard loader crane lifts the container over obstacles such as a fence, a hedge or straight into a courtyard. This makes it possible to place heavier construction and office containers as well as shipping containers, provided the crane's weight capacity and reach are sufficient. Crane operation requires a firm, load-bearing surface for the outriggers.

**Low loader / semi-trailer:** For transporting large, heavy units such as 20- or 40-foot shipping containers over longer distances. Unloading is then usually done with a separate crane or forklift on site. Low loaders need plenty of space and good access.

| Delivery method | Suitable for | Note |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Hook lift / roll-off truck | Roll-off containers, open skips | Container rolls back on unloading, manoeuvring space needed |
| Skip loader | Small skips | Precise, vertical drop-off in tight spaces |
| Truck with loader crane | Construction, office, shipping containers | Lifts over obstacles, firm outrigger surface required |
| Low loader / semi-trailer | Large/heavy shipping containers | For long transports, unloading often by crane/forklift |

## Requirements for access and standing area

Most failed delivery appointments are not caused by the container but by the access route. Check these points in advance:

- **Width:** Delivery vehicles are often around 3 m wide including mirrors. Allow at least roughly 3 to 3.5 m of clear width for passage; narrow gateways and parked cars quickly become obstacles.
- **Height:** Watch clearance heights at gates, archways, balconies and branches. A crane truck also needs headroom to extend the jib. Overhead power lines are a safety hazard.
- **Load capacity:** A loaded vehicle can easily weigh 20 to 40 t. Lawns, freshly paved paths, underground garage ceilings or manhole covers do not always take that. When in doubt, check the ground.
- **Manoeuvring space:** A roll-off truck needs extra space in the direction of travel because the container runs out as it is set down. For crane operation the vehicle needs lateral room for the outriggers.
- **Overhang and obstacles:** Canopies, awnings, lamp posts, road signs and parked vehicles restrict the crane's swing radius. Clear airspace above the standing area is decisive for crane use.

> Rule of thumb: if a concrete mixer or refuse truck can drive up without trouble, the container truck usually manages too. If unsure, providers will send dimensions in advance or inspect the access on request.

## The delivery process step by step

1. **Appointment and time window:** Usually a delivery day with a rough time window is agreed. Stay reachable so the driver can call with questions about the access.
2. **Prepare the standing area:** Move vehicles, bins and loose items out of the way. Mark the desired position if needed.
3. **Guidance:** Guide the driver on arrival, especially when reversing and in tight spots.
4. **Drop-off or crane operation:** The driver positions the vehicle, sets the outriggers for crane work and places the container at the agreed spot.
5. **Check and handover:** Check position and alignment before the vehicle leaves. Repositioning later often involves extra costs.
6. **Collection:** For rental containers and skips, collection is arranged separately. The container should be freely accessible and not overloaded.

## Delivery costs

Delivery is already included in many offers but may be charged separately depending on the effort involved. The following figures are rough guide ranges and can vary significantly by region and provider.

| Cost item | Approx. range | Note |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Delivery in the local area | approx. EUR 40 to 120 | Often included in rental or disposal price |
| Additional distance | approx. EUR 1 to 3 per km | Beyond a certain radius |
| Crane setup / crane use | approx. EUR 80 to 250 | Depending on duration and reach |
| Waiting time / standing fee | approx. EUR 30 to 90 per started hour | If the standing area is not clear |
| Wasted trip / failed delivery | approx. EUR 50 to 150 | If delivery was not possible |

The main cost drivers are distance, crane requirement and lost time on site. Preparing the access well avoids the most expensive items: waiting time and failed deliveries. For an overview of total costs, see the article [What does a container cost?](/en/ratgeber/was-kostet-ein-container).

> Note: all price figures are non-binding approximate values for rough orientation and do not replace an individual quote. The conditions of the respective provider always apply.

## Permission for placement on public ground

If the container stands on private ground, a permit is usually not required. If it is to be placed in public traffic space, however, such as on the road, the pavement or a public parking strip, a special-use permit from the municipality is generally required. Where traffic is affected, a traffic-law order with safeguarding and signage is often added.

Arrange this in good time, as processing can take a few days. Some providers handle the application for a surcharge. The cost of special use often lies in the low two- to three-digit range, depending on the municipality and duration.

## Checklist before the delivery date

- [ ] Access width and clearance height checked
- [ ] Load capacity of the ground clarified
- [ ] Standing area cleared (vehicles, bins, loose items)
- [ ] Clear airspace ensured for crane use
- [ ] Manoeuvring and outrigger space available
- [ ] For public ground: permit applied for
- [ ] On-site contact reachable
- [ ] Desired position marked or discussed

## Conclusion

The right delivery method depends above all on container type, standing area and access. Roll-off and skip loaders are standard for skips, a loader crane helps over obstacles and the low loader carries the large units. The biggest lever for a smooth and affordable appointment is preparation: those who check width, height, load capacity and manoeuvring space in advance, and obtain a permit in good time for public ground, avoid waiting times, failed deliveries and extra costs. When in doubt, a quick word with the provider before the delivery day is worthwhile.
