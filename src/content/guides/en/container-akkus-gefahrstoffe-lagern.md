Hardly anyone fills a storage container with boxes and shelves alone. A garden container holds the mower with leftover petrol, paint tins from the last repaint and the battery of the hedge trimmer. In a workshop there are aerosol cans, cleaning agents and a charger. A bike container may have several e-bike batteries on charge. "Is that allowed in there?" cannot be answered with a blanket yes or no. It depends on the substance, the quantity, the use (private or commercial) and how the container is equipped.

This guide complements the general advice in [Using a storage container properly](/en/ratgeber/lagercontainer-richtig-nutzen) and [Loading a container correctly](/en/ratgeber/container-richtig-beladen). It deliberately avoids stating specific limit values, which differ by substance and legal situation, and instead helps you classify your own contents and ask the right questions. The legal references are to German law.

## Why a container is not a neutral storage room

Steel does not burn. Still, three properties of a container matter for flammable or reactive contents:

- **Closed room, little air exchange.** Standard containers are largely airtight. Vapours from solvents, paints or fuels can collect inside unless there is ventilation (see [Ventilation in containers](/en/ratgeber/container-belueftung)).
- **Temperature swings.** An uninsulated steel box can heat up considerably more than the outside air in the sun and cool down strongly in winter. Many manufacturers specify permissible storage temperatures for batteries, aerosols and paints. Those limits apply inside a container too.
- **Little room to escape and limited access for firefighting.** The container usually has one door end. In an emergency, access to the seat of a fire and the stored goods is restricted.

In addition, the contents change the legal and insurance classification. A container with garden tools is something different from a store holding hazardous substances in significant quantities. Before changing the use, check whether building and fire-safety requirements are added (see [Fire safety in containers](/en/ratgeber/container-brandschutz-vorschriften) and [Building permit for containers](/en/ratgeber/container-baugenehmigung)).

## Decision table: which group your contents belong to

The table is a guide, not an approval. It sorts common contents into three groups: unproblematic, only with a plan, and only with a specialist solution.

| Contents | Classification | What matters |
|---|---|---|
| Tools without batteries, garden furniture, decorations, boxes | unproblematic | keep fire load low, avoid damp, keep aisles clear |
| Wood, paper, textiles, mattresses in larger quantities | unproblematic, but high fire load | do not overfill, keep away from ignition sources, smoke detector |
| Tool and e-bike batteries | only with a plan | manufacturer temperature limits, charging location, distance from flammables, detector |
| Paints, varnishes, solvents, aerosols | only with a plan; specialist solution for larger quantities | containers sealed, spill tray, ventilation, no ignition sources |
| Engine-driven equipment with residual fuel, jerrycans | only with a plan | tank as empty as possible, approved cans, spill tray, no ignition source |
| Fertiliser, plant protection products | only with a plan; plant protection often specialist solution only | information in the safety data sheet, separate from food |
| LPG and other compressed gas cylinders | specialist solution only | separate rules on siting and ventilation, involve a competent person |
| Larger quantities of flammable, toxic or corrosive substances | specialist solution only | hazardous-substance store as per regulations, risk assessment |
| Fireworks, ammunition, explosives | not in an ordinary container | separate licensing and storage rules |

"Only with a plan" means: it is solvable, but not without thought. "Only with a specialist solution" means an ordinary storage container is not enough; you need a room designed for the purpose, such as a [hazardous materials container](/en/container/gefahrstoffcontainer) with spill containment, ventilation and suitable electrics, or approved safety cabinets.

## Batteries: the most underestimated topic

Lithium-ion batteries are found in e-bikes, cordless drills, robotic mowers, power banks and home storage systems. The risk lies in "thermal runaway": if a cell is damaged, overcharged or too hot, it can heat itself further and drag neighbouring cells along. This is rare, but hard to extinguish when it happens.

### Storing

- Observe the storage and charging temperatures in the manual. A sunlit, uninsulated container can leave these ranges in summer. Some manufacturers explicitly advise keeping batteries inside a building.
- Do not store batteries loose among flammable items. Give them a fixed place on a non-combustible surface, away from paper, textiles, paints and fuels.
- Protect the contacts against short circuits, for example with the original cap or cover.
- Do not keep using damaged, swollen or wet batteries. Ask the manufacturer or the disposal firm about the correct route.

### Charging

- Charge only with the matching original charger and not unattended.
- Do not charge next to flammable stored goods or in a cluttered container.
- Have sockets and supply cable checked or installed by a qualified electrician (see [Power supply for containers](/en/ratgeber/container-stromanschluss) and [Electrical planning for the interior](/en/ratgeber/container-elektroplanung-innenausbau)).
- A smoke or heat detector buys time. Which extinguishing agent suits battery fires is best discussed with a fire-safety specialist or the fire brigade, not decided from the label on any random extinguisher.

### Example: how much energy is in a bike container?

Take a container with ten e-bikes, each battery with 625 Wh nominal energy. That is 10 × 625 Wh = 6,250 Wh, or 6.25 kWh – the order of magnitude of a small home storage system. How that energy would act in a fire cannot be stated in general. The comparison does show why the topic deserves attention as soon as several batteries are together. More on layout in [Container as a bike garage](/en/ratgeber/container-als-fahrradgarage). Home storage and solar systems on the container carry their own requirements (see [Photovoltaics on a container](/en/ratgeber/photovoltaik-auf-dem-container)).

## Liquids and gases: contain, ventilate, avoid ignition sources

For paints, solvents, fuels, cleaners and oils, two risks sit side by side: fire or explosion from vapours, and contamination of soil and water by spilled liquid.

**Containers and labelling.** Keep substances in original containers or suitable, labelled vessels, never in drink bottles. The manufacturer's safety data sheet lists hazards, storage advice and incompatible substances. In commercial use the German Hazardous Substances Ordinance (GefStoffV) requires a risk assessment; storage is covered by the Technical Rules for Hazardous Substances (in particular TRGS 510). Easements apply to small private quantities, which you should clarify locally.

**Containment.** Liquids belong in spill trays. Substances hazardous to water also fall under the German ordinance on facilities handling water-polluting substances (AwSV). A container has no floor drains, which is rather an advantage, but leaks at doors or floor remain possible. Do not place anything directly on the wooden floor; wood absorbs liquid and is hard to clean (see [Repairing or replacing the floor](/en/ratgeber/container-boden-sanieren-austauschen)).

**Ventilation.** Vapours are usually heavier than air and collect at the bottom. Supply and exhaust openings as described in [Ventilation in containers](/en/ratgeber/container-belueftung) make sense for permanent storage of vapour-emitting substances. Larger quantities need planned, possibly technically controlled ventilation.

**Ignition sources.** Fan heaters, sockets, lights and switches in the storage area are possible ignition sources. In rooms with an explosive atmosphere, requirements apply to the electrical installation. That is a job for specialists, not DIY. Welding and grinding do not belong in a container where solvents are stored (see [Workshop and garage in a container](/en/ratgeber/container-werkstatt-garage)).

**Gas cylinders.** LPG cylinders, acetylene, oxygen and other compressed gases are subject to their own siting rules. They do not belong in an ordinary closed storage or occupied container unless this has been clarified by a competent person first.

### Worked example: containment volume for storage in containers

A rule of thumb often cited for sizing spill trays: at least 10 % of the total quantity stored, but at least the volume of the largest single container. Whether and how this applies to your case depends on substance, quantity and legal basis; the competent authority or an expert will clarify that. The example only shows the logic:

| Storage | Total quantity | 10 % of it | largest container | containment volume by rule of thumb |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 8 cans of 20 l | 160 l | 16 l | 20 l | 20 l |
| 50 cans of 20 l | 1,000 l | 100 l | 20 l | 100 l |
| 5 drums of 200 l | 1,000 l | 100 l | 200 l | 200 l |

Same total quantity, different result: with many small containers the percentage rule decides, with few large ones the largest container does. The table also shows that a tray for 20 l is enough for 8 cans but no longer for 50 cans.

## Plant protection, fertiliser and agrochemicals

If you use a container for fertiliser and plant protection products in a garden or on a farm, separate substances according to the safety data sheet. Some fertilisers promote combustion; plant protection products often have their own storage requirements and must not stand next to food or feed. For farms, further points are collected in [Containers in agriculture](/en/ratgeber/container-landwirtschaft).

## Who decides: authority, insurer, landlord

- **Building authority and fire-safety office:** Whether the container becomes subject to permitting with these contents is a matter of state law. There is no blanket answer; it depends on location, duration and use (see [Building permit for containers](/en/ratgeber/container-baugenehmigung)).
- **Insurer:** Tell them what you store. The policy terms determine whether flammables, batteries or hazardous substances are excluded or restricted (see [Insuring a container](/en/ratgeber/container-versichern)).
- **Landlord or rental agreement:** With a rented container, the contract may prohibit certain contents. See [Container rental agreement: law and pitfalls](/en/ratgeber/container-mietvertrag-recht).
- **Occupational safety:** In a business, the employer is responsible for the risk assessment (see [Occupational safety on site](/en/ratgeber/container-arbeitsschutz-baustelle)).

## Checklist before storing

1. List of contents prepared, with quantities and safety data sheets for chemicals?
2. Each substance and battery assigned to a group in the table?
3. Manufacturer temperature limits compared with the container (summer and winter)?
4. Flammables kept apart from batteries, charging spot and electrics?
5. Spill tray present and checked for volume?
6. Ventilation present and unobstructed?
7. Smoke or heat detector installed, extinguishing agent agreed?
8. Access clear, path to the door not blocked?
9. Insurer, landlord and, where relevant, authority informed?
10. Waste and defective batteries disposed of by suitable routes (see [Dismantling and recycling](/en/ratgeber/container-rueckbau-verwertung-entsorgung))?
11. Lockable and secured against unauthorised access (see [Theft protection](/en/ratgeber/container-sichern-diebstahlschutz))?

## Important note

This article is general guidance and does not replace advice from an occupational safety specialist, fire-safety expert, authority or insurer. Legal bases, quantity thresholds and technical requirements depend on the substance, the quantity and the federal state, and they change. The regulations named serve as search terms, not as a complete account. The rule of thumb on containment volume is a worked example, not a binding dimensioning.

## Conclusion

A standard storage container suits unproblematic contents well. As soon as batteries, paints, fuels or gases are added, you need a plan: suitable temperature, separate storage, spill tray, ventilation, detectors and clarified responsibilities. Larger quantities of hazardous substances belong in a solution designed for them. Involving insurer and authority early avoids nasty surprises when something goes wrong.
