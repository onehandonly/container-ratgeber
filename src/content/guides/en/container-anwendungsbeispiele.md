A container is, at first glance, nothing more than a standardised, stackable steel box. That simplicity is what makes it versatile: it arrives by truck almost anywhere, needs no elaborate foundation, is lockable and weatherproof, and can disappear again within hours. Once you see how different the variants are – bare shipping container, insulated office module, refrigerated unit – applications open up far beyond the building site.

This collection covers 30 applications across eight sectors, each with the container type that works in practice. For all variants, see [Container types](/en/container).

## Construction & trades

The building site is the classic location, and still the largest market for rented containers.

**Site office.** Site management, plan reviews, measurements: an insulated module with windows, wiring and heating replaces the makeshift caravan, in 3, 6 or 8 metre lengths. **Suitable type:** insulated office container.

**Break and changing room.** Wet clothing and lockers call for heated, ventilated space with seating, washbasin and a separate changing zone. **Suitable type:** crew container.

**Tool and material store.** Machines, cable drums and small parts must stay dry and theft-proof; a steel container with double doors and lock box is enough. **Suitable type:** storage container, 10–20 ft.

**Rubble skip.** Demolition material, spoil or green waste goes into open skips the contractor swaps out. **Suitable type:** skip (7–10 m³) or roll-off container (20–40 m³).

## Industry & commerce

Where hall space is scarce and expensive, containers create extra floor area quickly, without a long planning phase.

**Spare parts store.** Wear parts, lubricants and tools sit where they are needed; a container beside the hall saves walking distance. **Suitable type:** 20 ft shipping container, ventilated.

**Outsourced workshop.** Welding, electrical and maintenance work continues in a module with three-phase power, workbench and extraction. **Suitable type:** workshop container.

**Test and measuring room.** Quality assurance needs stable temperature, low-dust air and quiet – better served by an air-conditioned module than a partitioned hall corner. **Suitable type:** office container with climate control.

**Gatehouse.** Access control, barrier operation, visitor reception: 2 to 3 metre modules with all-round glazing. **Suitable type:** compact gatehouse module.

> **Note on hazardous materials:** paints, solvents, oils and gas cylinders do not belong in an ordinary storage container. Certified hazardous-goods units exist for that, with spill sump, safety ventilation and tested fire resistance – a standard shipping container does not meet those rules.

## Retail & logistics

**Interim storage during refit.** When the shop floor is rebuilt, a container in the yard is cheaper than rented external space. **Suitable type:** 20 or 40 ft storage container.

**Seasonal storage.** Garden furniture in winter, Christmas stock in summer, tyres all year: for cyclical inventory a purchased container with shelving pays off. **Suitable type:** shipping container, insulated if needed.

**Sales stand and pop-up store.** With a side hatch or folding door a 20 ft container becomes a compact shop – popular at Christmas markets, ski resorts and brand activations. **Suitable type:** retail container.

**Collection point.** A lockable module with parcel lockers enables around-the-clock pickup. **Suitable type:** module container with electrical fit-out.

| Retail & logistics | Typical size | Critical point |
|---|---|---|
| Interim storage | 20 ft | Dry standing area, ventilation |
| Seasonal storage | 20–40 ft | Condensation, shelf fixings |

## Events & catering

Few sectors use containers as creatively as the events industry – they arrive fast and have become a deliberate design element.

**Bar container.** Unlike a trailer, a container stands firm, offers more working surface and opens into a counter. **Suitable type:** catering container with hatch, water and grease trap connection.

**Backstage.** Dressing rooms, make-up area, production office: stacked modules create a full backstage area within a day. **Suitable type:** office container, stackable.

**Ticket and information point.** A small module with a service window – lockable, heatable and gone after the event. **Suitable type:** ticket booth container.

**Event sanitary facilities.** Toilet and shower containers are more comfortable than single cabins and more hygienic at multi-day events. **Suitable type:** sanitary container with tank or mains connection.

**Refrigerated container for drinks.** A reefer keeps thousands of litres chilled, more economically than many single fridges, but needs permanent power. **Suitable type:** refrigerated container, 20 or 40 ft.

## Public sector & education

**School and nursery containers.** When a wing is renovated, classes move into modular buildings. With insulation, ventilation and acoustic protection these match permanent rooms. **Suitable type:** school-specification room module.

**Temporary administrative space.** Citizen services, archives or planning departments work from modular buildings in the interim. **Suitable type:** office container complex.

**Temporary health stations.** Vaccination and testing centres showed how quickly waiting areas and cubicles assemble from modules. **Suitable type:** office container with partitions and washbasins.

**Accommodation.** Living modules with kitchen, bathroom and bedrooms serve as transitional housing; insulation, fire safety and shared areas matter most. **Suitable type:** residential module with wet room.

## Agriculture & garden

**Machinery and feed store.** Attachments, seed and tools stay dry and locked away, even on remote plots. **Suitable type:** 20 or 40 ft shipping container.

**Farm shop.** A fitted container with window, chilled counter and payment system sells at the field edge, without staff. **Suitable type:** insulated retail container.

**Cooling for the harvest.** Berries, asparagus and cut flowers need chilling after picking; a reefer replaces a built cold room. **Suitable type:** refrigerated container.

**Garden house and workshop.** In a garden an insulated container replaces the timber shed: lower maintenance, harder to break into. **Suitable type:** 10 to 20 ft container, insulated.

## Sport & leisure

**Clubhouse.** Without a permanent building, coupled modules provide common room, bar and office. **Suitable type:** insulated office container complex.

**Changing rooms and showers.** Two modules give separate cabins for home and away teams. **Suitable type:** sanitary and changing container.

**Equipment store.** Goals, nets, hurdles, groundskeeping gear: a container at the pitch keeps kit dry. **Suitable type:** 10–20 ft storage container.

**Climbing and fitness box.** Containers become bouldering cubes, calisthenics boxes and mobile gyms; the steel frame carries the loads. **Suitable type:** shipping container reinforced by a specialist.

## Living & working

**Tiny house.** One or two coupled 20 ft containers give 15 to 30 square metres with bathroom, kitchenette and sleeping alcove. The appeal is cost, build time and mobility; the hurdles are insulation, permits and a site. **Suitable type:** high cube (extra height for the floor and ceiling build-up).

**Garden office.** A 3 to 6 metre module creates quiet working space separated from the home; power and network run from the house. **Suitable type:** insulated office module.

**Student and temporary housing.** Apartment modules of 18 to 25 square metres are series-produced and relocatable. **Suitable type:** residential module with wet room.

**Holiday accommodation.** By a lake, in woodland or on a campsite: cabins with large glazing are weatherproof and usable year-round. **Suitable type:** high cube with panoramic window.

## What applies to every use

**Permits.** In planning law a container quickly counts as a structure, especially when people occupy it or it stays longer. Storage on commercial land is often uncritical; living, retail and catering never are. Public land needs a special use permit.

**Insulation and condensation.** Bare steel sweats: warm air on cold walls forms condensate, the most common cause of mould and spoiled stock. Heated containers need continuous insulation and a vapour barrier; in storage use, vents and a dehumidifier help.

**Services.** Power, water, drainage and network must be planned before the crane arrives. Refrigerated and workshop units often need three-phase power; for sanitary containers, mains versus tank decides running costs.

**Standing area.** The ground must be level, load-bearing and drained – pad foundations, slabs or compacted gravel under the corner castings usually suffice. Access matters as much: a truck with a loading crane needs a clear approach and height free of branches or cables.

## Conclusion

Containers are a construction kit for temporary and permanent space. What matters is not the supplier but an honest answer to three questions: how long is the container needed, will people occupy it, and which services are available on site? Type, fit-out and the rent-or-buy decision follow from that.

For short projects with a defined end, renting carries the least risk. For recurring or permanent uses, buying pays off quickly: a well-maintained steel container lasts two decades and more.
