A container is, at first glance, nothing more than a standardised, stackable steel box. That very simplicity is what makes it so versatile: it can be delivered by truck to almost any location, needs no elaborate foundation, is lockable, weatherproof, and can disappear again within hours. Once you understand how different the variants are – from a bare shipping container to an insulated office module or a refrigerated unit – applications open up far beyond the building site.

The following collection covers 30 typical applications across eight sectors, each with a note on the container type that works in practice. For an overview of all variants, see [Container types](/en/container).

## Construction & trades

The building site is the classic location – and still the largest market for rented containers.

**Site office.** Site management, plan reviews, measurements: an insulated module with windows, wiring and heating replaces the makeshift caravan. Common lengths are 3, 6 or 8 metres; modules can be coupled and stacked. **Suitable type:** office container (insulated room module).

**Break and changing room.** Breaks, wet clothing and lockers call for heated, ventilated space – crew containers with a seating area, washbasin and a separate changing zone have proven themselves. **Suitable type:** crew or sanitary container.

**Tool and material store.** Machines, cable drums and small parts must stay dry and theft-proof. A steel container with double doors, shelving and a lock box is enough. **Suitable type:** storage or material container, 10–20 ft.

**Rubble skip.** Demolition material, spoil or green waste goes into open skips that the waste contractor swaps out – rented as part of the disposal contract rather than bought. **Suitable type:** skip (7–10 m³) or roll-off container (20–40 m³).

## Industry & commerce

Where hall space is scarce and expensive, containers create extra floor area quickly – without a building application for a hall and without a long planning phase.

**Spare parts store at the line.** Wear parts, lubricants and tools sit where they are needed; a container beside the production hall saves walking distance. **Suitable type:** 20 ft shipping container, ventilated or insulated for moisture-sensitive goods.

**Outsourced workshop.** Welding, electrical and maintenance work continues in a module fitted with three-phase power, workbench and extraction – for example during a hall refurbishment. **Suitable type:** workshop container with industrial connections.

**Test and measuring room.** Quality assurance needs stable temperature, low-dust air and quiet. An insulated module with air conditioning delivers that better than a partitioned corner of the hall. **Suitable type:** office container with climate control.

**Gatehouse and security container.** Access control, barrier operation, visitor reception: small 2 to 3 metre modules with all-round glazing are standard at works entrances. **Suitable type:** gatehouse container (compact module).

> **Note on hazardous materials:** paints, solvents, oils and gas cylinders do not belong in an ordinary storage container. Certified hazardous-goods and fire-rated containers exist for that purpose, with a spill sump, safety ventilation and tested fire resistance. Separate regulations apply – a standard shipping container does not meet them.

## Retail & logistics

**Interim storage during refit or relocation.** When the shop floor is being rebuilt, the stock still has to go somewhere. A container in the yard is cheaper than rented external space and sits right at the premises. **Suitable type:** 20 or 40 ft storage container.

**Seasonal storage.** Garden furniture in winter, Christmas stock in summer, tyres all year: for cyclical inventory a purchased container with shelving pays off. **Suitable type:** shipping container, insulated and ventilated for sensitive goods.

**Sales stand and pop-up store.** With a side hatch, glazing or folding door, a 20 ft container becomes a compact shop – popular at Christmas markets, farm shops, ski resorts and brand activations. **Suitable type:** fitted retail container with serving hatch or shop window.

**Collection point.** A lockable module with parcel lockers and a power supply enables around-the-clock pickup. **Suitable type:** module container with electrical fit-out.

| Retail & logistics | Typical size | Critical point |
|---|---|---|
| Interim storage | 20 ft | Dry standing area, ventilation |
| Seasonal storage | 20–40 ft | Condensation, shelf fixings |
| Pop-up store | 20 ft fitted out | Power, permit for public land |
| Collection point | 3–6 m module | Power supply, lighting |

## Events & catering

Few sectors use containers as creatively as the events industry – they arrive fast, take abuse, and have become a deliberate design element.

**Bar container as a food truck alternative.** Unlike a trailer, a container stands firm, offers more working surface and opens up into a counter via a hinged front. **Suitable type:** catering container with hatch, water supply and grease trap connection.

**Backstage and dressing rooms.** Artist dressing rooms, make-up area, production office: stacked modules create a complete backstage area within a day. **Suitable type:** office container, stackable.

**Ticket and information point.** A small module with a service window at the entrance – lockable, heatable and gone again after the event. **Suitable type:** ticket booth container.

**Event sanitary facilities.** Toilet and shower containers with a fresh water tank or mains connection are more comfortable than single cabins and more hygienic at multi-day events. **Suitable type:** sanitary container, with waste tank if required.

**Refrigerated container for drinks.** A reefer keeps several thousand litres reliably chilled – more economical than many individual fridges, but it needs permanent power. **Suitable type:** refrigerated container, 20 or 40 ft.

## Public sector & education

**School and nursery containers during refurbishment.** When a wing is being renovated, classes move into modular buildings. With insulation, ventilation and acoustic protection these now match permanent rooms and can be stacked over several storeys. **Suitable type:** room module in school specification (higher fire safety and structural requirements).

**Temporary administrative space.** Citizen services, archives or planning departments work from modular buildings in the interim – made accessible via ramps and walkways. **Suitable type:** office container complex.

**Temporary health stations.** Vaccination and testing centres showed how quickly waiting areas, cubicles and administration can be assembled from modules; the same principle carries blood donation drives. **Suitable type:** office container with partitions and washbasins.

**Accommodation.** Living modules with a kitchen, bathroom and bedrooms serve as transitional housing. What matters here is insulation performance, fire safety and a sensible layout with shared areas. **Suitable type:** living container (residential module with wet room).

## Agriculture & garden

**Machinery and feed store.** Attachments, seed and tools stay dry and locked away – even on remote plots far from the farmyard. **Suitable type:** 20 or 40 ft shipping container.

**Farm shop and self-service stand.** A fitted container with a window, chilled counter and payment system works as a sales point at the field edge – open around the clock, without staff. **Suitable type:** insulated retail container.

**Cooling for the harvest.** Berries, asparagus and cut flowers need chilling soon after picking. A reefer replaces building a cold room and can be rented seasonally. **Suitable type:** refrigerated container.

**Garden house and hobby workshop.** In a private garden an insulated container replaces the timber shed – lower maintenance, harder to break into and visually crisp. **Suitable type:** 10 to 20 ft container, insulated.

## Sport & leisure

**Clubhouse.** On sports grounds without a permanent building, coupled modules provide a common room, bar and office – often as a long-term solution with a terrace. **Suitable type:** insulated office container complex.

**Changing rooms and showers.** Two modules give separate cabins for home and away teams, with benches, hooks and sanitary facilities. **Suitable type:** sanitary and changing container.

**Equipment store.** Goals, nets, hurdles, groundskeeping gear: a container at the edge of the pitch keeps kit dry and is quicker to reach than the cellar. **Suitable type:** 10–20 ft storage container.

**Climbing and fitness box.** Containers are converted into bouldering cubes, calisthenics boxes and mobile gyms – the steel frame carries the loads. **Suitable type:** shipping container with structural reinforcement by a specialist.

## Living & working

**Tiny house.** One or two coupled 20 ft containers give 15 to 30 square metres with a bathroom, kitchenette and sleeping alcove. The appeal lies in cost, build time and mobility; the hurdles are insulation, permits and a site. **Suitable type:** high cube (extra height for floor and ceiling build-up).

**Garden office and home working.** A 3 to 6 metre module in the garden creates quiet working space clearly separated from the home; power and network run from the house in a conduit. **Suitable type:** insulated office module.

**Student and temporary housing.** Apartment modules of 18 to 25 square metres can be produced in series and relocated when needs change. **Suitable type:** residential module with wet room.

**Holiday accommodation.** By a lake, in woodland or on a campsite: cabins with large glazing are weatherproof, low-maintenance and usable year-round. **Suitable type:** high cube living container with panoramic window.

As a rule of thumb, renting is usually cheaper up to around two years of use; beyond that buying wins – provided there is a permanent site and someone to handle maintenance and rust protection. The detailed calculation is in [Buy or rent?](/en/ratgeber/container-kaufen-oder-mieten).

## What applies to every use

**Permits.** In planning law a container quickly counts as a structure – especially when people occupy it or it stays for a longer period. Pure storage on commercial land is often uncritical; living, retail and catering never are. On public land a special use permit is also required.

**Insulation and condensation.** Bare steel sweats: when warm air meets cold walls, condensate forms – the most common cause of mould and spoiled stock. Heated containers need a continuous insulation layer and a vapour barrier; in pure storage use, vents and a dehumidifier help.

**Services.** Power, water, drainage and network must be planned before the crane arrives. Refrigerated and workshop containers often need three-phase power; for sanitary units, mains connection versus tank decides the running costs.

**Standing area.** The ground must be level, load-bearing and drained – pad foundations, concrete slabs or compacted gravel under the corner castings are usually sufficient. Access matters just as much: a truck with a loading crane needs a clear approach, room to manoeuvre and height free of branches or cables.

## Conclusion

Containers are a construction kit for temporary and permanent space. What matters is not the choice of supplier but an honest answer to three questions: how long is the container needed, will people occupy it, and which services are available on site? Type, fit-out and the rent-or-buy decision follow almost automatically from those answers.

For short projects with a defined end, renting carries the least risk. For recurring or permanent uses, buying pays off quickly, because a well-maintained steel container lasts two decades and more.
