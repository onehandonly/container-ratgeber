A container with a serving hatch in a car park, at a bathing spot or in front of a business park: what starts as a quick solution is often meant to run for years. This is exactly where permanent operation differs fundamentally from the two uses that already have their own guides on this site. A [pop-up store](/en/ratgeber/container-als-pop-up-store) is a time-limited test with a rented container; use at [events and in catering](/en/ratgeber/container-fuer-events-und-gastronomie) lasts days and runs on temporary connections. A permanent snack bar or kiosk, by contrast, needs fixed connections, a fit-out suitable for all seasons and, as a rule, a building-law procedure. The legal points below refer to the situation in Germany.

This guide first helps you classify your project and then works through the issues in the order in which they arise in practice.

## Which type of use is it?

The boundaries are fluid, but the classification determines fit-out, costs and the route through the authorities. The table describes typical cases, not legal thresholds.

| Feature | Event | Pop-up | Seasonal stand | Permanent operation |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Time on site | days | weeks to a few months | a few months every year | all year, several years |
| Usual basis | hire | hire | purchase or hire | usually purchase |
| Connections | distribution board, hose, tank | temporary or simple | set up and removed each season | fixed, to the site's mains |
| Fit-out | simple, robust | simple | may be uninsulated, depending on season | insulated, heated, washable |
| Building law | usually covered by the event | depends on duration and site | to be clarified each time | as a rule a building application |
| Main cost drivers | transport, equipment hire | rent, pitch | setting up and dismantling | site, connections, requirements |

Within permanent operation, a kiosk selling only packaged goods and a snack bar with a deep-fat fryer differ considerably again: open food, cooking steam and grease bring extraction, a grease separator and stricter hygiene requirements. Where the text distinguishes between the two, it says so explicitly.

## Permit sequence in six steps

The order matters: each step can stop the project, and the early steps cost little. Anyone who buys the container first and asks afterwards carries the risk alone.

1. **Secure the site, but not irrevocably.** Agree written permission from the landowner with a fixed term and on condition that consent is granted. Land owned by the municipality has its own rules, see [Containers on public land](/en/ratgeber/container-oeffentlicher-grund-sondernutzung). If the container stands on someone else's land, also read [Ownership, temporary fixtures and the land register](/en/ratgeber/container-eigentum-scheinbestandteil-grundbuch).
2. **Check planning law.** Whether a snack bar is permissible at this location follows from the development plan or, without one, from the surrounding area, and in undesignated outlying areas from Section 35 of the Federal Building Code (BauGB). Basics in [Development plans and outlying areas](/en/ratgeber/container-bebauungsplan-aussenbereich).
3. **Contact the building control authority.** A permanently placed sales container is a structure. Whether a building application is needed, which documents are required and whether parking spaces must be provided depends on location, duration and use, and is governed by the relevant state building code. A preliminary enquiry brings clarity early. More in [Containers and planning permission](/en/ratgeber/container-baugenehmigung) and [Building application and change of use](/en/ratgeber/container-bauantrag-nutzungsaenderung).
4. **Involve food control.** Anyone supplying food must register the business with the competent authority (Article 6 of Regulation (EC) No 852/2004). The regulation sets stricter structural requirements for fixed premises than for movable stalls – which requirements apply to your container is decided by the authority. Clarify this before fitting out, because surfaces, hand-wash basins and layout are expensive to change later. Staff handling open food need an instruction under Section 43 of the German Infection Protection Act (IfSG) before starting work.
5. **Trade registration and licences.** Register the business with the trade office and, depending on the federal state and what you sell, notify or obtain a licence under hospitality law – especially if alcohol is served. Hospitality law is partly state law and varies considerably, so ask locally.
6. **Drainage and fire safety.** Whether a grease separator is required for greasy waste water is set by the municipality's drainage bylaw. Fire-safety requirements, particularly for fryers and gas, come from building control and the insurer; basics in [Fire safety in containers](/en/ratgeber/container-brandschutz-vorschriften).

Only once these six points are settled at least in principle should you place binding orders for the container, fit-out and appliances.

## What year-round operation demands technically

An event container has to last a weekend; a permanent unit has to last ten winters. That shifts the priorities.

**Moisture is the main problem, not cold.** Cooking, washing up and coffee machines release a lot of water vapour. When it meets uninsulated steel, condensation forms and causes rust and mould behind linings. Continuous insulation with a carefully planned vapour control layer and effective extraction therefore belong together. Details in [Insulating a container](/en/ratgeber/container-daemmen), [Thermal bridges](/en/ratgeber/waermebruecken-container) and [Container ventilation](/en/ratgeber/container-belueftung).

**The serving opening weakens the steel frame.** The wall panels of a shipping container contribute to its stiffness. A large hatch or serving window needs a welded-in frame and, for projects requiring consent, a structural calculation. In winter the open hatch is also the biggest thermal bridge in the business: a smaller serving window with a sliding pane is often more practical for year-round trading than a full-length lift-up flap.

**Frost-proof pipes.** Supply and drain must enter the container frost-free; pipes in an unheated substructure will otherwise freeze in the first cold night. More in [Water connection and waste water](/en/ratgeber/container-wasseranschluss-abwasser).

**Fixed power instead of a CEE plug.** For permanent operation a fixed connection with its own distribution board, earthing of the steel body and regular inspection is usual. The calculation example in the events guide shows how to estimate the load of catering equipment; for the installation itself see [Power connection](/en/ratgeber/container-stromanschluss) and [Electrical planning](/en/ratgeber/container-elektroplanung-innenausbau).

**A workplace, not just a sales room.** For employees the German Workplace Ordinance (ArbStättV) applies, for example on room temperature (detailed in technical rule ASR A3.5) and toilets. Whether a nearby toilet may be shared or a dedicated one is needed should be settled before fitting out; see also [Sanitary containers](/en/ratgeber/sanitaercontainer-ratgeber).

**Noise at night.** Extraction fan, refrigeration and the outdoor unit of an air conditioner often run longer than opening hours. If there are homes nearby, plan where the equipment goes at an early stage, see [Noise control and acoustics](/en/ratgeber/container-laermschutz-akustik).

## Worked example: what does the container cost per trading day?

For a permanent business it is worth looking at the cost per trading day, because it shows how large a share of the total the container actually represents. The example assumes a used 20 ft shipping container converted into a snack bar by contractors and placed on owned or leased land. The ranges follow the guides [Fit-out costs by trade](/en/ratgeber/container-ausbaukosten-gewerke), [Container foundations](/en/ratgeber/container-fundament) and [Transport and delivery costs](/en/ratgeber/container-transport-lieferkosten); some items, such as the serving opening and extraction, are our own assumptions.

| Item | basic (approx.) | elaborate (approx.) |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 20 ft container, used | €1,500 | €3,000 |
| Serving opening with frame, flap or serving window, door | €1,500 | €4,000 |
| Insulation and washable interior lining | €5,000 | €9,000 |
| Electrics with distribution board, earthing, external connection | €3,000 | €6,000 |
| Sink, hand-wash basin, hot water | €1,900 | €5,200 |
| Extraction or cooker hood (strongly appliance-dependent) | €1,500 | €5,000 |
| Floor: remove old floor, new build-up, slip-resistant covering | €1,700 | €3,700 |
| Split air conditioner for heating and cooling | €1,500 | €3,500 |
| **Subtotal container and fit-out** | **€17,600** | **€39,400** |
| Pad foundations | €600 | €2,000 |
| Connection to sewer and water | €1,500 | €8,000 |
| Delivery by lorry with loader crane (local area) | €350 | €800 |
| **Total acquisition** | **€20,050** | **€50,200** |

Not included: kitchen appliances, refrigeration and till, planning and permit fees, a new electricity supply connection if needed, and lease, energy, staff and stock.

The five-year calculation adds three assumptions: maintenance (corrosion protection, seals, electrical inspection) of approx. €400 a year in the basic case and approx. €800 in the elaborate case, removal at the end for €350 or €800 respectively, and a residual value of 30 % of the container and fit-out subtotal. The residual value is deliberately below the figures for office containers in [Running costs and depreciation](/en/ratgeber/container-betriebskosten-wertverlust), because a catering fit-out has a small market. Foundations and connections have no residual value.

| Five-year calculation | basic (approx.) | elaborate (approx.) |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Total acquisition | €20,050 | €50,200 |
| Maintenance, 5 years | €2,000 | €4,000 |
| Removal | €350 | €800 |
| Residual value (30 % of €17,600 or €39,400) | −€5,280 | −€11,820 |
| **Net container cost** | **€17,120** | **€43,180** |
| per month (60 months) | approx. €285 | approx. €720 |
| per trading day (280 days a year, 1,400 days) | approx. €12 | approx. €31 |

Two conclusions follow. First, in permanent operation the container is rarely the largest cost block: every €100 of monthly lease alone equals around €4.30 per trading day at 280 trading days a year, and staff and cost of goods are usually well above that. Second, the term is decisive: if the site is given up after two years instead of five, the foundations, connections and most of the depreciation remain – and the cost per trading day rises markedly. To weigh up whether hire is the better basis, use the [Buy or hire calculator](/en/rechner/kaufen-oder-mieten) and the guide [Buy or hire?](/en/ratgeber/container-kaufen-oder-mieten). Multi-year hire offers for catering units are rarely standardised, however, and should be compared individually.

## Taking over a used snack-bar container: checklist

Fitted-out snack-bar containers are offered second-hand, often after a site has been given up. That can save a lot of money but carries its own risks. In addition to the general [inspection checklist](/en/ratgeber/container-besichtigung-pruefprotokoll):

- [ ] **The permit stays at the old site.** Planning permission applies to the project at a specific location. At the new site the procedure starts again.
- [ ] **Inspection records available?** Ask for the electrical inspection, gas inspection where relevant, and service records for extraction and air conditioner.
- [ ] **Moisture damage behind the lining.** Look for discolouration, blistering and smell on the ceiling, in corners and below the serving opening; if in doubt, have a lining panel opened.
- [ ] **Floor and substructure.** Years of wet mopping take a particular toll near the sink and entrance, see [Repairing the floor](/en/ratgeber/container-boden-sanieren-austauschen).
- [ ] **Does the extraction suit the new site?** Extraction blowing out to the side may end up too close to windows or footpaths at the new location.
- [ ] **Electrics sufficient for your appliances?** Check protection and connected load against your planned equipment list.
- [ ] **Clarify hygiene requirements at the new site** before buying – not every authority assesses the same fit-out in the same way.
- [ ] **Written purchase contract** with a condition description and photos; on warranty and exclusions see [Purchase contract and warranty](/en/ratgeber/container-kaufvertrag-gewaehrleistung).
- [ ] **Transport with built-in equipment** to be agreed with the haulier in advance: secure appliances, state weight and centre of gravity.

## Running for years: recurring tasks

After opening, the work shifts from planning to upkeep. These points belong in a fixed calendar:

| Task | Interval (guide) | Note |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Clean extraction and grease filters | per manufacturer, frequently with fryers | grease deposits are a fire load |
| Empty and service the grease separator | per bylaw and manufacturer | keep records |
| Have the electrical installation inspected | at fixed intervals for businesses | agree intervals with the electrician |
| Check the exterior for paint damage | annually | touch up scratches early, see [Care and corrosion protection](/en/ratgeber/container-pflege-und-rostschutz) |
| Check roof and drainage | annually, before winter | avoid standing water |
| Review insurance cover | whenever equipment or offer changes | see [Insuring a container](/en/ratgeber/container-versichern) |

There are also business obligations unrelated to the container that are often underestimated: in Germany, electronic tills must have a certified technical security device (Section 146a of the Fiscal Code, AO), and for depreciation and tax see [Containers and tax](/en/ratgeber/container-steuer-abschreibung). Against break-ins overnight, see [Theft protection](/en/ratgeber/container-sichern-diebstahlschutz).

## Important note

This guide offers general orientation on the legal situation in Germany and does not replace legal, tax or professional advice. Building law, hospitality law and parts of hygiene enforcement are state law or a matter for local authorities and vary noticeably. The information given by the local building control authority, food control, trade office and drainage operator is decisive. Electrical, gas and extraction systems belong in the hands of qualified contractors. All costs are guide values without guarantee.

## Conclusion

A snack bar or kiosk in a container can run profitably for years if it is planned as a fixed business from the outset: with a secured site, clarified planning law, hygiene requirements agreed early and a fit-out that copes with moisture and winter. In the example the container itself costs around €12 to €31 per trading day over five years. Far more weight lies with the lease, staff and whether the site is kept long enough for foundations, connections and fit-out to pay off. For short-term use, a [pop-up store](/en/ratgeber/container-als-pop-up-store) or an [event container](/en/container/eventcontainer) remains the better route.
