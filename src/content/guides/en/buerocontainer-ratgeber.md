When space is short and time is pressing, the office container is often the most pragmatic answer: a single module can often be set down in a day, is usable once power is connected, and can be relocated or sold later. The market is hard to survey, though – the range runs from a simple site office to a two-storey unit with corridor, server room and air conditioning. This guide shows what defines an office container, which sizes and equipment levels are usual, what matters in the energy standard, and what it costs.

## What defines an office container

An office container is a **modular room container** – a prefabricated building module, not a converted shipping container. The load-bearing element is a steel frame made of floor and roof frames and four corner posts, into which insulated sandwich panels for wall, roof and floor are fitted. Walls are lined with coated boards, the floor gets a hard-wearing finish.

Typical features setting it apart:

- **Double-glazed windows** with turn-and-tilt fittings, usually plus shutters or grilles
- **Complete electrics** ex works: sub-distribution with RCD, sockets, lights, external connection
- **Heating** as an electric convector or wall heater with room thermostat
- **Insulated steel exterior door** with a security lock
- **Coupling capability**: connecting fittings and prepared wall openings

The decisive difference from a shipping container lies in building physics. An office container is designed for occupancy: closed insulation layer, defined transmittance values, daylight and ventilation. A converted shipping container can do the same, but takes far more effort – see [Insulating a container](/en/ratgeber/container-daemmen). How cheap flat-pack kits differ from solidly built modules is covered in [Office container quality](/en/ratgeber/buerocontainer-qualitaet-bausatz). If you need more room width, the [XL container](/en/container/xl-container) offers around 3 m external width.

## Sizes and floor areas

Office containers follow a module grid roughly 2.44 m wide. Length and coupling produce almost any floor area.

| Module | Exterior approx. | Usable area approx. | Permanent workplaces approx. | Typical use |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 10 foot | 3.00 × 2.44 m | 6.5 – 7 m² | 1 (rather short-term) | Gatehouse, kiosk, single desk |
| 15 foot | 4.50 × 2.44 m | 10 – 11 m² | 1 | Single or small site office |
| 20 foot | 6.06 × 2.44 m | 13.5 – 14.5 m² | 1 – 2 | Standard office, site management |
| 2 × 20 foot | 6.06 × 4.88 m | 27 – 29 m² | 3 | Team office, meeting corner |
| 4 × 20 foot | 6.06 × 9.76 m | 55 – 58 m² | 5 – 6 | Administration unit, corridor |
| 8 × 20 foot, 2 storeys | 6.06 × 9.76 m per storey | 110 – 116 m² | 10 – 13 | Temporary office building |

The workplace figures assume **8 to 10 m² per workplace including furniture and a share of circulation space** – the range the German Technical Rule for Workplaces ASR A1.2 gives as guidance for office and screen workplaces. A 20-foot module with 13.5 to 14.5 m² therefore yields 1.35 to 1.8 places in arithmetic terms: one workplace is comfortable, two are possible but tight. In larger units, corridor, stairs and technical space take further area, which is why the values for 4 and 8 modules sit below simple division. Many quotations state higher numbers; these usually refer to short-term site use. For permanent office use, the German workplace rules on floor area, clear room height, visual contact with the outside and ventilation apply in full – get the module's clear internal height confirmed in writing and clarify borderline cases with your occupational safety specialist.

### Planning example: a team with six workplaces

A design office needs six screen workplaces and a meeting room for six people for two years.

| Requirement | Area approx. |
| --- | --- |
| 6 workplaces × 8–10 m² | 48 – 60 m² |
| Meeting room (one 20-foot module) | 13.5 – 14.5 m² |
| **Total** | **approx. 61.5 – 74.5 m²** |

Five coupled 20-foot modules provide approx. 67.5 to 72.5 m² together – enough at a mid-range area assumption; with generous planning a sixth module becomes necessary. At a monthly rent of approx. €200 – €350 per standard module, five modules come to approx. €1,000 – €1,750 a month, plus transport, crane, coupling and foundations. Toilets are not included: permanent workplaces must have access to toilets – if there is no building with toilets within reasonable reach, a [sanitary container](/en/ratgeber/sanitaercontainer-ratgeber) is added (20 foot rent approx. €300 – €700 a month). How to divide the modules sensibly inside is shown in [Furnishing an office container](/en/ratgeber/buerocontainer-einrichten).

## Equipment levels

Suppliers usually distinguish three fit-out levels. Labels vary, contents overlap.

| Feature | Basic | Standard | Comfort |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Insulation | Basic, seasonal | Increased, year-round | Enhanced, low U-value |
| Windows | 1, insulating glass | 2 with shutters | Several, solar control glass |
| Electrics | Basic sockets | Multiple sockets, network outlets | Trunking, server feed, UPS provision |
| Heating | Convector, 2 kW | Thermostat, several units | Split unit with heating |
| Air conditioning | Not included | Prepared/optional | Standard |
| Floor | PVC | Reinforced PVC | Needle felt or design covering |
| Security | Standard lock | Security lock, grilles | Alarm provision, security door |

The basic level suffices for short projects in the warm season. Once work runs year-round or over several years, there is a strong case for the standard level: besides lower heating costs, there is less condensation in the corners and a more even room temperature. Whether the extra cost of roughly €3,000 (new price basic approx. €8,000 – €12,000, standard approx. €11,000 – €16,000) is recovered through energy alone depends on service life, heating technology and electricity price – there is no blanket answer. The guide [Running costs and depreciation](/en/ratgeber/container-betriebskosten-wertverlust) gives approx. €450 – €900 a year in electricity for an insulated office container with a split heat pump, and approx. €1,200 – €2,400 for a poorly insulated module with direct electric heating (both at approx. €0.30/kWh).

## Technology in detail

**Electrics and network:** allow at least six sockets per workplace, some of them at desk height, and at least one network outlet. More important than the number is the distribution: socket strips near the floor on one wall lead to cable clutter; continuous dado trunking is far better. The external connection uses a CEE plug (16 A or 32 A); with several modules a shared main distribution board is sensible. How to estimate the power demand and which protection is needed is explained in [Power connection for a container](/en/ratgeber/container-stromanschluss) and [Electrical planning for a container fit-out](/en/ratgeber/container-elektroplanung-innenausbau).

**Heating:** electric convectors are standard because they need no infrastructure. Allow roughly 60 to 100 W per square metre, depending on insulation and window area – for a 20-foot module of approx. 14 m², that is roughly 0.8 to 1.4 kW, which a 2 kW convector covers. In continuous operation with high electricity prices, split units with a heat pump function are cheaper to run, since one kilowatt hour yields several times as much heat.

**Air conditioning and ventilation:** containers have an unfavourable envelope-to-volume ratio and heat up quickly in summer. External solar shading, a light roof coating and a split unit are the effective levers. Window ventilation suffices for two people; from about four, mechanical ventilation with heat recovery is clearly better.

**Lighting:** LED panels giving around 500 lux at the desk are standard. Look for glare-free versions and a light colour around 4000 K – warm white feels tiring in offices.

## Insulation and energy

Insulation quality determines comfort and running costs. Typical thermal transmittance values:

| Component | Basic approx. | Standard approx. | Comfort package approx. |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| External wall | 0.55 – 0.70 W/(m²·K) | 0.35 – 0.45 W/(m²·K) | 0.20 – 0.30 W/(m²·K) |
| Roof | 0.45 – 0.60 W/(m²·K) | 0.30 – 0.40 W/(m²·K) | 0.18 – 0.25 W/(m²·K) |
| Floor | 0.55 – 0.70 W/(m²·K) | 0.35 – 0.45 W/(m²·K) | 0.25 – 0.32 W/(m²·K) |
| Windows | 2.6 – 3.0 W/(m²·K) | 1.3 – 1.6 W/(m²·K) | 0.9 – 1.2 W/(m²·K) |

These are guide values varying by manufacturer and build-up. For permanent, approval-bound use as a building, statutory requirements may demand far stricter values than the basic version delivers – a common stumbling block when converting a site office into permanent offices.

Also watch thermal bridges: steel frame, corner posts and coupling joints conduct heat far better than the panel surface, which shows as condensation in the corners. Thermally broken profiles, careful joint insulation and adequate ventilation help – details in [Thermal bridges in containers](/en/ratgeber/waermebruecken-container). Which German energy requirements may apply to permanent use is explained in [The GEG and containers](/en/ratgeber/container-geg-energierecht).

## Coupling and multi-storey units

Larger areas come from coupling modules. Containers placed side by side are joined and braced via corner fittings; if partition walls are removed, a structurally verified beam replaces the load-bearing wall. Removing walls without verification jeopardises the stability and stackability of the whole unit. More on connectors and load paths in [Stacking and coupling containers](/en/ratgeber/container-stapeln-und-koppeln).

Two-storey units are common with office containers; many systems are designed by the manufacturer for up to three storeys – what counts is the clearance for the specific system. Required then:

- External staircase with landing and railings, or an internal stair module
- Escape route concept with two exits above a certain occupancy
- Higher foundation requirements, since the loads at the corner points rise considerably (see [Container foundations](/en/ratgeber/container-fundament))
- Narrower corridor or half modules for circulation

## Purchase and rental prices

| Configuration | New approx. | Used approx. | Monthly rent approx. |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 10 foot | €5,000 – €9,000 | €2,500 – €5,000 | €100 – €200 |
| 20 foot, basic | €8,000 – €12,000 | €3,500 – €6,500 | €150 – €250 |
| 20 foot, standard | €11,000 – €16,000 | €5,000 – €9,000 | €200 – €350 |
| 20 foot, comfort with A/C | €15,000 – €24,000 | €7,000 – €13,000 | €300 – €500 |
| Unit, 4 × 20 foot | €45,000 – €75,000 | €20,000 – €40,000 | €750 – €1,400 |
| Two-storey, 8 modules | €95,000 – €160,000 | €45,000 – €85,000 | €1,600 – €3,000 |

> **Note:** all figures are guide values, varying by condition, equipment and region (as of 2026), and are not binding quotations. Excluded are transport, crane hire, foundations, coupling assembly, staircases, connection and dismantling.

General overviews often quote approx. €150 – €350 monthly rent for a single 20-foot module; that corresponds to the basic and standard levels. Comfort modules with air conditioning cost more.

For larger units, assembly is a substantial share – allow roughly 15 to 30 percent of the module price for erection, coupling and services. A single module is usually delivered by a lorry with loader crane, at approx. €350 – €800 per trip locally; larger units are set with an articulated lorry and mobile crane. Details in [Transport and delivery costs](/en/ratgeber/container-transport-lieferkosten) and the [transport cost calculator](/en/rechner/transportkosten).

## Worked example: buying versus renting a 20-foot standard module

Whether buying pays depends above all on the period of use – and on what the module fetches at the end. The example assumes a used 20-foot standard module.

| Assumption | Buy | Rent |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Purchase (used, standard) | approx. €7,000 | – |
| Delivery by loader-crane lorry (mid value approx. €550 per trip) | approx. €550 | approx. €1,100 (delivery and collection, one-off) |
| Monthly rent | – | approx. €275 |
| Maintenance and insurance borne by the owner | approx. €25 a month | in the rent or with the hire company |

| Period of use | Buy, no resale | Rent |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 12 months | approx. €7,850 | approx. €4,400 |
| 24 months | approx. €8,150 | approx. €7,700 |
| 36 months | approx. €8,450 | approx. €11,000 |

Without resale, break-even arithmetically falls at around 26 months: (7,000 + 550 − 1,100) ÷ (275 − 25) ≈ 25.8. That matches the rough range of approx. 24 to 36 months given for insulated office containers in [Understanding container hire prices](/en/ratgeber/container-mietpreise-kalkulation). If the module can later be sold for approx. €3,500 and the buyer collects it, break-even moves to around 12 months: (7,000 + 550 − 3,500 − 1,100) ÷ 250 ≈ 11.8 – provided that price is actually achieved at the time. After 36 months, buying would then cost approx. €4,950 against approx. €11,000 for renting. Electricity costs are the same in both cases and are therefore left out. As resale proceeds are uncertain, work through both variants – with your own figures in the [buy or rent calculator](/en/rechner/kaufen-oder-mieten). The general trade-off, including leasing and hire purchase, is covered in [Buying or renting a container](/en/ratgeber/container-kaufen-oder-mieten).

## Uses beyond the construction site

Office containers left the building site long ago. Common applications:

- **Sales and service:** sales pavilions, show offices in new developments, workshop reception
- **Education:** interim classrooms during refurbishment, care rooms, staff rooms (with their own requirements, see [Classrooms and nursery interim space](/en/ratgeber/container-schulraum-kita-interim))
- **Administration:** expansions during staff growth, gatehouse buildings, test centres
- **Events:** production offices, accreditation, ticketing, security control rooms

These uses face higher demands on appearance, accessibility and energy standard than a site. Anyone planning publicly accessible rooms should factor in ramps, door widths and lighting early.

## Approval

An office container erected permanently and intended for occupancy is generally a structure under building law – regardless of being movable. Building application, type of use and separation distances therefore matter. For site facilities during construction, the German state building codes often provide relief. Whether a permit is needed depends on location, duration and use and must be clarified with the local building authority in each case. Details in [Container building permit](/en/ratgeber/container-baugenehmigung); what applies when switching from site use to permanent use is covered in [Building application and change of use](/en/ratgeber/container-bauantrag-nutzungsaenderung).

## Checklist before purchase

- How many workplaces are needed? Allow 8 to 10 m² per place plus meeting room, corridor and technical space.
- Are toilets within reasonable reach, or is a sanitary container needed?
- Year-round use? Choose standard or comfort insulation and get U-values confirmed in writing.
- What is the power supply – site distribution board, mains connection, capacity in amperes?
- Are network, telephony and server location clarified?
- Is air conditioning needed, or do solar shading plus ventilation suffice?
- Coupled or stacked modules? Clarify structural design and manufacturer clearance.
- Is access suitable for lorries, is there side clearance for the loader crane's outriggers, or is a mobile crane needed?
- Approval checked – construction-period use or permanent structure?
- When renting: contract term, notice period, return condition, maintenance duties (see [Rental contracts and law](/en/ratgeber/container-mietvertrag-recht)).
- On delivery: record condition, equipment and damage – template in [Delivery and handover](/en/ratgeber/container-anlieferung-abnahmeprotokoll).

## Important note

The information on workplace law, building law, energy standards and electrical engineering gives a general overview of the legal position in Germany and does not replace advice on individual cases. Binding are the requirements of the competent building authority, the manufacturer's clearances for coupling and stacking, and the assessment by your occupational safety specialist and a qualified electrician. ## Conclusion

Office containers are a mature, calculable solution for additional workplaces – provided requirements are defined properly in advance. The three levers with the greatest influence on satisfaction and cost are insulation standard, electrical equipment and summer heat protection. The basic version saves on purchase and costs more in operation. For short projects renting is usually cheaper; without resale, buying pays off after just over two years in the example, and considerably earlier with a realistic resale price – often as a used, well-documented module. For space planning: better to allow 8 to 10 m² per workplace than the tight figures in some quotations. Transport, foundations, assembly and connections belong in the calculation from the start, not in the final invoice.
