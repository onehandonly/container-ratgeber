Hardly any construction site works without them: site containers provide an office, break room, changing room, sanitary facilities and storage within a few hours – and disappear just as quickly once the project ends. Anyone procuring containers for a site for the first time faces a series of questions. Which variant covers which need? How many modules does a given workforce require? And is buying worthwhile, or is renting smarter? This guide sorts out the common designs, shows typical dimensions and equipment, and names the criteria that really count.

## What a site container is

"Site container" is a collective label for **modular room containers** placed temporarily on construction sites or other project areas. Technically they are prefabricated room cells with a self-supporting steel frame, insulated wall, roof and floor panels, plus factory-installed wiring. They arrive fully fitted out and are ready for use as soon as power (and, depending on type, water and drainage) is connected.

The **distinction from a shipping container** matters. A shipping container is an uninsulated, ISO-standardised transport box made of Corten steel, designed for stacking and sea freight. A room container is a building module: more lightly built, insulated, with windows, interior lining and a floor build-up. Both look similar from outside but differ fundamentally in construction, purpose and price. Only the material container in the narrower sense is often a classic shipping container.

| Feature | Room container (site container) | Shipping container |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Construction | Steel frame, sandwich panels | Steel frame of corner posts and longitudinal rails, corrugated Corten steel walls |
| Insulation | Integrated as standard | None (retrofittable) |
| Windows/doors | Factory-fitted | End doors only |
| Stackability | Usually 2–3 levels (with manufacturer approval) | Several tiers high when loaded on ships, locked with twistlocks |
| Typical use | Break rooms, offices, sanitary | Transport, storage |
| Service life | 15–25 years with maintenance | 20–30 years |

## The variants at a glance

Site containers are distinguished by use. The basic structure is almost always identical – differences arise from the fit-out, sanitary technology and window arrangement.

| Variant | Typical use | Typical equipment |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Crew/break container | Breaks, meals, small meetings | Windows with shutters, electric heating, sink, seating, coat rack |
| Office container | Site management, foremen, plan reviews | Double glazing, sockets, network outlets, heating, optional air conditioning |
| Sanitary container | WC, washing, showering | WC cubicles, urinals, wash basins, showers, water heater, frost protection |
| Changing container | Changing and drying work clothing | Lockers, benches, heating, reinforced ventilation |
| Material/tool container | Tools, small parts, machines | Shelving, workbench, bar lock, usually uninsulated |
| Storage container | Building materials, equipment, packaging | Timber floor, double-leaf door, ventilation slots |

**Crew containers** are the standard for break rooms. A standard-width 20-foot module offers about 13 m² inside. For break rooms, the German workplace rule ASR A4.2 gives a guide figure of about 1 m² per person using the room at the same time, including table and seat; after deducting the areas at the door, heater and sink, there is room in practice for a table with around six to eight seats. Larger gangs need two coupled modules or an XL version 3.00 m wide. How to work out the floor area for a specific workforce is shown in the worked example for 14 workers in [Containers on site: occupational safety](/en/ratgeber/container-arbeitsschutz-baustelle).

**Sanitary containers** are the most demanding units because they require fresh water and drainage. Without a sewer connection, versions with a waste water tank or collection pit are used. In winter, frost protection is decisive: trace heating and reliable base-level temperature control.

**Material containers** are built more simply – burglary protection matters more than comfort. They are frequently used shipping containers fitted with a lock box.

## Dimensions and module sizes

Room containers are not ISO-standardised, but they follow a grid based on shipping container dimensions. The standard exterior width is around 2.44 m and the height usually around 2.60 m; some manufacturers offer taller versions. Variation comes mainly from length. Insulation and interior lining make the internal dimensions noticeably smaller than those of a shipping container of the same length: a 20-foot module offers about 5.85 × 2.23 m inside instead of about 5.90 × 2.35 m.

| Module size | Exterior length approx. | Exterior width approx. | Exterior height approx. | Usable floor area approx. | Typical use |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 10 foot | 3.00 m | 2.44 m | 2.60 m | 6.2 m² | Gatehouse, small office, tools |
| 15 foot | 4.55 m | 2.44 m | 2.60 m | 9.7 m² | Single office, small break room |
| 20 foot | 6.06 m | 2.44 m | 2.60 m | 13.0 m² | Office, crew room, sanitary |
| 20 foot XL | 6.06 m | 3.00 m | 2.60 m | 16.3 m² | Break room with two rows of tables (oversize transport) |
| 2 × 20 foot coupled | 6.06 m | 4.87 m | 2.60 m | approx. 26 – 27 m² | Site management, meetings |
| 4 × 20 foot coupled | 6.06 m | 9.74 m | 2.60 m | approx. 52 – 55 m² | Site office, larger projects |

Figures for coupled units depend on whether the intermediate walls are removed; each double wall that goes adds a few tenths of a square metre. All values are approximate and vary by manufacturer.

Because of the floor build-up and ceiling panel, the clear internal height of standard modules is usually 2.30 to 2.40 m. Anyone planning shelving or tall cabinets should work from that figure, not the exterior height. Further data: [Container sizes & dimensions](/en/ratgeber/container-groessen-und-masse).

## Equipment in detail

Equipment determines comfort, running costs and year-round suitability. Ask about these points when comparing quotes:

- **Electrical installation:** number and layout of sockets, distribution board with residual current device, CEE external connection, option to loop through modules.
- **Heating:** usually electric convectors or wall heaters with a thermostat. Output must match the insulation quality – an undersized heater drives electricity costs.
- **Windows:** double glazing as standard, plus shutters or grilles for privacy and burglary protection. A tilt function matters more than it first appears.
- **Doors:** steel exterior doors with multi-point locking, cylinders keyed alike across modules.
- **Floor covering:** PVC or linoleum on a wood-based panel; slip-resistant with a coved skirting in sanitary and changing containers.
- **Insulation:** mineral wool or rigid PU foam in walls, roof and floor. For year-round use, insulation thickness is the single most important criterion – see [Insulating a container](/en/ratgeber/container-daemmen).
- **Fittings:** corner fittings at the four top corners for attaching slings when craning, forklift pockets in the base frame, connecting fittings for coupling. How to rig correctly is covered in [Lifting a container properly](/en/ratgeber/container-richtig-anheben); the manufacturer's instructions are decisive.

## Buying or renting for the site

On sites, renting is the norm because project duration and requirements change. Buying pays off with recurring demand, a stable workforce size and your own storage and transport capability. As a rough guide, [Container rental prices](/en/ratgeber/container-mietpreise-kalkulation) puts the break-even for insulated office containers at around 24 to 36 months of rental, and for sanitary containers at around 30 to 48 months.

| Variant | New purchase approx. | Used purchase approx. | Monthly rent approx. |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Crew, 20 foot | €7,000 – €12,000 | €3,000 – €6,500 | €130 – €260 |
| Office, 20 foot | €8,000 – €16,000 | €3,500 – €9,000 | €150 – €350 |
| Sanitary, 20 foot | €13,000 – €20,000 | €7,000 – €14,000 | €300 – €700 |
| Changing, 20 foot | €7,500 – €13,000 | €3,200 – €7,000 | €140 – €280 |
| Material, 20 foot (storage container) | €2,500 – €5,500 | €1,500 – €3,200 | €70 – €130 |

> **Note on all prices:** these are guide values without guarantee, varying by condition, equipment and region (as of 2026). They exclude delivery, crane hire, foundations and connection costs, and are not binding quotations. The sanitary range is especially wide because it covers both WC-only units and combined WC and shower units – details in [Sanitary containers](/en/ratgeber/sanitaercontainer-ratgeber).

Also budget for delivery and collection. Room containers are usually delivered and set down by a lorry with a loader crane; locally, one trip costs approx. €350 – €800 according to [Transport and delivery costs](/en/ratgeber/container-transport-lieferkosten). A material container with a suitable frame can also come by roll-off (hook-lift) truck, at approx. €180 – €350 per trip. When renting, delivery and collection are one-off costs per job. Add, where needed, a separate mobile crane, final cleaning and, when renting, often a deposit. A detailed comparison is in [Buying or renting a container](/en/ratgeber/container-kaufen-oder-mieten); for your own case, use the [buy-or-rent calculator](/en/rechner/kaufen-oder-mieten).

### Worked example: one crew container for changing sites

A construction firm needs a break room regularly over **five years**: two sites per year, each running about **five months**, with the container idle in between. That gives **50 months of use** in 60 calendar months. The comparison is for a used 20-foot crew container.

Assumptions (each within the ranges above or in the linked reference guides):

- Used purchase price: €5,000
- Monthly rent: €200
- Running costs when owned (insurance, testing of the electrical installation, minor repairs): approx. €450 per year, so approx. €2,250 over five years
- Residual value after five years: approx. 50–65 % of the purchase price, as given for site and crew containers in [Running costs and depreciation](/en/ratgeber/container-betriebskosten-wertverlust)
- Transport: delivery and collection per site, i.e. 20 trips – the same number whether bought or rented, so left out of the comparison

| Item | Buy | Rent |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Purchase | €5,000 | – |
| Running costs over 5 years | approx. €2,250 | – |
| Rent 50 × €200 | – | €10,000 |
| Residual value after 5 years | approx. −€2,500 to −€3,250 | – |
| **Total excluding transport** | **approx. €4,000 – €4,750** | **€10,000** |

In this scenario, buying is approx. €5,250 – €6,000 cheaper. Without resale, the purchase price would be reached after 25 months of rent (€5,000 ÷ €200); including running costs of around €37.50 per month, the threshold moves to approx. 31 months of use. That matches the 24-to-36-month rule of thumb.

Three factors can tip the result:

- **Idle time:** if there is only one short project of, say, ten months, renting costs approx. €2,000. Buying then only pays if the container is sold at a good price afterwards or used again.
- **Transport:** 20 trips by loader-crane lorry at approx. €350 – €800 each add up to approx. €7,000 – €16,000 – more than the container itself. A firm with its own loader-crane lorry largely saves this item when buying; anyone renting should choose a supplier close to the sites.
- **Yard space:** between jobs, an owned container needs a place in the yard. If that has to be rented, the running-costs guide puts it at approx. €300 – €1,500 per year, so approx. €1,500 – €7,500 over five years. At the top of that range, buying would lose its advantage.

## Siting and logistics

The standing area determines the effort involved. It should be load-bearing, level and drained; point loads are transferred through concrete slabs or strip footings beneath the corners. A fall of a few centimetres prevents standing water under the module.

Check **access** before ordering: a 20-foot room container usually arrives on a lorry with a loader crane, which needs space at the side for its outriggers. If the crane's reach does not extend to the standing position, a separate mobile crane becomes necessary (approx. €600 – €1,800 per day according to the transport guide) – the most common unexpected cost item. Also clarify clearance heights, turning circles, trees and overhead lines. What to check and record at handover is described in [Delivery and handover: the acceptance protocol](/en/ratgeber/container-anlieferung-abnahmeprotokoll).

For **coupling and multi-storey arrangements**, manufacturer specifications apply; the basics are in [Stacking and coupling containers](/en/ratgeber/container-stapeln-und-koppeln). Modules placed side by side are braced with connecting fittings; removed partition walls require a certified beam. Two-storey installations need an external staircase, landing and railings plus manufacturer clearance for stackability.

## Approval on construction sites

Containers serving the construction work itself and standing only for the duration of the works are treated more leniently than permanent structures in many jurisdictions. Do not rely on that: as soon as containers stand outside the approved construction area, occupy public land or are used beyond the construction period, an approval or special use permit may be required. Whether a permit is needed therefore depends on location, duration and use; in Germany the relevant state building code (Landesbauordnung) is decisive. If in doubt, ask the local building authority – details in [Container building permit](/en/ratgeber/container-baugenehmigung), and for containers on pavements or roads in [Containers on public land](/en/ratgeber/container-oeffentlicher-grund-sondernutzung). Independently of building law, break, changing and sanitary rooms must meet occupational health and safety requirements, see [Containers on site: occupational safety](/en/ratgeber/container-arbeitsschutz-baustelle).

## Security and theft protection

Construction sites are a favoured target for tool and material theft. What works are simple measures applied consistently: concealed lock boxes instead of exposed padlocks, doors facing a visible side, containers placed door-side tight against a neighbouring module or wall, motion detectors and lighting. More depth in [Securing a container](/en/ratgeber/container-sichern-diebstahlschutz).

## Selection checklist

- How many people are on site at once – and how many break, changing and WC places follow from that?
- Year-round use? Then prioritise insulation thickness, heating output and frost protection.
- Are power and water available, or are a site distribution board and waste water tank needed?
- How long does the project run – and will others follow? With more than about 24 to 36 months of use over a few years, a purchase comparison like the worked example is worthwhile.
- Do you have your own lorry with a loader crane and a yard space for the periods between jobs?
- Is access sufficient for the loader-crane lorry, or must a separate mobile crane be planned in?
- Should modules be coupled or stacked? Obtain manufacturer clearance.
- Are return condition, cleaning obligations and damage rules clear in the rental contract?
- How will tools and materials be secured against theft?

## Important note

> This article provides general information and is not legal or tax advice. Prices and costs are guide values without guarantee; the worked example is based on assumptions and does not replace quotations. It describes the situation in Germany: whether a permit is needed depends on the relevant state building code and the individual case, and the requirements for break, changing and sanitary rooms follow the Workplace Ordinance (ArbStättV) and the ASR technical rules in their current version. Binding information comes from the local building authority, the occupational safety authority and the construction trade accident insurer (BG BAU). Stack, couple and rig only with the manufacturer's approval and instructions.

## Conclusion

Site containers are not an off-the-shelf product but a construction kit. Anyone who surveys the requirement properly – headcount, duration, season, connections and access – almost always arrives at a clear configuration and avoids follow-up orders. For individual short and medium projects, renting is usually more economical. With recurring demand, buying pays off, often second-hand: in the worked example, a used crew container comes out approx. €5,250 – €6,000 below renting after 50 months of use over five years – as long as no expensive yard space is added. What counts is less the purchase price than the sum of transport, siting, connection and running costs; with frequent site changes, the loader-crane trips alone can cost more than the container itself. These items should be shown separately in every [quote comparison](/en/ratgeber/container-angebote-vergleichen).
