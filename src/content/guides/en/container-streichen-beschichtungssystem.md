A new coat of paint is one of the most common jobs on a used container: the paint has faded, the colour does not suit the company's branding, or rust spots are to be dealt with for good. The guide [Maintaining a container and protecting it from rust](/en/ratgeber/container-pflege-und-rostschutz) explains how to touch up individual rust spots. This article deals with the questions before and beyond that: is a repaint worthwhile at all, which coating system fits, what do the materials cost, and how should the work be done so that it does not flake off after two winters?

The legal references in this article relate to Germany; coating and safety requirements elsewhere may differ.

## Decide first: paint, wrap or leave it

Not every container needs painting. The table below helps to classify the situation.

| Starting point | Sensible measure | Reasoning |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Paint faded but intact, no rust | Clean, possibly renew only the topcoat | Corrosion protection is intact; it is a matter of looks and UV protection |
| Individual scratches and rust spots | Touch up locally, then topcoat | See the maintenance guide; a full repaint would be extra effort |
| Many paint defects, blisters, rust under the paint | Complete recoating including primer | The old layer no longer adheres; a topcoat alone will not last |
| New corporate colour on intact paint | Paint or vinyl wrap | A wrap can be removed but is no substitute for rust protection |
| Rust-through, holes, soft steel | Welding repair first, then coating | Paint does not cover structural damage |

The last row matters: a coating protects material, it does not restore load-bearing capacity. Whether frame, corner posts or floor are still sound is clarified by the inspection before purchase ([Inspection and test record](/en/ratgeber/container-besichtigung-pruefprotokoll)) or by an honest assessment before painting.

## The substrate determines the result

With steel coatings, preparation is the most laborious and most important part. The sequence is always the same:

1. **Clean:** remove salt, dust, bird droppings and algae with water and a brush. Wash off oil and grease with a suitable degreaser, because nothing adheres to greasy steel.
2. **Assess the old paint:** firmly adhering, intact paint can be sanded (keyed) and overpainted. Loose, flaking or undercut areas must be removed down to sound substrate.
3. **Remove rust:** by hand or machine with a wire brush, sander or needle scaler. Data sheets and quotations often describe the preparation grade according to ISO 8501-1, such as "St 2" (thorough hand or power-tool cleaning) or "Sa 2½" (blast cleaning to near-bare metal). For a full recoat, blasting is the most thorough method, but also the most laborious.
4. **Remove dust:** after sanding or blasting, sweep, vacuum or blow off; sanding dust under the paint acts like a separating layer.
5. **Prime straight away:** bare steel starts to rust again within hours in damp air. As little time as possible should pass between rust removal and priming.

> Practical tip: take photos after de-rusting and mark with chalk where the steel was deeply attacked. After six months you can check whether the coating holds in those places.

## Coating systems at a glance

Containers are usually coated in several layers. Product names differ by manufacturer; what matters is the build-up and the compatibility of the layers.

| Layer | Purpose | Typical type |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Primer | Adhesion and corrosion protection directly on the steel | Anti-corrosion primer, e.g. zinc-dust, epoxy or synthetic-resin based |
| Intermediate coat (optional) | Film thickness and moisture barrier | depending on the system |
| Topcoat | Weather and UV protection, colour | weather-resistant paint, e.g. acrylic, alkyd or polyurethane based |

Two basic rules apply to every system:

- **One manufacturer, one system.** Primer and topcoat must be compatible according to the data sheet. Wrong combinations lead to wrinkling, blisters or flaking.
- **Data sheet before marketing claims.** Coverage, application temperature, drying time, recoat intervals and the required dry film thickness are in the technical data sheet. These figures count, not advertising copy.

So-called direct-to-rust paints promise to replace primer and topcoat in one operation. For decorative surfaces or stopgap repairs that may be enough. For a coating that is meant to last many years, the classic build-up on a properly de-rusted substrate is usually the more reliable choice; base the decision on the data sheet.

## Colour: looks, heat and local rules

- **Dark surfaces heat up more in the sun** than light ones. For office, residential and refrigerated containers the roof colour affects interior temperature and condensation ([Ventilation](/en/ratgeber/container-belueftung)).
- **RAL colours** can generally be mixed by paint manufacturers. Ask for a sample before buying in bulk and check it in daylight.
- **Local requirements:** purely aesthetic questions such as colour are only partly covered by building law, but may be regulated by municipal design bylaws, heritage or conservation-area protection, or conditions attached to a permit. Whether and how this applies to your container depends on location, duration and use; basics are in [Building permit for containers](/en/ratgeber/container-baugenehmigung) and, for outlying areas, [Development plan and outlying areas](/en/ratgeber/container-bebauungsplan-aussenbereich). A short enquiry to the building authority settles this before you buy material.

## What must not be painted over

Before masking, go through the areas that must stay free or need special treatment:

- **CSC plate and container number:** both must stay legible if the container is still to count as a transport container. Their meaning is explained in [Container number and CSC plate](/en/ratgeber/container-nummer-csc-plakette). Mask or leave out the plate; have the number re-applied in the same position after painting if necessary.
- **Door seals, locking bars and hinges:** mask or remove. Paint on rubber hardens the seal, paint in hinges jams them.
- **Corner fitting apertures:** thick paint layers or runs can make it harder to insert twistlocks. If you intend to stack or transport the container, check this after drying ([Stacking and coupling](/en/ratgeber/container-stapeln-und-koppeln)).
- **Underside of the floor:** many shipping containers have a thick underbody coating. Do not remove or paint over this layer without reason; for floor repair see [Repairing or replacing the floor](/en/ratgeber/container-boden-sanieren-austauschen).

## Weather window and application

Paints cure properly only within certain conditions. Typical data sheet requirements are:

- Application only above a minimum temperature, often around 5 °C, and not in frost.
- Steel temperature clearly above the dew point (at least about 3 K is common) so that no invisible moisture film forms.
- No application in rain, fog, dew or in full midday sun on hot sheet metal.
- Observe the recoat intervals between layers, otherwise adhesion suffers.

These values are general rules of thumb; the data sheet of the chosen product is decisive. In Germany the period between late spring and early autumn is usually the most favourable.

## Worked example: material quantity and cost

The example is a 20-foot container with external dimensions of approx. 6.06 × 2.44 × 2.59 m (standard height). The flat external area excluding the floor is approx. 58.8 m² (roof 14.8 m², long sides 31.4 m², end walls 12.6 m²). The corrugated profile increases the area; with an assumed allowance of 20–30 % this gives approx. 70.6–76.5 m², of which the roof is approx. 17.7–19.2 m². More dimensions are in [Container sizes and dimensions](/en/ratgeber/container-groessen-und-masse).

Assumptions that you must replace with your own data sheets and quotes: coverage 6–10 m² per litre and coat, topcoat approx. €10–20 per litre, primer approx. €15–30 per litre. These prices are pure calculation assumptions, not market data.

| Variant | Coats | Quantity | Material cost (approx.) |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Roof only: primer + 2 × topcoat | 1 + 2 | approx. 1.8–3.2 l primer, approx. 3.5–6.4 l topcoat | approx. €60–225 |
| Whole surface, spot-primed (about 10 % of the area), 2 × topcoat | 1 (rust zones only) + 2 | approx. 0.7–1.3 l primer, approx. 14–25.5 l topcoat | approx. €150–550 |
| Whole surface fully primed, 2 × topcoat | 1 + 2 | approx. 7–12.7 l primer, approx. 14–25.5 l topcoat | approx. €250–890 |

On top come rust-removal tools, abrasives, masking tape and sheeting, brushes and rollers, degreaser and, if needed, scaffolding or a lift. For DIY work the materials are only part of the bill; the larger item is the time spent on preparation. A specialist firm calculates that time and usually charges by area. Obtain several written quotes and have the coating system, preparation grade and warranty stated ([Comparing quotes](/en/ratgeber/container-angebote-vergleichen)). For a container that is meant to keep resale value, a well-kept coating strengthens the sales case ([Selling a used container](/en/ratgeber/container-gebraucht-verkaufen)).

## DIY or specialist firm?

| Criterion | DIY | Specialist firm |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Suitable for | Touch-ups, topcoat renewal on sound substrate | Full recoat, heavy rust, blasting work |
| Preparation | Hand or power-tool cleaning (dust protection needed) | Blasting or mechanical cleaning with proof of preparation grade |
| Time required | high, weather dependent | plannable, with own equipment and covers |
| Warranty | none | possible, depending on contract |
| Risk | Paint failure through recoating too early, insufficient preparation | Price, availability |

Blasting produces dust and abrasive that must be contained and disposed of properly. On public land or a rented plot, clarify beforehand whether and how work is permitted ([Special use of public land](/en/ratgeber/container-oeffentlicher-grund-sondernutzung)).

## Health, safety and disposal

- **Respiratory and eye protection** when sanding, brushing and blasting. Old coatings may contain hazardous substances; be careful with unknown old paintwork.
- **Fall hazard:** the roof is about 2.6 m above ground. Use stable ladders or scaffolding and observe the load capacity of the roof ([Container roof: load and sealing](/en/ratgeber/container-dach-abdichten-dachlast)).
- **Solvent and paint residues** do not belong in drains, soil or household waste. Dispose of residues and sanding dust separately; ask your local waste service.
- **Fire and health protection:** solvent-based products need ventilation and distance from ignition sources. The safety data sheet takes precedence.

## Checklist before the first brushstroke

- Condition of frame, corner posts and floor checked, structural damage repaired first
- Coating system of primer and topcoat from one manufacturer chosen, data sheet read
- Colour checked as a sample, local requirements enquired about
- CSC plate, number, seals, hinges and corner fittings masked or protected
- Weather window and recoat intervals planned
- Protective equipment and disposal arranged
- Photo documentation before and after

## Important note

All information is general guidance without guarantee. Application and safety instructions from the paint manufacturers take precedence. Building-law requirements in Germany follow the respective state building code and municipal bylaws; for binding information the local authority is responsible. This guide does not replace professional assessment of damage to load-bearing parts.

## Conclusion

A repaint pays off when the substrate is right: check the structure first, then remove rust and prime, then protect with a matched topcoat. The material is the smaller cost block; preparation is the time and quality factor. Anyone who protects the CSC plate, seals and corner fittings and keeps to the weather window avoids the typical mistakes. For smaller touch-ups the [maintenance guide](/en/ratgeber/container-pflege-und-rostschutz) is enough; plan a full recoat with a data sheet, a test patch and several quotes.
